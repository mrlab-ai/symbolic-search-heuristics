% ============================== abstract =================================
\begin{abstract}
Product-at-evaluation symbolic \astar{} splits every binary decision diagram
(BDD) frontier into one bucket per heuristic value. Even the perfect heuristic
can thereby increase the cumulative number of expanded BDD nodes
exponentially. We give a sufficient condition that rules this out. The
cofactor width $W$ of a heuristic is the largest number of distinct residual
functions that remain after fixing any prefix of the variable order. On
solvable tasks with positive action costs, a consistent heuristic with
$\values\leq W$ finite values expands at most $3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$ times as
many BDD nodes as blind forward search, where $n$ is the number of state bits.
The known exponential example has exponential width under its analyzed order,
and a new family attains an $\Omega(W^2)$ ratio. Width and total ADD size give
incomparable bounds. Integer potentials, pattern databases (PDBs) with bounded
abstract state spaces and linear merge-and-shrink aligned with the search order
all have bounded width.

We evaluate PDB selection under exact width budgets alongside a
construction-bounded merge-and-shrink safeguard. The winning budget admits
only the empty PDB, so the selector behaves like blind search in every stage.
It ties blind search on a 50-task screen (30/50 solved), solves 41 tasks versus
40 for a counterexample-guided PDB on a disjoint 92-task sample, with a
domain-resampling interval that includes zero, and solves 612 of 1377 tasks in
a full census, three fewer than blind search and 21 fewer than capped
merge-and-shrink. Cofactor width therefore certifies representational safety in
this search architecture, not heuristic quality, construction cost or runtime.
\end{abstract}

% ============================ introduction ===============================
\section{Introduction}

Heuristics guide explicit \astar\ by ranking individual states
\citep{hart-et-al-ieeessc1968,pearl-1984}. Symbolic search instead expands
sets of states represented as binary decision diagrams
\citep[BDDs;][]{mcmillan-1993,edelkamp-helmert-ecp1999,bryant-ieeecomp1986},
and a heuristic used there also partitions these sets. Since the BDD of a
subset can be much larger than the BDD of its superset, a split that is
informative for search can be costly to represent. The trade-off matters
because blind symbolic search, particularly in its bidirectional form, remains
a strong approach to cost-optimal planning
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,speck-et-al-jair2025} and a key component of
the winning entry of the optimal track of the International Planning
Competition (IPC) 2023 \citep{speck-ipc2023}.

Several algorithms combine symbolic search with heuristics. \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} represents a heuristic $h$ as one BDD per
heuristic value and splits each search layer into buckets of states that
share a $g$- and an $h$-value. SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\
\citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008} instead fold the change in heuristic value into
the transition relations, and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
\citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002} stores $h$ as an algebraic decision diagram
(ADD). Each of these variants beats blind symbolic search in some domains and
loses in others \citep{torralba-phd2015,torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} explained why: the effort of a symbolic
expansion depends on the number of BDD nodes rather than the number of
represented states, and even the perfect heuristic \hstar\ can increase the
cumulative size of the expanded BDDs exponentially. Operator-potential
heuristics \citep{fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}, which integrate
potential heuristics based on linear programming
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015} into \ghseta, report little growth of BDD
sizes in practice. This evidence calls for a structural explanation, but it
does not yield a condition under which a heuristic \emph{provably} cannot
cause a large representational overhead. \citet{speck-helmert-ecai2025}
recently bounded the worst-case performance of symbolic search relative to
explicit search, but their analysis concerns transition relations and goal
formulas and explicitly leaves heuristics aside.

We provide such a condition. It rests on a standard measure for decision
diagrams, the fixed-order residual width of a multi-terminal BDD (MTBDD)
\citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}, which we apply to the total ADD
of a heuristic and call \emph{cofactor width}. It is the largest number of
distinct residual functions after any prefix of the search order, or
equivalently the widest level of the quasi-reduced total ADD. The
product-graph argument behind binary synthesis is also standard
\citep[Thm.~3.3.6]{wegener-2000}. Our contribution is to couple width to the
exact bucket semantics of symbolic search across cost layers, to derive
cumulative effort bounds and separations and to instantiate them for families
of planning heuristics.

We analyze product-at-evaluation forward search, which partitions every
frontier by heuristic value; we do not analyze transition partitions by
heuristic deltas or cumulative bidirectional search. Our first contribution
bounds fragmentation. For a heuristic with width $W$ and $T$ terminals, the
slices of a state-set BDD $B$ have total size at most $TWn(|B|+2)$. An exact
characterization of the expanded buckets turns this into the cut-sensitive
search bound $\values W\sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})$, where $L_g$ is the exact cost layer and $\cofcount$
sums fixed-order residual counts, and into the ratio $3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$
relative to blind search. The known exponential example has exponential width
under its analyzed order, and a new family attains an $\Omega(W^2)$ overhead;
whether the joint $\values Wn$ bound is tight remains open. Before cofactor
count is relaxed, the per-bucket width bound and the bound that the Apply
algorithm gives for the total ADD are incomparable. Two variants refine the
cost model: an image-aware model counts idealized high-level image
invocations, and pruning-only search removes the factor \values\ by keeping
the blind layers and using the heuristic only for sound discards.

Our second contribution identifies width-bounded heuristics. Safe terminal
transforms, including nested uniform caps, never increase width. Integer
potentials, pattern databases (PDBs) with bounded abstract state spaces and
linear merge-and-shrink aligned with the search order have bounded width; the
latter has width at most $d(N+1)$, where $d$ is the maximum variable-domain
size, when every cascade output, including the final abstraction, has at most
$N$ states. The potential result certifies level sets, not operator-potential
search with delta partitions.

Our third contribution is a diagnostic evaluation in SymK
\citep{speck-et-al-jair2025}. We implement a PDB selector that measures exact
cofactor width and evaluate it alongside an uncertified probe that batches
buckets with equal $g$-values and a merge-and-shrink safeguard with bounded
construction time. A frozen screen (P4) evaluates the selector and batching.
A disjoint held-out stage (P5) and a full census (P6) carry the winning
selector forward and add the safeguard alongside blind search, conventional
PDBs and merge-and-shrink. In both later stages the selected candidate is the
blind endpoint, so the evaluation does not test whether a nontrivial
width-bounded PDB improves search.

% ============================ background =================================
\section{Background}

We use finite deterministic SAS$^{+}$ tasks
$\task=\langle\vars,\ops,\sinit,\sgoal\rangle$
\citep{backstrom-nebel-compint1995}. Operators have positive integer costs.
The functions $\gstar(s)$ and $\hstar(s)$ are respectively the cheapest
cost from \sinit\ to $s$ and from $s$ to a goal. We set $\gstar(s)=\infty$
when $s$ is unreachable and $\hstar(s)=\infty$ when $s$ is a dead end. We
write $\copt=\hstar(\sinit)$ and assume
$0<\copt<\infty$. A heuristic
$h\colon\states\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible}
if $h\leq\hstar$, and \emph{consistent} if
$h(s)\leq\cost(o)+h(s[o])$ for every applicable $o$ and $h=0$ on goals.
The blind heuristic is $\hblind\equiv0$.

We encode every SAS$^{+}$ variable $v$ with $\lceil\log_2|D_v|\rceil$
Boolean bits and write $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ for all bits in the variable order
$\pi$. The order is \emph{variable-contiguous} if the bits of each SAS$^{+}$
variable are consecutive. Some bit strings encode no state, so we fix for
every heuristic a total extension to all of $\{0,1\}^n$; cofactor width
depends on this choice. A state set $S$ is represented by the reduced ordered
BDD (OBDD) of its characteristic function $\chi_S$, and $\bddsize{S}$ counts
its inner nodes \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986}. For a prefix
$\rho\in\{0,1\}^i$, $f|_\rho$ denotes the residual function of $f$, and
$\sub{i}{f}=\{f|_\rho\mid\rho\in\{0,1\}^i\}$ is the set of distinct
cofactors at cut $i$.

\begin{definition}[cofactor count]
\label{def-cofcount}
For a function $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the \emph{cofactor count} is
$\cofcount(f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} |\sub{i}{f}|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem-qsize}
For every Boolean $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$,
$\bddsize{f} \leq \cofcount(f) \leq n \cdot (\bddsize{f} + 2)$.
\end{lemma}

The cofactor count $\cofcount(f)$ is the number of inner nodes of the
quasi-reduced OBDD of $f$. The lemma holds because, at every cut, the reduced
nodes inject into the cofactors, while every cofactor is a constant or is
rooted at a unique reduced node. The factor $n$ is unavoidable already for
$f=x_1$, where $\cofcount(f)=2n-1$ but $\bddsize{f}=1$.

Symbolic uniform-cost search represents each exact layer
$L_g=\{s\mid\gstar(s)=g\}$ by one BDD \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
Product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\ \citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998}
additionally intersects these layers with the level sets
$\level{h}{v}=\{s\mid h(s)=v\}$ of the heuristic
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. It discards states with $h=\infty$, represents
each $(g,v)$ bucket by one BDD, selects buckets by increasing
$(f,g)=(g+v,g)$ and tests for a goal when it selects a bucket. An open bucket
persists until its exact key is selected. Immediately before expansion, the
selected bucket is intersected with the complement of all states closed at an
equal or lower $g$-value. An empty result is skipped, and a nonempty result is
moved entirely to the closed list before its successors are generated. These
assumptions about the
queue, stale duplicates, goal tests and positive costs define the scope of
our cumulative guarantees.

\begin{definition}[expansion size, \citealp{speck-et-al-icaps2020}]
\label{def-effort}
If $B_1,\ldots,B_q$ are the nonempty BDDs actually passed to expansion before
optimal termination, the \emph{expansion size} is
$\effort(\task,h)=\sum_{j=1}^q|B_j|$. Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} shows that,
under the assumptions above, these BDDs are exactly the nonempty $(g,v)$
slices with $g+v\leq\copt$ and $g<\copt$.
\end{definition}

For $\hblind$, \bddastar\ is uniform-cost search and
$\effort(\task,\hblind) = \sum_{g < \copt} \bddsize{L_g}$.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} prove that on some tasks even \hstar\ has
exponentially larger effort than blind search. Since a subset can have a much
larger BDD than its superset, both slicing and pruning can inflate
representations. Expansion size measures representation only; it does not
bound runtime, setup cost, memory or primitive relational products. We seek a
property of the heuristic that controls the BDD size of every subset it
selects by value.

% ===================== width-bounded heuristics ==========================
\section{Bounded Cofactor Width}

\begin{definition}[cofactor width]
\label{def-width}
The \emph{cofactor width} of a heuristic $h$ under variable order $\pi$
is $\width_\pi(h) = \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} |\sub{i}{h}|$. We call $h$
\emph{$(\pi,W)$-width-bounded} if $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and write
\emph{width} for cofactor width throughout.
\end{definition}

The cofactors are precisely the states of a deterministic layered automaton
that reads the bits in order $\pi$, so width is also the largest level of the
heuristic's quasi-reduced MTBDD \citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}.
Width is thus a standard representation property applied to heuristics, not a
new measure, and it is unrelated to novelty width in planning
\citep{lipovetzky-geffner-ecai2012}.

\begin{proposition}[value range]
\label{prop-range}
If $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, then $|\hrange(h)| \leq W$.
\end{proposition}

The proposition holds because the cofactors at the last cut are the attained
constants. We write $\values=|h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}|$ for the number
of finite values on planning states and $T=|\hrange(h)|$ for the number of
terminals of the total extension; hence $\values\leq T\leq W$.

\begin{proposition}[closure]
\label{prop-closure}
Let $h_1, h_2$ have widths $W_1, W_2$ under $\pi$ and let
$\oplus \in \{+, \max, \min\}$. Then
$\width_\pi(h_1 \oplus h_2) \leq W_1 \cdot W_2$.
\end{proposition}

At each cut, a combined cofactor is determined by a pair of operand
cofactors. Maxima of admissible and consistent components, and sums under an
admissible and consistent cost allocation, therefore keep the product of the
component widths as a bound.

Some heuristics, such as potential heuristics, are computed from functions
that can be negative. We call a function
$q\colon\states\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$ a \emph{raw admissible and
consistent estimate} if $q(s)\leq \hstar(s)$ for every state,
$q(s)\leq c+q(s')$ for every transition $s\xrightarrow{c}s'$, and
$q(s)\leq0$ on goals.

\begin{proposition}[safe terminal transforms]
\label{prop-transform}
Let $q$ be a raw admissible and consistent estimate, equipped with a
total extension
$q \colon \{0,1\}^{n} \to \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}$. Let
$\phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}
 \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \cup \{\infty\}$
be nondecreasing, satisfy $\phi(0)=0$, $\phi(\infty)=\infty$, and for
every $a\in\mathbb Z$ and integer $c\geq0$ satisfy
\[
\phi(a+c) \leq \phi(a)+c.
\]
Then $h=\phi\circ q$ is admissible and consistent and
$\width_\pi(h)\leq\width_\pi(q)$.
\end{proposition}

Monotonicity and the cost condition transfer consistency and imply
$\phi(a)\leq\max(0,a)$, and mapping terminals cannot create cofactors. Hence
rectification and the cap $\phi_\kappa(a)=\min(\max(0,a),\kappa)$ for finite
$a$, with $\phi_\kappa(\infty)=\infty$, are safe and do not increase width.
The cap has at most $\kappa+1$ finite values and is pointwise the strongest
safe transform with output ceiling $\kappa$. If $\kappa_1\leq\kappa_2$, then
$\phi_{\kappa_1}\circ q$ is a terminal relabeling of $\phi_{\kappa_2}\circ q$
and has no larger width. Caps therefore form a nested ladder between safety
and informativeness: increasing $\kappa$ weakly strengthens the heuristic,
while decreasing it weakly decreases the number of finite values, the width
and hence the coarse $3\values Wn$ certificate.

Width is closely related to the size of the reduced ordered ADD
$\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)$ under $\pi$ \citep{bahar-et-al-iccad1993}.

\begin{proposition}[ADD characterization]
\label{prop-add}
$\width_\pi(h) \leq |\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| + |\hrange(h)|$ and
$|\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| \leq n \cdot \width_\pi(h)$.
\end{proposition}

Every cofactor is a terminal or a residual ADD node, and the ADD nodes at
one level are distinct cofactors. If $A$ is the number of inner nodes, the
rooted binary DAG reaches at most $A+1$ terminals, so
$\width_\pi(h)\leq A+T\leq2A+1$. Small width and a small ADD are therefore
polynomially equivalent, but only under the order that the search actually
uses \citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.

\begin{remark}[total extensions]
\label{rem-encoding}
Definitions over $\{0,1\}^n$ require fixing $h$ on Boolean assignments
that encode no state. Upper bounds apply to the chosen extension; the
families below use natural arithmetic or table extensions. Lower bounds
witnessed on genuine states apply to every extension.
\end{remark}

% ======================= fragmentation theorems ==========================
\section{Bounded Fragmentation}
\label{sec-fragmentation}

We now bound how much a heuristic of bounded width can fragment a state
set. Fix $\pi$ and write $W=\width_\pi(h)$. The key observation is that, at
every cut, the cofactor of a subset selected by heuristic value is determined
by the pair of the state-set cofactor and the heuristic cofactor.

\begin{theorem}[bucket bound]
\label{thm-bucket}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and $U \subseteq \hrange(h)$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bddsize{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}
  &\leq \cofcount(\chi_{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}) \\
  &\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq Wn(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At cut $i$, equal pairs $(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)$ induce equal slice
cofactors, so their number is at most
$|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|\,|\sub{i}{h}|\leq
W|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|$. Sum over cuts and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize}. The supplement gives the explicit injection.
\end{proof}

The theorem covers single buckets ($U=\{v\}$), the removal of recognized dead
ends and threshold pruning. Up to the additive term, no subset selected by
heuristic value has a reduced BDD more than $Wn$ times as large as that of its
source set.

\begin{theorem}[partition bound]
\label{thm-partition}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{v \in \hrange(h)} \bddsize{S \cap \level{h}{v}}
  &\leq TW\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq TWn(\bddsize{S}+2) \\
  &\leq W^2n(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
If $S\subseteq\states$ and the sum ranges only over finite values attained
on planning states, the first two bounds hold with $T$ replaced by $\values$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to each of $T$ buckets (or the $\values$
finite planning-state buckets), then use $T\leq W$.
\end{proof}

The sum counts each reduced bucket separately, even if a shared unique table
reuses nodes across buckets. The factor $n$ cannot be removed: the supplement
gives a width-two parity slice whose reduced BDD is $\Theta(n)$ times as large
as its one-node source set.

Total ADD size gives a second certificate. Let $A$ be the number of inner
nodes of the total heuristic ADD and $b=\bddsize{S}$. Relabeling the ADD
terminals by membership in $U$ \citep[Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} and applying the
standard Boolean Apply algorithm \citep[Sec.~4.3]{bryant-ieeecomp1986} gives
$\bddsize{S\cap h^{-1}(U)}\leq bA+b+A$, so the two certificates combine as
$\min\{W\cofcount(\chi_S),bA+b+A\}$. With the bucket identity of the next
section, the ADD certificate bounds the ratio to blind effort by
$\values(2A+1)$. Since every ADD level has at most $W$ nonconstant residuals,
$A\leq nW$, so the coarse $3\values Wn$ ratio never substantially improves on
this ADD-based ratio. Before $\cofcount$ is relaxed, however, the width bound
can be sharper; the supplement gives separations in both directions. We use
width because it captures this cut-sensitive structure and yields
compositional bounds for heuristic families.

% ======================= search-effort guarantees ========================
\section{Forward Product-at-Evaluation Search Guarantees}
\label{sec-effort}

To lift the slice bounds to complete searches, we first characterize exactly
which buckets \bddastar\ expands.

\begin{lemma}[bucket identity]
\label{lem-identity}
Let $h$ be consistent. \bddastar\ on \task\ with $h$ selects every
bucket key $(g,v)$ with $g + v \leq \copt$ and $g < \copt$ and
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v} \neq \emptyset$ exactly once before terminating,
and the BDD expanded with key $(g,v)$ represents exactly
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v}$. No other bucket is expanded before
termination.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Selection is lexicographic in $(f,g)$ by definition. Consistency makes the
$f$-keys of generated successors nondecreasing, and strictly positive costs
together with the $g$ tie-break prevent a selected key from being repopulated.
Intersecting with the states closed at an equal or lower $g$-value removes
stale duplicates. Induction along cheapest paths gives optimal $g$-values and
complete slices, and an open prefix of an optimal plan prevents termination or
selection above $\copt$. The supplement proves all five claims in full:
monotone selection, no repopulation, optimal $g$-values, completeness and no
other expansions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[effort bound]
\label{thm-effort}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\effort(\task,h)
  &\leq \values W
    \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
      \cofcount(\chi_{L_g}) \\
  &\leq \values Wn
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr) \\
  &\leq W^2n
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} and Theorem~\ref{thm-partition} bound all selected
slices of each nonempty $L_g$ by
$\values W\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})$, and Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} bounds this by
$\values Wn(\bddsize{L_g}+2)$. If $N$ is the number of nonempty layers
below $\copt$, summing gives
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N)$. Positive integer costs imply
$N\leq\copt$, and $\values\leq W$ gives the coarse bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-effort}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort},
$\effort(\task, h) \leq 3\,\values W n \cdot
\effort(\task, \hblind) \leq
3\, W^{2} n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

The corollary follows because every nonempty pre-goal layer is a nonempty
proper subset of the states, so its characteristic function is nonconstant and
has at least one BDD node. Hence $N\leq\effort(\task,\hblind)$.

The guarantee is one-sided: a heuristic can still save arbitrarily much effort
by pruning. It concerns frontier sizes in forward product-at-evaluation
\bddastar, not primitive relational products in SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} or
\ghseta, and not cumulative bidirectional effort. Variants that visit the same
semantic buckets inherit the bound on their state sets.

\subsection{A Quadratic Ratio in $W$ Is Realizable}

\begin{theorem}[quadratic width-ratio family]
\label{thm-tight}
For all integers $W \geq 2$ and $k \geq 4W$ there are tasks
$\task_{k,W}$ with $n=2k+2$ Boolean variables and consistent, admissible
heuristics $h$ with $\width_\pi(h)=W$ such that
$\effort(\task_{k,W},h)\geq
(W^2/128)\effort(\task_{k,W},\hblind)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Use a $k$-step binary-choice chain followed by a cost-$W$ goal action and
order the chain bits before the choice bits. The non-goal heuristic is the
negated choice residue modulo $W$; action costs make it admissible and
consistent, and its $W$ residuals give exact width $W$. Blind effort is
$\Theta(k^2)$. On $\Theta(k)$ late layers, all $W$ buckets are selected
and each has $\Omega(kW)$ nodes, yielding $\Omega(k^2W^2)$ effort. The
supplement gives the construction and cofactor witnesses.
\end{proof}

The family attains $\Omega(W^2)$ but does not show that the joint
$\values Wn$ bound is tight: it has $\values=W$ and $n=\Omega(W)$, which
leaves open, e.g., an $O(Wn)$ bound. It is a worst-case construction, not a
prediction for typical tasks.

\subsection{Exponential Width in the Known Pathology}

In the family of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, indexed here by $r$, chain
variables precede two blocks $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ and
$v_{r+1},\ldots,v_{2r}$ in an order $\pi_r$ over $4r+2$ state bits.
At the critical layer, a state is not a dead end exactly when
$\bigvee_i(v_i\wedge v_{r+i})$ holds, which yields their exponentially large
bucket.

\begin{theorem}[width lower bound]
\label{thm-lb}
For the tasks $\task_r$ and orders $\pi_r$ of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020},
$\width_{\pi_r}(\hstar) \geq 2^{r}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the cut after the first $v$-block, encode each
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}$ as a
prefix. If $i\in A\setminus A'$, the valid suffix setting only
$v_{r+i}$ distinguishes the prefixes: one state has goal distance one and
the other is a dead end. Thus one cut has $2^r$ distinct \hstar-cofactors,
for every total extension.
\end{proof}

Together, Theorems~\ref{thm-effort} and~\ref{thm-lb} explain the known
pathology: its perfect heuristic has exponential width and thus lies outside
the bounded-width regime. The converse does not hold, since bounded width is
sufficient but not necessary for small buckets. Moreover, \hstar\ takes only
$O(n)$ values here, so the number of values alone does not control how much
prefix information its level sets entangle.

\subsection{A Refined Cost Model: Image Calls}
\label{sec-cost}

Expansion size charges only for BDD nodes, but every image computation also
has a fixed overhead. Splitting layers into many buckets can therefore cost
time even when the buckets are small.

\begin{definition}[image-aware effort]
\label{def-image-effort}
For $\alpha \geq 0$, the \emph{$\alpha$-effort} of a symbolic search run
is $\effort_\alpha=\sum_B(\alpha+|B|)$ over idealized high-level cost-image
invocations and their argument BDDs.
\end{definition}

At $\alpha=0$, this equals Definition~\ref{def-effort} only in the idealized
case where each expanded bucket is exactly the argument of one invocation.

\begin{proposition}[image-call counts]
\label{prop-calls}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h)\leq W$, and let $N_\ell$ be the
number of nonempty semantic blind layers $L_g$ with
$g<\copt$.  An idealized blind implementation that stores each semantic
$L_g$ in one BDD performs $N_\ell$ counted high-level cost-image invocations,
one per nonempty semantic layer.
An idealized implementation of \bddastar\ that invokes its high-level
cost-image routine once
for every expanded nonempty $(g,v)$ bucket performs
$\sum_g\values_g$ such invocations, where $\values_g$ is the number of
nonempty expanded buckets in layer $g$ and
$\values_g\leq\values\leq W$. Hence it performs at most
$W N_\ell$ counted invocations, and at most $\values N_\ell$ when the finite
valid-state value count is known.
\end{proposition}

The claim holds because each active layer has at most $\values\leq W$
nonempty buckets. The construction of Theorem~\ref{thm-tight} attains
$\Theta(W)$ invocations per late semantic layer. The count concerns idealized
high-level cost-image invocations, not the delta-partitioned relational
products of \ghseta; the supplement gives the full operational details.
Writing $E_0=\effort(\task,\hblind)$, the node and invocation bounds combine
for every $\alpha\geq0$ to
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \effort_{\alpha,h}
  &\leq \values Wn(E_0+2N_\ell) \\
  &\quad+\alpha\values N_\ell \\
  &\leq3\values Wn(E_0+\alpha N_\ell) \\
  &=3\values Wn\,\effort_{\alpha,\hblind}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\subsection{Pruning-Only Search}
\label{sec-prune}

Pruning-only search keeps one BDD per uniform-cost layer and uses the
heuristic only to discard states. Given the cost $c$ of the incumbent
solution, it discards states with $h=\infty$ or $g+h\geq c$, reapplies the
current bound at expansion and stops when the next layer cannot improve $c$.
Admissibility makes these discards sound. If $c_g$ is the incumbent when layer $g$ is
expanded, consistency along cheapest paths gives the exact slice
$L_g\cap h^{-1}(\{v\mid v<c_g-g\})$.

\begin{corollary}[pruning bound]
\label{cor-prune}
Let $h$ be admissible and consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. The
cumulative size of the layers expanded by pruning-only search is at most
$W n \cdot (\effort(\task, \hblind) + 2N_\ell)
\leq W n \cdot (\effort(\task, \hblind) + 2\copt)$, removing the factor
$\values$ due to value partitioning from the refined \bddastar\ bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, with one idealized high-level cost-image invocation
per expanded semantic layer as in blind search. With $c = \infty$ throughout
(no solution found yet) and no dead ends, its semantic layers and expansions
coincide with blind forward search; heuristic construction and evaluation can
still consume time.
\end{corollary}

Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} proves the corollary because every layer yields one
threshold slice. The $\alpha$-effort of pruning-only search is at most
$Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N_\ell)+\alpha N_\ell$, hence at most $3Wn$ times
the blind $\alpha$-effort. Pruning is also sound pointwise in either direction
of bidirectional search if forward search uses a goal-distance estimate and
backward search uses a distance-from-initial estimate. We claim no cumulative
bidirectional bound, however, because closed sets, meeting points, incumbents
and the direction schedule all feed back on the frontiers. The supplement
gives details.


% ============================ instances ==================================
\section{Width-Bounded Heuristic Families}
\label{sec-instances}

We now show that three common heuristic families have bounded width.
Throughout this section, $\pi$ is variable-contiguous,
$d=\max_{v\in\vars}|D_v|$ is the maximum domain size and $m=|\vars|$ is the
number of variables.

\subsection{Potential Heuristics}

Potential heuristics \citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022}
assign an integer potential $P(v,w)$ to every fact and compute
$p(s)=\sum_vP(v,s(v))$; we use the safe rectification $\hpot=\max(0,p)$. The
standard constraints on potentials make $p$ a raw admissible and consistent
estimate, so $\hpot$ is admissible and consistent by
Proposition~\ref{prop-transform}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pot}
Let $p$ have integer potentials with $|P(v,w)|\leq M$ for all facts,
extended to non-state assignments by the same sum over the bit
encoding's induced table, with table values on invalid bit codes also
in $[-M,M]$, e.g., $0$, and let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\width_\pi(\hpot)&\leq\width_\pi(p)\leq d(2mM+1),\\
|\hrange(\hpot)|&\leq mM+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-pot}
For rectified consistent integer potential heuristics with magnitudes at
most $M$,
\[
\effort(\task,\hpot)
 \leq 3d(mM+1)(2mM+1)n\cdot\effort(\task,\hblind),
\]
a factor polynomial in the task size and the numeric magnitude $M$.
\end{corollary}

The width bound holds because a cofactor is determined by the accumulated
sum of the completed variables and by one of at most $d$ partial codes of the
current variable. Corollary~\ref{cor-pot} substitutes this bound into
Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}. The result is pseudo-polynomial, i.e., polynomial
in the magnitude $M$ but not in its bit length, and it does not cover
delta-partitioned \ghseta.

\subsection{Pattern Databases}

A pattern database \citep[PDB;][]{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,%
edelkamp-ecp2001} projects \task\ onto a pattern
$P\subseteq\vars$ and stores the abstract goal distance $h^P(s)$ of the
abstract state induced by $s$; this distance is admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pdb}
Let $P\subseteq\vars$ be any pattern under a fixed Boolean order
$\pi$. Extend its table to invalid encodings by ignoring non-pattern
bits and mapping an invalid pattern-variable code to a fixed sink
terminal. Let
$|\states^P|=\prod_{v\in P}|D_v|$. Then
\[
\width_\pi(h^P)\leq |\states^P|+1.
\]
\end{proposition}

At any cut, each distinct non-sink cofactor is induced by a prefix of the
pattern bits that has a valid completion. Choosing one completion for each
such prefix maps them injectively to distinct abstract states, and all invalid
prefixes share the fixed sink. Interleaved non-pattern bits are ignored. A
budget of $N$ abstract states thus gives width at most $N+1$ and at most $N$
finite values, and hence an effort ratio of at most $3N(N+1)n$. The bound need
not be tight because the sink may coincide with a PDB value. Maxima over PDBs
keep the product bound on width (Proposition~\ref{prop-closure}); sums
additionally require an admissible and consistent cost allocation.

\subsection{Linear Merge-and-Shrink}

Merge-and-shrink (M\&S) abstractions
\citep{helmert-et-al-jacm2014} iteratively merge factors of a factored
transition system and shrink intermediate abstractions to at most $N$
abstract states. With a \emph{linear} merge strategy, the abstraction
mapping is a cascade of tables, also called a factored mapping
\citep{helmert-et-al-icaps2015}: table $\tau_j$ maps the previous intermediate
abstract state and the value of variable $v_j$ to the next intermediate
abstract state, for $j=1,\ldots,m$. The goal distance in the final abstraction
is admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-ms}
Let $h^{\mathrm{MS}}$ be an M\&S heuristic built with a linear merge
strategy whose variable order agrees with the variable-contiguous
$\pi$, and suppose the valid output set after every cascade table, including
the final abstraction, has at most $N$ states. Extend each
table with a distinguished sink for invalid variable codes. Then
$\width_\pi(h^{\mathrm{MS}}) \leq d \cdot (N+1)$.
\end{proposition}

The premise bounds the output of every table, not merely selected products
after shrinking. While reading the bits in order, the cascade then remembers
at most $N$ intermediate states plus the sink for invalid codes, together with
the bits of the current variable read so far. The final abstraction has at
most $N$ finite distances, which gives an effort ratio of at most
$3dN(N+1)n$.

The alignment premise is essential. Since \texttt{align\_merge\_order} is
explicitly false for our causal-graph M\&S control, the a~priori formula does
\emph{not} cover it, although its exact width, measured after construction in
the search order, can instantiate the general theorems. Aligning the orders
yields the formula but may change heuristic quality. We establish no analogous
guarantee for $h^{\max}$, $h^{\mathrm{add}}$ or LM-cut, and
Theorem~\ref{thm-lb} shows that perfect information can require exponential
width. The supplement gives the full proofs for all families.

% ============================ synthesis ==================================
\section{Width-Constrained Heuristic Selection}
\label{sec-synthesis}

The theorems control fragmentation, but not successor computation or
construction cost. To see whether width helps to select heuristics in
practice, we implement a diagnostic PDB selector. For a fixed budget of
$B=100{,}000$ abstract states, it collects candidates from five sources and
removes duplicates: the empty pattern, the longest prefix of the BDD variable
order, the longest goal/causal prefix, a goal-fill variant that skips
variables that do not fit, and seeded CEGAR. The selector builds every unique
candidate within $B$ as a PDB and a total ADD \emph{before} it filters by
exact width with budget $K$, so $K$ does not limit setup time. A
lexicographic score then prefers initial-state dead-end recognition, a larger
initial value, a larger mean finite goal distance and a larger fraction of
abstract dead ends, followed by smaller width and fewer abstract states.
Width one forces the attained value zero, so the pool deliberately includes
the blind endpoint. Width measurement covers invalid codes and skipped manager
levels. The fixed $B$ and the 60-second safeguard below are protocol design
points, not optimized or theoretically justified budgets. The matrix contains
no ablation with a budget on total ADD size, so it cannot establish which
quantity is the better operational constraint.

We add two engineering diagnostics. An uncertified batching probe computes one
image for the union of the selected bucket and other eligible fresh buckets
with the same $g$-value, while leaving the additional sources pending at their
original keys. Its oracle compares keys, BDD-node and state counts and
solution cost, but not set equality, so the probe supports no semantic or
performance claim. A separate M\&S safeguard aborts construction after
60\,seconds, falls back to blind search and charges the setup time. The
evaluated causal-graph M\&S is unaligned, so the $d(N+1)$ formula does not
apply, but its measured width can still instantiate the general theorem on
solvable cells. The supplement gives the full algorithms and contracts.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec-experiments}

We ask whether the frozen width filter retains an informative PDB and whether
two safeguards mitigate costs that the theorems do not cover. The evaluation
omits potential heuristics, aligned M\&S and pruning-only search.

All configurations use forward symbolic search with a fixed, task-specific BDD
order in one environment. Nonblind heuristics use product-at-evaluation
\bddastar, while blind and fallback runs use the equivalent $h\equiv0$ path. A
cell is one run of one configuration on one task, and a symbolic image is one
successor-set computation. Each run has a limit of 300\,seconds of aggregate
process CPU time, including heuristic construction, and 8\,GiB of memory on
one AMD EPYC 9755 core. Micro-PAR2 averages the reported CPU time of solved
cells and twice the cutoff (600\,seconds) for all other cells. No outcome from
an earlier run is reused as a performance observation. Coverage is
planner-reported: we reconcile costs across internal outputs but do not check
plans with an external validator.

The source inventory contains 1697 optimal sequential IPC tasks up to 2023.
Frozen, disjoint exclusions of 290 tasks with zero-cost operators and 30 tasks
with axioms after normalization leave 1377 tasks with positive costs and no
axioms. Direct SAS scans or a pinned-parser proof certify positive costs, a
separate check after normalization certifies the absence of axioms, and source
hashes are frozen. The \texttt{release\_no\_lp} matrix excludes potential
heuristics. The screen crosses five controls---blind search, uncapped and
value-32-capped M\&S with 10,000 states, a goal-fill PDB and a CEGAR
PDB---with fifteen selector treatments. Ten are unbatched with
$K\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256,\infty\}$, four combine selected budgets with
gated $\Delta=64$ batching, and one uses $K=32$ and $\Delta=16$.

\paragraph{Stage 1 (P4 screen).}
The frozen manifest contains 50 tasks, two from each of 25 domains, and 20
configurations (1000 cells). We assembled it during development and used
archived outcome summaries to enrich it with difficult or discriminating
tasks. These measurements influenced only the composition of the screen and
are not reused as P4 performance observations, so P4 is a selection screen,
not confirmatory evidence. A single lexicographic order fixes both the overall
winner and the winner of the selector family: it maximizes coverage, then
minimizes micro-PAR2, then, conditionally, total image time, and finally
breaks ties by label.

\paragraph{Stage 2 (P5 disjoint held-out sample).}
The separately frozen manifest contains 92 tasks that are disjoint from the
P4 screen, two from each of 46 domains. Within each domain, a fixed-seed
SHA-256 ordering selects the first two eligible task names without using any
planner outcome. P5 carries the unbatched winner of the selector family over
from Stage~1 without retuning. It evaluates the five controls and adds
value-32-capped M\&S with a 60-second build limit and blind fallback; the
overall screen winner is already among the controls. Together with the
selector, this gives seven unique configurations and 644 cells. The primary
estimand is the selector-minus-CEGAR difference in paired coverage, averaged
equally over domains, and a deterministic domain bootstrap with 100,000
replicates gives its 95\% stability interval. All other comparisons are
descriptive. The interval resamples the 46 fixed domains; it captures neither
task selection nor run-to-run variation and is not an interval for the census.
Here, \emph{held out} means held out from P4 selection; we do not claim that
the P5 tasks were unseen in the broader project.

\paragraph{Stage 3 (P6 full census).}
P6 runs the same seven configurations unchanged on all 1377 tasks (9639
cells), including the screen and held-out tasks. It is a descriptive census,
not another sample-based evaluation, and its only sensitivity analysis
excludes the 50 screen tasks. Neither P5 nor P6 reopens or reranks the
selector, matrix, parameters, contrasts or statistical policy, and all three
stages have separately frozen source and launch identities. Missing,
duplicate, unexpected or unattested cells invalidate a stage instead of
changing a denominator. Coverage and PAR2 keep the fixed task denominator for
outcomes that the protocol recognizes as unsolvable, whereas the formal effort
guarantees apply only to solvable tasks with $0<\copt<\infty$. The supplement
gives the complete protocol for execution, acceptance, measurement, provenance
and recovery, including the prospective corrections that produced the accepted
P4, P5 and P6 records.

We also froze a secondary structural diagnostic during P6, after a few
individual records had been shown but before any comparative analysis. It
contrasts $W$ with the total ADD size $U=A+T$ and with logged partition
ratios. Since it was not declared before launch, it is descriptive only, and
it cannot test the theorem because the logs omit cofactor profiles and paired
sizes of source sets and slices.

\section{Related Work}

Symbolic heuristic search algorithms differ in where they apply the heuristic.
\bddastar\ partitions frontiers by heuristic value
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998}, SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\ partition
transitions \citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008}, and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}} uses
algebraic decision diagrams \citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002}.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} showed that even \hstar\ can increase BDD-node
effort exponentially. We give a checkable sufficient condition that excludes
this behavior and show that their construction has exponential width under its
analyzed order. Fixed-order residual width and the binary product construction
are standard; our contribution couples them to exact bucket semantics,
cumulative search effort, separations and bounds for heuristic families. The
result is a representation-safety certificate for one search architecture,
not a general runtime theorem.

All heuristic families we analyze predate this work: PDBs and their symbolic
use \citep{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,edelkamp-ecp2001}, CEGAR pattern
generation \citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019}, symbolic compilation
\citep{edelkamp-et-al-ecai2012}, factored M\&S mappings
\citep{torralba-phd2015,helmert-et-al-icaps2015} and potential heuristics
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}.
The only new step of our selector is to measure exact total-ADD width and
reject candidates within a fixed, deduplicated pool that preserves provenance;
we do not claim the candidate generators or the score as new. Our M\&S bound
requires aligned merge and search orders, whereas the evaluated control is
deliberately unaligned.

Our batching probe differs from whole-$g$ rows, best-set-first grouping and
bounded transition trees \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,torralba-et-al-icaps2013}
in that the sources share one speculative image but remain pending at their
original keys. Since its regression oracle does not test set equality,
batching is exploratory rather than a certified search transformation. Work
on symbolic versus explicit blind search \citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025} and on
symbolic perimeter abstractions
\citep{torralba-et-al-ijcai2016a,torralba-et-al-aij2018} addresses
complementary representation choices. Variable ordering
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014} is directly coupled to our certificate
because it determines $W$.

\section{Conclusions}

We showed that cofactor width certifies representational safety for
product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\ on solvable tasks with positive costs
under a fixed variable order. A consistent heuristic with width $W$ and
$\values$ finite values has effort at most
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt)$, and hence at most
$3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$ times blind effort. The known exponential example has
exponential width under its analyzed order, and an $\Omega(W^2)$ family shows
that a quadratic ratio is attainable. Before cofactor count is relaxed, the per-bucket width bound and
the Apply bound for the total ADD are incomparable; the latter gives the ratio
$\values(2A+1)$ for ADD size $A$. Integer potentials, PDBs with bounded
abstract state spaces and aligned linear M\&S have bounded width. The
certificate bounds representational harm, not heuristic quality, construction
cost or runtime.

Our evaluation filtered a fixed PDB pool by exact width but omitted potential
heuristics, aligned M\&S and pruning-only search. Across all three stages, the
winning selector remained blind-equivalent: all 1355 certified P6 traces
selected the empty PDB, it solved 612/1377 tasks compared to 615 for blind
search, and capped M\&S led with 633. Batching lowered coverage on the screen,
the P5 interval for the selector--CEGAR difference included zero, and the
build-bounded M\&S safeguard was competitive but only descriptively evaluated.
The secondary P6 diagnostic found substantial, family-dependent gaps between
$W$ and total ADD size and mixed correlations with logged partition ratios;
without cofactor profiles and paired sizes of source sets and slices, it does
not validate the bound. The evaluated width filter thus does not establish
practical usefulness on the frozen population. Because $K=1$ admits only the
blind endpoint, no ranking score could have rescued this choice.

Future selectors must budget certified width and candidate quality jointly,
compare informative candidates and evaluate the already available terminal
caps without collapsing to $h\equiv0$. Open theoretical problems include the
tightness of the joint $\values Wn$ bound and coupling heuristic quality to
certified safety.
