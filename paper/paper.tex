\begin{abstract}
Why can an informative heuristic reduce symbolic search yet make its
representation larger?  In product-at-evaluation symbolic \astar, a heuristic
changes both the states expanded and their partition into value buckets.  We
factor cumulative expanded-BDD nodes exactly into unsplit representation
effort and fragmentation.  To certify the latter, we introduce the
\emph{co-occurring cofactor profile}, which counts state-set and heuristic
residuals induced by the same variable-prefix assignments.  It is bounded by
the obvious Cartesian product and by standard cofactor width.  For $V$ finite
heuristic values, width $W$, and $n$ state bits, heuristic effort is at most
$2VWn$ times blind effort; a polynomial-size planning family attains
$VWn/120$.  Safe value coarsening contracts the certificate and yields
width-constrained, cap-aware pattern-database selectors.

A frozen remeasurement supports the two-effect decomposition but exposes a
shared-denominator ambiguity.  We therefore run a prospective validation that
holds the heuristic and expanded states fixed while perturbing the BDD order
with four unseen seeds.  On \SeedPrimaryPairs\ strict pairs, the normalized
co-occurring profile reaches \SeedJointMacro\ equal-domain concordance,
compared with \SeedNullMacro\ for the denominator-only null; its primary gate
fails.  Nevertheless, co-occurrence recovers signal discarded by the
Cartesian, width, and ADD relaxations (\SeedJointMacro\ versus
\SeedCartesianMacro, \SeedWidthMacro, and \SeedAddMacro), and the absolute
certificate passes its secondary gate at \SeedAbsoluteJointMacro.
Width-constrained heuristics reduce conditional effort relative to
unconstrained heuristic selection but remain worse than blind search.  Thus
co-occurrence gives a tighter certificate and a useful diagnostic, not a
stand-alone performance predictor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Why does one heuristic help symbolic search while another of comparable
quality hurts it?  In explicit \astar, a heuristic ranks individual states
\citep{hart-et-al-ieeessc1968,pearl-1984}.  In symbolic search, it also splits
compact state sets into separately represented value buckets.  Binary decision
diagrams (BDDs)\footnote{All diagram and width statements use the planner's
fixed variable order; cofactor width is unrelated to planning novelty width.}
can represent enormous sets succinctly
\citep{mcmillan-1993,edelkamp-helmert-ecp1999,bryant-ieeecomp1986}, but their
size is not monotone under subsets.  A useful partition can therefore reduce
the search space and simultaneously make its representation larger.

This tension is real rather than pathological bookkeeping.  Even \hstar\ can
increase cumulative expanded-BDD size exponentially
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, and heuristic symbolic planners help in some
domains but hurt in others
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998,hansen-et-al-sara2002,jensen-et-al-aij2008,
torralba-et-al-aij2017}.  Blind symbolic search consequently remains a strong
cost-optimal method \citep{speck-et-al-jair2025}.  Heuristic quality alone
cannot explain these outcomes.

We separate the two effects exactly.  For a completed run, cumulative bucket
effort equals the BDD size of the corresponding unsplit expanded sets times a
fragmentation factor.  Information measures can explain the first term.  The
second needs a representation-sensitive quantity that relates the heuristic
to the particular sets it partitions.

The obvious residual bound multiplies, at every cut of the BDD order, the
number of state-set cofactors by the number of heuristic cofactors.  This
Cartesian profile treats every cross-pair as feasible.  We instead introduce
the \emph{co-occurring cofactor profile}: it counts only pairs induced by the
same prefix assignment.  This distinction is the paper's central structural
idea.  Every value-selected bucket is bounded by the co-occurring profile,
which is in turn bounded by the Cartesian profile and by standard cofactor
width \citep{wegener-2000}.  Total ADD size yields a separate incomparable
Apply bound.

Accumulating the certificate over search layers gives a blind-relative effort
bound of $O(VWn)$, where $V$ is the number of finite values, $W$ is cofactor
width, and $n$ is the number of state bits.  A polynomial-size planning family
attains $\Omega(VWn)$ throughout the feasible parameter range, and the known
exponential example necessarily has exponential width under its analyzed
order.  Thus width is a sufficient static safeguard, while the co-occurring
profile is the tighter run-specific explanation.

Because the run-specific profile is unavailable before search, we use its
width relaxation for design.  Safe terminal maps, including uniform caps,
cannot increase any cofactor cut.  This yields width-constrained, cap-aware
PDB selection with candidate-set and score dominance over uncapped exact-width
selection on the same pool.  The guarantee concerns representation and the
shared score, not runtime superiority.

We test these distinctions with SymK \citep{speck-et-al-jair2025}.  A frozen
remeasurement across alternative heuristics supports the two-effect
decomposition: information is aligned with search reduction, whereas the
Cartesian profile is aligned with fragmentation.  Its denominator audit
fails, however.  We then preregister a cleaner intervention: four previously
unused Gamer-order seeds vary only the BDD order while the PDB, values, costs,
expanded states, and active buckets remain fixed.  The co-occurring score is
far stronger than its structural relaxations, but does not beat
$1/\unioneffort$; the prespecified primary gate therefore fails.  This result
sets the claim boundary rather than becoming a new tuning signal.

Finally, we stress-test the theory-derived PDB selectors over five width
budgets.  Relative to unconstrained exact PDB selection, all constrained
selectors pool to effort ratio \HoldoutConstrainedUnconstrainedPooledRatio\
with \HoldoutConstrainedUnconstrainedCoverageDelta\ additional solved
configuration--task pairs.  Relative to blind search, the same selectors pool
to \HoldoutAllSelectorsBlindPooledRatio\ with
\HoldoutAllSelectorsBlindCoverageDelta\ fewer.  Width therefore controls
representation among heuristic choices without overcoming the cost of
heuristic partitioning in this benchmark.

% ============================ background =================================
\section{Background}

We use finite deterministic SAS$^{+}$ tasks
$\task=\langle\vars,\ops,\sinit,\sgoal\rangle$
\citep{backstrom-nebel-compint1995}. Operators have positive integer costs.
The functions $\gstar(s)$ and $\hstar(s)$ are respectively the cheapest
cost from \sinit\ to $s$ and from $s$ to a goal, with value $\infty$ for a
dead end. We write $\copt=\hstar(\sinit)$ and assume
$0<\copt<\infty$. A heuristic
$h\colon\states\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible}
if $h\leq\hstar$, and \emph{consistent} if
$h(s)\leq\cost(o)+h(s[o])$ for every applicable $o$ and $h=0$ on goals.
The blind heuristic is $\hblind\equiv0$.

Fix a Boolean state encoding in which every SAS$^{+}$ variable $v$ uses
$\lceil\log_2|D_v|\rceil$ Boolean bits. Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be those
bits in order $\pi$, and give every heuristic a total extension to Boolean
strings that encode no state. The order is \emph{variable-contiguous} if
all bits of each SAS$^{+}$ variable are consecutive. A state set $S$ is
represented by the reduced ordered BDD (OBDD) of its characteristic function
$\chi_S$ with complemented edges, as in our CUDD implementation;
$\bddsize{S}$ counts regular inner nodes \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986}. A signed
pointer records a regular node together with its polarity, so $f$ and $\neg f$
share regular nodes but remain distinct residual functions. For a prefix
$\rho\in\{0,1\}^i$, let $f|_\rho$ be the residual function and
$\sub{i}{f}=\{f|_\rho\mid\rho\in\{0,1\}^i\}$ its distinct cofactors.

Symbolic uniform-cost search represents each exact layer
$L_g=\{s\mid\gstar(s)=g\}$ by one BDD
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}. Product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} intersects layers with the level sets
$\level{h}{v}=\{s\mid h(s)=v\}$. It removes closed states, discards
$h=\infty$, represents each $(g,v)$ bucket by one BDD, selects by increasing
$(f,g)=(g+v,g)$, and tests for a goal when a bucket is selected
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. Each open bucket persists until that exact key
is selected. Immediately before expansion, its resident set is intersected
with the complement of states already closed at their best known $g$;
``bucket'' below means this residual set. Selection moves the entire residual
bucket to closed before generating successors. These queue, duplicate,
goal-test, and positive-cost assumptions define the scope of our cumulative
guarantees.

\begin{definition}[expansion size, \citealp{speck-et-al-icaps2020}]
\label{def-effort}
Let $B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ denote the unique BDD expanded by \bddastar\ with
$g$-value $g$ and $h$-value $v$ on task \task\ with heuristic $h$, with
$B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ representing the empty set if no such expansion
occurs. In the consistent product-at-evaluation setting above, the
\emph{expansion size} of \bddastar\ is
$\effort(\task,h) = \sum_{\,g + v \leq \copt,\ g < \copt}
|B_{\task,h}(g,v)|$; Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} proves that these and only these
nonempty buckets are expanded before termination.
\end{definition}

For $\hblind$, \bddastar\ is uniform-cost search and
$\effort(\task,\hblind) = \sum_{g < \copt} \bddsize{L_g}$.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} prove that there are tasks on which even
$\hstar$ has exponentially larger effort
than blind search: a subset can have a much larger BDD than its superset,
so both slicing and pruning can inflate representations. We seek a
heuristic property that controls every value-selected subset.

For any completed search, let $A_g(h)$ be the heuristic values whose
nonempty buckets are expanded at cost $g$, and let
$S_g^h=\bigcup_{v\in A_g(h)}B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ be their union. Define the
corresponding unsplit representation effort and fragmentation factor by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \unioneffort(\task,h)&=\sum_g\bddsize{S_g^h},\\
 \fragfactor(\task,h)&=
 \frac{\effort(\task,h)}{\unioneffort(\task,h)},
 \end{aligned}
\]
whenever the denominator is positive. Hence
$\effort=\unioneffort\cdot\fragfactor$ exactly. Blind search has one bucket
per expanded layer and therefore $\fragfactor(\task,\hblind)=1$. The ratio
need not exceed one because a union BDD can be larger than the sum of the
reduced BDDs for its disjoint pieces. It isolates the representational effect
of value partitioning without presuming its direction.

\begin{definition}[cofactor count]
\label{def-cofcount}
For a function $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the \emph{cofactor count} is
$\cofcount(f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} |\sub{i}{f}|$.
\end{definition}

Write $\cutcount{i}{f}=|\sub{i}{f}|$ for its cut-$i$ cofactor count.
For a state set $S$ and heuristic $h$, define the number of residual pairs
that actually co-occur under one prefix by
\[
 \jointcount{i}{S}{h}=
 \left|\left\{(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)
       \mid \rho\in\{0,1\}^i\right\}\right|,
\]
and their \emph{co-occurring cofactor profile} by
\[
  \jointprofile{S}{h}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\jointcount{i}{S}{h}.
\]
Its Cartesian relaxation is the \emph{cut-aligned cofactor-profile product}
\[
  \cutprod{S}{h}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
  \cutcount{i}{\chi_S}\cutcount{i}{h}.
\]
At every cut,
\[
 \max\{\cutcount{i}{\chi_S},\cutcount{i}{h}\}
 \leq\jointcount{i}{S}{h}
 \leq\cutcount{i}{\chi_S}\cutcount{i}{h}.
\]
The first quantity retains which residuals arise from the \emph{same} prefix;
the second counts all possible cross-pairs.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem-qsize}
For every Boolean $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$,
$\bddsize{f} \leq \cofcount(f) \leq n \cdot (\bddsize{f} + 2)$.
If $f$ is nonconstant, also
$\cofcount(f)\leq2n\bddsize{f}$.
\end{lemma}

$\cofcount(f)$ is the polarity-sensitive state count of the quasi-reduced
OBDD. At a fixed cut, let $U$ and $D$ be the regular inner nodes above and on
the projected frontier, let $r\leq D$ count frontier nodes reached in both
polarities, and let $t\leq2$ count signed constants. The projected regular
binary DAG gives $D+\delta\leq U+1$, where $\delta$ records whether its
terminal is reached, while the residual count is $D+r+t$. If $t=0$, this is
at most $D+U+1$; if $t>0$, then $\delta=1$ and it is at most $D+U+2$.
Both are at most $\bddsize{f}+2$. Conversely, every regular node is witnessed
by a signed residual at its own level; summing over levels proves the lemma.
Its factor
$n$ is unavoidable already for $f=x_1$, where
$\cofcount(f)=2n-1$ but $\bddsize{f}=1$. For the last inequality, the displayed
upper bound suffices when $\bddsize{f}\geq2$. A nonconstant one-node BDD is a
literal, whose cofactor count is at most $2n$.

\section{Static Relaxations and Safe Transforms}

\begin{definition}[cofactor width]
\label{def-width}
The \emph{cofactor width} of a heuristic $h$ under variable order $\pi$
is $\width_\pi(h) = \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} |\sub{i}{h}|$. We call $h$
\emph{$(\pi,W)$-width-bounded} if $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and write
\emph{width} for cofactor width throughout.
\end{definition}

The cofactors are precisely the states of a deterministic layered automaton
that reads $\pi$; hence width is also the largest level of the heuristic's
quasi-reduced multi-terminal BDD \citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}. It is a
standard fixed-order residual/MTBDD representation property applied here to
heuristics. It is neither a newly proposed width measure nor the unrelated
planning notion of novelty width \citep{lipovetzky-geffner-ecai2012}.

\begin{proposition}[value range]
\label{prop-range}
If $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, then $|\hrange(h)| \leq W$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, the final cofactors are the attained constants. Write
$\values=|h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}|$ for finite values on planning
states and $T=|\hrange(h)|$ for terminals of the total extension; then
$\values\leq T\leq W$.

\begin{proposition}[closure]
\label{prop-closure}
Let $h_1, h_2$ have widths $W_1, W_2$ under $\pi$ and let
$\oplus \in \{+, \max, \min\}$. Then
$\width_\pi(h_1 \oplus h_2) \leq W_1 \cdot W_2$.
\end{proposition}

At each cut, a combined cofactor is determined by a pair of operand
cofactors. Thus maximization and admissible additive combinations remain
width-bounded, with multiplicative component bounds.

Call a possibly signed function
$q\colon\states\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$ a \emph{raw admissible and
consistent estimate} if $q(s)\leq \hstar(s)$ for every state,
$q(s)\leq c+q(s')$ for every transition $s\xrightarrow{c}s'$, and
$q(s)\leq0$ on goals.

\begin{proposition}[safe terminal transforms]
\label{prop-transform}
Let $q$ be a raw admissible and consistent estimate, equipped with a
total extension
$q \colon \{0,1\}^{n} \to \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}$. Let
$\phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}
 \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \cup \{\infty\}$
be nondecreasing, satisfy $\phi(0)=0$, $\phi(\infty)=\infty$, and for
every $a\in\mathbb Z$ and integer $c\geq0$ satisfy
\[
\phi(a+c) \leq \phi(a)+c.
\]
Then $h=\phi\circ q$ is admissible and consistent and
$\cutcount{i}{h}\leq\cutcount{i}{q}$ for every cut $i$. Consequently,
$\width_\pi(h)\leq\width_\pi(q)$ and
$\jointprofile{S}{h}\leq\jointprofile{S}{q}$ and
$\cutprod{S}{h}\leq\cutprod{S}{q}$ for every fixed state set $S$.
\end{proposition}

Monotonicity and the cost condition transfer consistency and imply
$\phi(a)\leq\max(0,a)$; terminal mapping cannot create cofactors. Mapping
$(\chi_S|_\rho,q|_\rho)$ to
$(\chi_S|_\rho,\phi\circ(q|_\rho))$ also cannot create co-occurring pairs.
Thus both
rectification and the cap
$\phi_\kappa(a)=\min(\max(0,a),\kappa)$ for finite $a$ (and
$\phi_\kappa(\infty)=\infty$) are safe and width nonincreasing. The cap has
at most $\kappa+1$ finite values and is pointwise strongest among such safe
transforms with that output ceiling. If $\kappa_1\leq\kappa_2$, then
$\phi_{\kappa_1}\circ q$ is a terminal relabeling of
$\phi_{\kappa_2}\circ q$, so every cut count, its profile product with any
fixed set, and its cofactor width are no larger. Caps therefore form a nested
safety--informativeness ladder: increasing $\kappa$ weakly strengthens the
heuristic, while decreasing it weakly contracts both fixed-set profiles and
the coarse $2\values Wn$ certificate. This fixed-set statement does not imply
monotonicity of a complete run, whose expanded sets may change with the cap.

Let $\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)$ be the reduced ordered ADD under $\pi$
\citep{bahar-et-al-iccad1993}.

\begin{proposition}[ADD characterization]
\label{prop-add}
$\width_\pi(h) \leq |\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| + |\hrange(h)|$ and
$|\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| \leq n \cdot \width_\pi(h)$.
\end{proposition}

Every cofactor is a terminal or a residual ADD node, and every ADD node at
a level is a distinct cofactor. Hence small width and a small ADD are
polynomially equivalent only in the order actually used by the search
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.

\begin{remark}[total extensions]
\label{rem-encoding}
Definitions over $\{0,1\}^n$ require fixing $h$ on Boolean assignments
that encode no state. Upper bounds apply to the chosen extension; the
families below use natural arithmetic or table extensions. Lower bounds
witnessed on genuine states apply to every extension.
\end{remark}

% ======================= fragmentation theorems ==========================
\section{The Co-occurring Profile Certificate}
\label{sec-fragmentation}

Fix $\pi$ and write $W=\width_\pi(h)$. At every cut, the cofactor of a
value-selected subset is determined by the pair consisting of the state-set
cofactor and the heuristic cofactor induced by the same prefix. Thus
$\jointprofile{S}{h}\leq\cutprod{S}{h}\leq
\width_\pi(h)\cofcount(\chi_S)$.

\begin{theorem}[bucket bound]
\label{thm-bucket}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and $U \subseteq \hrange(h)$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bddsize{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}
  &\leq \cofcount(\chi_{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}) \\
  &\leq \jointprofile{S}{h} \\
  &\leq \cutprod{S}{h} \\
  &\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq Wn(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At cut $i$, equal co-occurring pairs
$(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)$ induce equal slice cofactors. Map each distinct
slice residual injectively to a witnessing pair, sum over cuts, relax the
co-occurring pairs to their Cartesian product, then relax each heuristic cut
count to $W$. Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} completes the chain; the supplement gives
the explicit injection.
\end{proof}

This covers a bucket ($U=\{v\}$), recognized-dead-end removal, and
threshold pruning. No value-selected subset can inflate the reduced BDD by
more than $Wn$ up to the additive term.

\begin{theorem}[partition bound]
\label{thm-partition}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{v \in \hrange(h)} \bddsize{S \cap \level{h}{v}}
  &\leq T\jointprofile{S}{h} \\
  &\leq T\cutprod{S}{h} \\
  &\leq TW\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq TWn(\bddsize{S}+2) \\
  &\leq W^2n(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
If $S\subseteq\states$ and the sum ranges only over finite values attained
on planning states, every $T$-dependent bound holds with $T$ replaced by
$\values$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to each of $T$ buckets (or the $\values$
finite planning-state buckets), then relax the profile and use $T\leq W$.
\end{proof}

The partition theorem gives a run-level predictor that retains the cutwise
interaction discarded by width. Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \jointbound(\task,h)&=
 \sum_g |A_g(h)|\jointprofile{S_g^h}{h},\\
 \jointscore(\task,h)&=
 \frac{\jointbound(\task,h)}{\unioneffort(\task,h)},\\
 \profilebound(\task,h)&=
 \sum_g |A_g(h)|\cutprod{S_g^h}{h},\\
 \profilescore(\task,h)&=
 \frac{\profilebound(\task,h)}{\unioneffort(\task,h)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
We call $\jointscore$ the \emph{normalized co-occurring profile certificate};
$\profilescore$ is its normalized Cartesian relaxation. Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to every expanded bucket gives
$\fragfactor\leq\jointscore\leq\profilescore$. Unlike heuristic quality,
$\jointscore$ is designed to explain the representational effect conditional
on the unsplit expanded sets. Unlike its Cartesian and width relaxations, it
retains which state-set and heuristic residuals arise from the same prefixes.
Width is the frontier-independent relaxation used to certify a heuristic
before search.

The effort measure sums each reduced bucket separately even if a shared
unique table reuses nodes. The factor $n$ cannot be removed: the supplement
gives a width-two parity slice whose reduced BDD is $\Theta(n)$ times its
one-node source set.

Writing $A$ for the total heuristic ADD's inner-node count and
$b=\bddsize{S}$, relabeling its terminals by membership in $U$
\citep[Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} gives an ordinary Boolean OBDD with at most
$A$ nodes. Materializing both polarities of $S$ uses at most $2b$ ordinary
nodes, so standard Boolean Apply \citep[Sec.~4.3]{bryant-ieeecomp1986} gives
$\bddsize{S\cap h^{-1}(U)}\leq2(A+1)b+A$. Thus the two certificates
combine as $\min\{\jointprofile{S}{h},2(A+1)b+A\}$. With the bucket identity
below, the ADD certificate yields the blind-effort ratio bound
$\values(3A+2)$. A nonconstant ADD has one root and at most $W$ nodes on
each other level, so $A\leq1+(n-1)W$ and the ratio is at most
$3\values Wn$. Width is therefore not a uniformly stronger scalar than ADD
size. The profile certificate has a different role: it exploits
cut-by-cut alignment between the actual frontier and the heuristic, while
width is its reusable, frontier-independent envelope. The two certificates
are genuinely incomparable. For parity $h$ and the set of strings whose
Hamming weight is $0$ modulo $3$, $\cutprod{S}{h}=\Theta(n)$ while Apply gives
$\Theta(n^2)$; for $h=x_1$ and $S=\{x_1=1\}$, the CUDD-safe Apply
certificate gives $5$ while the profile is $\Theta(n)$. The supplement proves
both separations. We retain
Apply and take the minimum; width supplies the compositional family bounds
and the pre-search certificate.

\section{Forward Product-at-Evaluation Search Guarantees}
\label{sec-effort}

\begin{lemma}[bucket identity]
\label{lem-identity}
Let $h$ be consistent. \bddastar\ on \task\ with $h$ selects every
bucket key $(g,v)$ with $g + v \leq \copt$ and $g < \copt$ and
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v} \neq \emptyset$ exactly once before terminating,
and the BDD expanded with key $(g,v)$ represents exactly
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v}$. No other bucket is expanded before
termination.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Selection is lexicographic in $(f,g)$ by definition; consistency makes
generated successor $f$-keys nondecreasing. Consistency, positive costs, and
increasing-$g$ tie breaking ensure that every predecessor that could populate
a key at equal $f$ is selected first, so a selected key cannot be repopulated.
Induction along cheapest
paths gives optimal $g$-values and complete slices. An open prefix of an
optimal plan prevents termination or selection above $\copt$. The
supplement proves the five claims---monotone selection, no re-population,
optimal $g$-values, completeness, and no other expansions---in full.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[effort bound]
\label{thm-effort}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. Then
for
$\values_g=|\{v\in h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}\mid
g+v\leq\copt,\ L_g\cap\level{h}{v}\ne\emptyset\}|$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\effort(\task,h)
  &\leq \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
    \values_g\jointprofile{L_g}{h} \\
  &\leq \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
    \values_g\cutprod{L_g}{h} \\
  &\leq \values\sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
    \cutprod{L_g}{h} \\
  &\leq \values W
    \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
      \cofcount(\chi_{L_g}) \\
  &\leq \values Wn
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr) \\
  &\leq W^2n
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} selects exactly $\values_g$ buckets on layer $g$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} bounds each by $\jointprofile{L_g}{h}$.
The remaining inequalities relax co-occurrence to the Cartesian profile, use
$\values_g\leq\values$, bound each heuristic cut by $W$, and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize}. If $N$ is the number
of nonempty layers below $\copt$, summing gives
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N)$. Positive integer costs imply
$N\leq\copt$, and $\values\leq W$ gives the coarse bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-effort}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort},
$\effort(\task, h) \leq 2\,\values W n \cdot
\effort(\task, \hblind) \leq
2\, W^{2} n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

Every nonempty pre-goal layer is proper, so Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} gives
$\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})\leq2n\bddsize{L_g}$. Substituting this bound into the
third inequality of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort} proves the corollary directly.

The guarantee is one-sided: pruning savings remain unbounded. It concerns
frontier sizes in forward product-at-evaluation \bddastar, not primitive
relational products in SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} or \ghseta, and not cumulative
bidirectional effort. Variants visiting the same semantic buckets inherit
the state-set bound.

\subsection{The Coarse Bound Is Jointly Tight}

\begin{theorem}[joint lower bound]
\label{thm-tight}
For every $2\leq\values\leq W$ and every power of two $B\geq4W$, there
is a polynomial-size task with $n=\Theta(B)$ Boolean state bits and a
consistent, admissible heuristic $h$, represented by an $O(BW)$-node ordered
ADD, with exactly $\values$ finite values and $\width_\pi(h)=W$ such that
\[
  \frac{\effort(\task,h)}{\effort(\task,\hblind)}
  \geq \frac{\values Wn}{120}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The task generates an address $a$, modular bits $y$, and data bits $z$, then
uses a multiplexer filter $z_a=1$ and carries the retained set through $B$
layers.  The heuristic colors $\sum_i y_i\bmod W$ with $\values$ values.  Its
$W$ translated residuals give width $W$, an $O(BW)$-node ADD, and consistency
under the chosen action costs.  At every central $y$-cut, each selected bucket
has $BW$ distinct address--residue residuals.  Across $\values$ buckets and
$B$ retained layers this yields at least $\values WB^3/2$ nodes, whereas the
unsplit multiplexer has $O(B)$ nodes per layer and blind effort at most
$15B^2$.  Since $n\leq4B$, the ratio is at least $\values Wn/120$.  The
supplement gives the operators, residual injection, and exact counts.
\end{proof}

Together with Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}, Theorem~\ref{thm-tight} closes the
worst-case parameter gap for the stated fixed-order,
product-at-evaluation BDD-node-effort model: none of $\values$, $W$ or $n$ can
be removed from its universal ratio. For each fixed power-of-two $B$, the
construction covers the full region $2\leq\values\leq W\leq B/4$; varying $B$
scales $n$. Its repeated multiplexer layers are constructed and are not a
typical-case prediction.

\subsection{Exponential Width Is Necessary for the Known Pathology}

In the family of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, chain variables precede
two blocks $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and $v_{n+1},\ldots,v_{2n}$ in $\pi_n$.
At the critical layer, a state is not a dead end exactly when
$\bigvee_i(v_i\wedge v_{n+i})$ holds, producing their exponential bucket.

\begin{theorem}[width lower bound]
\label{thm-lb}
For the tasks $\task_n$ and orders $\pi_n$ of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020},
$\width_{\pi_n}(\hstar) \geq 2^{n}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the cut after the first $v$-block, encode each
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ as a
prefix. If $i\in A\setminus A'$, the valid suffix setting only
$v_{n+i}$ distinguishes the prefixes: one state has goal distance one and
the other is a dead end. Thus one cut has $2^n$ distinct \hstar-cofactors,
for every total extension.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm-effort} and~\ref{thm-lb} together delimit the
known pathology: its perfect heuristic has exponential width and therefore
evades the bounded-width regime. Our result is a parameterized upper bound,
not a converse: bounded width is sufficient, not necessary, for small buckets.
Moreover \hstar\
uses only $O(n)$ values here, so value count alone does not control the
prefix information entangled by its level sets.

The supplement derives additional consequences for idealized image-call
counts and pruning-only search.

\section{Width-Bounded Heuristic Families}
\label{sec-instances}

Let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous,
$d=\max_{v\in\vars}|D_v|$, and $m=|\vars|$.

\subsection{Pattern Databases}

A pattern database \citep[PDB;][]{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,%
edelkamp-ecp2001} projects \task\ onto a pattern
$P\subseteq\vars$ and stores the abstract goal distance $h^P(s)$ of the
abstract state induced by $s$; this distance is admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pdb}
Let $P\subseteq\vars$ be any pattern under a variable-contiguous order
$\pi$. Extend its table to invalid encodings by ignoring non-pattern
bits and mapping an invalid pattern-variable code to $0$. Let
$|\states^P|=\prod_{v\in P}|D_v|$. Then
\[
\width_\pi(h^P)\leq |\states^P|.
\]
\end{proposition}

The residual lookup is determined by a partial abstract state and the current
bit buffer; invalid prefixes share the valid zero terminal. Pattern variables
need not form a prefix because non-pattern bits are skipped. Hence an
abstract-state budget itself induces a width bound. A PDB has at most
$|\states^P|$ finite values, so its effort ratio is at most
$2|\states^P|^2n$. A distinct invalid-code sink instead gives width at most
$|\states^P|+1$. Max combinations, and additive combinations under an
admissible cost partition, have at most the product of the component-width
bounds; this representational statement does not itself establish
admissibility.

The supplement gives two further specializations.  Integer potentials
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022} with coefficients
bounded by $M$ have width at most $d(2mM+1)$ and at most $mM+1$ values.
Order-aligned linear merge-and-shrink cascades
\citep{helmert-et-al-jacm2014,helmert-et-al-icaps2015} with at most $N$
outputs per table satisfy the following bound.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-ms}
If the linear merge order agrees with $\pi$, every cascade table has at most
$N$ valid outputs, and invalid codes share a sink, then
$\width_\pi(h^\alpha)\leq d(N+1)$.
\end{proposition}
Both bounds are conditional:
the potential bound is pseudo-polynomial in $M$, and the M\&S bound requires
the merge and BDD orders to agree.  Exact post-construction width can still
instantiate the general theorem for other heuristic families.

\section{Heuristic Design: Width-Constrained PDBs}
\label{sec-synthesis}

The co-occurring profile certificate is measured on a completed run, whereas width is its
frontier-independent envelope and can be checked before search.  We therefore
use width as a representation constraint during PDB selection and use safe
terminal coarsening when an informative PDB exceeds the constraint.  Both
selectors build the same pool of PDBs with at most $100{,}000$ abstract states
from five fixed generators, including BDD-order, goal/causal-order, and CEGAR
patterns \citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019}.  Patterns are normalized and
deduplicated while retaining provenance; pairwise traces certify the same raw
pool.  The empty pattern guarantees a feasible width-one endpoint.  The
supplement fixes every generator option and the trace contract.

For each raw PDB, we build the heuristic's total ADD in the fixed search order
and count distinct residual nodes after every Boolean-bit prefix.  Dynamic
reordering is disabled; residuals at skipped manager levels remain in the
frontier, and invalid encodings retain the default-zero extension. Thus the
measured maximum is the exact cofactor width of the total extension used by
search. A candidate
is feasible exactly when its measured width is at most budget $K$.

The exact selector tests only the uncapped PDB.  For each pattern, the
cap-aware selector materializes one raw PDB and derives
\[
  h_\kappa(s)=
  \begin{cases}
    \min\{h(s),\kappa\},&h(s)<\infty,\\
    \infty,&h(s)=\infty
  \end{cases}
\]
for caps $\kappa\in\{0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256\}$ below the raw
maximum finite value, followed by the exact transform.  Proposition
\ref{prop-transform} preserves admissibility and consistency, and the nested
cap order is nondecreasing in pointwise heuristic strength, while decreasing
the cap cannot increase any cut count.  The selector therefore retains the
strongest feasible tested ladder member of each pattern
before comparing patterns. This per-pattern
step prevents a weak cap of one pattern from defeating a stronger feasible
transform of that same pattern.

Both selectors then apply the same frozen lexicographic score: initial-state
dead-end recognition, initial value, mean finite value, and abstract dead-end
fraction, followed by smaller width, abstract-state count, and pattern as tie
breakers.  The score is an information surrogate, not a runtime claim.  A
selection is called \emph{semantic-nontrivial} when its finite-value sum is
positive or it recognizes an abstract dead end.

\begin{proposition}[candidate-set and score dominance]
\label{prop-selector-dominance}
Suppose both selectors complete on the same score-bearing normalized raw-PDB
pool. The
cap-aware final candidate set contains the exact selector's final candidate
set, and its selected lexicographic score is no smaller. Consequently, if the
exact winner recognizes the initial state as a dead end or is
semantic-nontrivial, so is the cap-aware winner. If neither winner recognizes
the initial state as a dead end, the cap-aware initial value is no smaller.
Moreover, if the cap-aware winner is an exact endpoint, both selectors return
the same pattern and heuristic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every exact-feasible raw PDB, the exact transform is the last endpoint of
the cap-aware ladder and is retained as that pattern's strongest feasible
member. Thus every exact candidate remains available when the same score is
maximized. The three consequences follow from the score's first four
components. For the last claim, let $e$ and $p$ be the exact-selector and
cap-aware winners, and suppose $p$ is exact. Candidate containment gives
$\mathrm{score}(p)\geq\mathrm{score}(e)$, while $p$ belongs to the exact set
and hence $\mathrm{score}(e)\geq\mathrm{score}(p)$. The deterministic total
score, including the pattern tie breaker, therefore gives $p=e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[selector safety]
\label{cor-selector-safety}
On every task satisfying Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, each completed selection by
either selector has effort at most $2K^2n$ times blind effort. At $K=8$, this
factor is at most $128n$. If the selected transform has finite cap $\kappa$,
the sharper factor is $2K\min\{K,\kappa+1\}n$.
\end{corollary}

Every returned transform is admissible and consistent, has measured width at
most $K$, and has at most $K$ finite values; a cap has at most $\kappa+1$.
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}. The guarantee concerns representation
effort, not heuristic quality or runtime.

For empirical attribution, we certify trace completeness and reconstructed
raw-pool identity pairwise. The supplement gives the producer, parser, score,
and trace contracts.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec-experiments}

We ask three questions.  \emph{Q1:} Does heuristic information explain search
reduction while cofactor structure explains fragmentation?  \emph{Q2:} Does
the co-occurring profile predict previously unseen changes of representation
order beyond a denominator-only null, and how does it compare with its
Cartesian, width, and ADD relaxations?  \emph{Q3:} What happens when the static
width relaxation is used to construct PDB heuristics?

The source population contains 1,327 positive-cost, axiom-free optimal IPC
tasks in 46 domains.  Formula development used a deterministic
domain-balanced 275-task cohort.  A disjoint 1,052-task, 45-domain cohort was
fixed before launch and remeasured; because those tasks occurred in an
earlier matrix, we do not call it unseen.  Its 17 configurations are profiled
blind search and 16 PDB searches: exact-width, cap-aware width, and cap-aware
matched-ADD selection at $K\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}$, plus unconstrained exact
selection.  Every cell uses the same pinned planner, product-at-evaluation
forward symbolic \astar, a task-specific fixed order, a 300-second aggregate
CPU limit, and 8\,GiB; the matrix has \HoldoutCells\ cells.

For each solved, completely profiled heuristic in the remeasurement matrix, we
compute $\unioneffort$, $\fragfactor$, and normalized structural certificates.
The frozen information tuple---initial dead-end recognition, initial value,
mean finite value, and abstract dead-end fraction---predicts fewer expanded
states and lower unsplit effort; structural scores predict greater
fragmentation.  Comparisons use within-task strict pairs, collapse
semantically duplicate heuristics, weight domains equally, and report all 45
leave-one-domain-out (LODO) values.  This matrix is evidence for the two-effect
decomposition and the selector stress test, but not an unseen-task validation.

The prospective representation experiment supplies the unseen perturbations
for Q2.  It builds one deterministic goal-fill PDB per task and runs it under
the first four positive Gamer-order seeds; seed 0 was used in development and
is excluded.  The seeds were fixed before any corresponding planner output.
A task is eligible only when all four runs have identical patterns, heuristic
values, costs, expanded states, and active values at every $g$; only the BDD
order may change.  The primary comparison of $\jointscore$ with
$1/\unioneffort$ and its exact support, concordance, margin, and LODO gates
were frozen before launch.  Missing cells, inconsistent streams, certificate
violations, or semantic disagreement abort analysis rather than alter a
denominator.  The supplement gives the complete protocol, recovery rules,
development history, and secondary analyses.

\section{Related Work}

\bddastar\ partitions frontiers by value \citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998};
SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\ partition transitions
\citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008}, and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}} uses algebraic diagrams
\citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002}. Even \hstar\ can cause exponential BDD effort
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}; that work identifies size-aware heuristics as
an open direction but gives no structural predictor. Its hard family has
exponential cofactor width.
Residual width, products, and Apply are standard
\citep{wegener-2000,bryant-ieeecomp1986}. New here are their connection to
exact search buckets, the co-occurring residual-pair certificate, its
Cartesian and width relaxations, the cumulative blind-relative bounds, the
Apply incomparability, and the matching planning family.

PDBs, automatic pattern selection, and symbolic compilation predate this work
\citep{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,edelkamp-ecp2001,
haslum-et-al-aaai2007,rovner-et-al-icaps2019,edelkamp-et-al-ecai2012}.
Compression may merge entries or coarsen values
\citep{felner-et-al-jair2007,sturtevant-et-al-aaai2017}; our uniform cap is not
a new primitive. Its role is to preserve consistency, contract every cut, and
meet an exact width budget before ranking. Total ADD size is the closest
incomparable representation budget; our matched-budget experiment tests one
construction regime rather than universal superiority.

Factored M\&S and potentials are established
\citep{torralba-phd2015,helmert-et-al-icaps2015,pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,
fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}; delta-partitioned \ghseta\ is a
different architecture. Relaxed BDD heuristics bound action-choice diagrams
\citep{castro-et-al-icaps2019}, and cofactor width is not planning novelty
width. The certificate fixes its order \citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.
Symbolic/explicit guarantees \citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025} and perimeter
abstractions \citep{torralba-et-al-ijcai2016a,torralba-et-al-aij2018} address
different mechanisms.

\section{Limitations}

The guarantee is specific to fixed-order forward product-at-evaluation search,
positive costs, consistent heuristics, and our duplicate policy. It bounds BDD
nodes, not time or memory; bounded width is sufficient, not necessary, and the
construction is worst case.  The profile is a post-run explanation because it
depends on the expanded unions; width is only a conservative pre-search
relaxation and both quantities depend on the fixed variable order.

Empirical comparisons condition on solved runs with complete profiles and on
within-task strict pairs.  They do not explain timeouts, coverage, or absolute
runtime, and equal-domain concordance is descriptive rather than a
superpopulation estimate.  The information tuple is a frozen surrogate, not a
complete theory of heuristic quality.  The 1,052-task matrix is a frozen
remeasurement, not an unseen-task holdout.  Only the four order seeds are
prospectively unseen, and their intervention uses one goal-fill PDB family.

Most importantly, the primary unseen-order gate fails: $\jointscore$ reaches
\SeedJointMacro, below both its 0.65 floor and the \SeedNullMacro\
denominator-only null.  The absolute co-occurring certificate passes its
secondary gate, and the normalized score is much stronger than the three
structural relaxations, but neither observation establishes an independent or
general performance predictor.  The experiment therefore tests and limits the
explanation rather than confirming it.

Each experiment has one run per cell and a hard cutoff.  Selector coverage
keeps the full \HoldoutTasks-task denominator, but effort ratios condition on
jointly solved, completely profiled pairs and may therefore point in a
different direction.  Candidate-set dominance concerns the shared score, not
search performance.  We report all five budgets and do not use a favorable
budget to replace either frozen aggregate gate.

% ============================ conclusions ================================
\section{Conclusions}

Symbolic heuristics have two separable effects: they change the unsplit states
expanded and fragment those states into value buckets.  The exact
factorization $\effort=\unioneffort\cdot\fragfactor$ exposes the distinction.
The co-occurring profile is a tighter certificate than the Cartesian product,
gives a $2\values Wn$ blind-relative bound with a matching
$\values Wn/120$ family, and contracts under safe coarsening.

The experiments draw a sharper boundary than the intended success story.
Information and Cartesian structure separate the two effects in the frozen
remeasurement.  Under prospective order changes, co-occurrence recovers a
directional signal lost by Cartesian, width, and ADD relaxations, and its
absolute certificate passes the secondary gate.  It does not outperform the
normalized denominator-only null, so the primary predictor claim is rejected.
Width-constrained PDBs improve on unconstrained heuristic selection in pooled
effort and coverage, but lose to blind search.  Cofactor structure therefore
provides rigorous safety bounds and useful diagnostics; predicting whether a
heuristic will improve end-to-end symbolic search still requires both the
search-space and representation effects, not one scalar surrogate.
