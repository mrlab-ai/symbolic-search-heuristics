% ============================== abstract =================================
\begin{abstract}
Cost-optimal symbolic search uses binary decision diagrams (BDDs), yet
heuristic slicing can exponentially increase effort even for the perfect
heuristic. We introduce \emph{cofactor width} $W$, the maximum
residual-function count after a prefix of the fixed Boolean order. A width-$W$,
$T$-terminal heuristic gives total slice size at most $TWn(|B|+2)$ for any
$n$-variable state-set BDD $B$. For positive-cost tasks with optimum \copt,
product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\ with a consistent width-$W$,
$\values$-valued heuristic has effort at most
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt)$ and blind-effort
ratio at most $3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$. The known exponential pathology has
exponential width; an $\Omega(W^2)$ family leaves joint $\values Wn$ tightness
open. Integer potentials, bounded-state PDBs, and aligned linear
merge-and-shrink satisfy it. Image-aware pruning-only search removes the factor
\values\ with one image per active $g$-layer.

We implement an exact-width selector, same-$g$ batching, and a
construction-bounded merge-and-shrink safeguard. On a 50-task screen, capped
and uncapped merge-and-shrink each solve 43; uncapped wins the frozen
PAR2 tie-break; the selector-family winner is the empty width-one PDB
(30/50). Batching lowers coverage; over 24--27 complete pairs per treatment,
completed image calls fall but image time rises. On 92 held-out tasks,
the selector solves 41 versus
CEGAR's 40, but its domain-resampling interval spans zero; the safeguard solves
43 with five blind fallbacks. Width certifies representation safety, not
quality or runtime.
Potentials and pruning-only lie outside the matrix; the 1377-task census
remains pending.
\end{abstract}

% ============================ introduction ===============================
\section{Introduction}

A common approach to solving classical planning tasks optimally is
\astar\ search \citep{hart-et-al-ieeessc1968} with an admissible heuristic
\citep{pearl-1984}. Symbolic search
\citep{mcmillan-1993,edelkamp-helmert-ecp1999} replaces the expansion of
individual states with the expansion of state sets, compactly represented
as binary decision diagrams \citep[BDDs;][]{bryant-ieeecomp1986}. Symbolic
blind search, particularly in its bidirectional form, is a strong approach
to cost-optimal planning
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,speck-et-al-jair2025}, and a key component
of the winning entry of the optimal track of the International Planning
Competition (IPC) 2023.

Combining the two paradigms has a long history. \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} represents a heuristic $h$ as one BDD per
heuristic value and splits each search layer into buckets of states that
share a $g$- and an $h$-value. SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\
\citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008} instead fold the heuristic-value change into
the transition relations, and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
\citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002} stores $h$ as an algebraic decision diagram
(ADD). Empirically, all of these variants beat blind symbolic search in
some domains and lose in others
\citep{torralba-phd2015,torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} explained why: in symbolic search the
effort of an expansion is governed by the number of BDD nodes rather than
the number of represented states, and they prove that even the perfect
heuristic \hstar\ can increase the cumulative size of the expanded BDDs
exponentially. Against this backdrop, heuristic symbolic search has seen
limited uptake. A notable recent direction uses operator-potential heuristics
\citep{fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}, which integrate
LP-based potential heuristics \citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015} into
\ghseta\ and empirically ``hardly ever suffer'' from increased BDD sizes.
This evidence motivates a structural explanation, but does not itself give
a condition under which a heuristic \emph{provably} cannot cause a large
representational overhead. Recently,
\citet{speck-helmert-ecai2025} gave worst-case performance
guarantees for symbolic search relative to explicit search, but their
analysis concerns the representation of transition relations and goal
formulas and explicitly leaves heuristics aside.

In this paper we introduce \emph{width-bounded heuristics}. Under the search
variable order, cofactor width $W$ is the largest number of distinct residual
functions after any prefix, equivalently the largest cut through the
heuristic's quasi-reduced total ADD. Reduced ADDs may skip levels, so displayed
level size need not equal width.

We make six contributions. First, the \emph{fragmentation theorem}
bounds the total size of the slices of every state-set BDD $B$ by
$TWn(|B|+2)$ for a width-$W$, $T$-terminal heuristic. It covers
frontier fragmentation and the exponential BDD growth possible under subsets.
Second, product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\ with a consistent width-$W$
heuristic taking \values\ finite values has cumulative BDD size at most
$\values Wn$ times blind effort plus the layer-additive term, and a blind-effort
ratio at most
$3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$. Third, the pathology of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} has width $2^{\Omega(n)}$, while a new family
realizes $\Omega(W^2)$ overhead; because it has $\values=W$ and
$n=\Omega(W)$, joint $\values Wn$ tightness remains open. Fourth, integer
potentials, bounded-state PDBs, and linear merge-and-shrink aligned with the
search order are width-bounded; the last has width at most $d(N+1)$. This
certifies integer-potential level sets, not delta-partitioned operator-potential
search. Fifth, an image-aware model counts high-level image operations, and
pruning-only search removes the factor \values\ by preserving blind layers and
using heuristics only for sound discards.

Sixth, we operationalize safety as an exact-width selector in SymK
\citep{speck-et-al-jair2025}: it filters a fixed PDB pool by a declared budget
before deterministic informativeness ranking. We also implement same-$g$
speculative batching and a construction-bounded merge-and-shrink safeguard,
then compare
against blind, conventional PDB, and merge-and-shrink controls in frozen
screen and predeclared held-out stages. P5's seven-configuration held-out
matrix carries unchanged into the separately attested, pending P6 full-suite
stage. Archived outcomes shaped only the screen composition; all reported
performance observations come from compatible Arrhenius logs.

% ============================ background =================================
\section{Background}

We use finite deterministic SAS$^{+}$ tasks
$\task=\langle\vars,\ops,\sinit,\sgoal\rangle$
\citep{backstrom-nebel-compint1995}. Operators have positive integer costs.
The functions $\gstar(s)$ and $\hstar(s)$ are respectively the cheapest
cost from \sinit\ to $s$ and from $s$ to a goal, with value $\infty$ for a
dead end. We write $\copt=\hstar(\sinit)$ and assume
$0<\copt<\infty$. A heuristic
$h\colon\states\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible}
if $h\leq\hstar$, and \emph{consistent} if
$h(s)\leq\cost(o)+h(s[o])$ for every applicable $o$ and $h=0$ on goals.
The blind heuristic is $\hblind\equiv0$.

Fix a Boolean state encoding in which every SAS$^{+}$ variable $v$ uses
$\lceil\log_2|D_v|\rceil$ Boolean bits. Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be those
bits in order $\pi$, and give every heuristic a total extension to Boolean
strings that encode no state. The order is \emph{variable-contiguous} if
all bits of each SAS$^{+}$ variable are consecutive. A state set $S$ is represented
by the reduced ordered BDD of its characteristic function $\chi_S$;
$\bddsize{S}$ counts its inner nodes \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986}. For a prefix
$\rho\in\{0,1\}^i$, let $f|_\rho$ be the residual function and
$\sub{i}{f}=\{f|_\rho\mid\rho\in\{0,1\}^i\}$ its distinct cofactors.

\begin{definition}[cofactor count]
\label{def-cofcount}
For a function $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the \emph{cofactor count} is
$\cofcount(f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} |\sub{i}{f}|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem-qsize}
For every Boolean $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$,
$\bddsize{f} \leq \cofcount(f) \leq n \cdot (\bddsize{f} + 2)$.
\end{lemma}

$\cofcount(f)$ is the inner-node count of the quasi-reduced OBDD. At every
cut, reduced nodes inject into cofactors, while every cofactor is a constant
or is rooted at a unique reduced node; this proves the lemma. Its factor
$n$ is unavoidable already for $f=x_1$, where
$\cofcount(f)=2n-1$ but $\bddsize{f}=1$.

Symbolic uniform-cost search represents each exact layer
$L_g=\{s\mid\gstar(s)=g\}$ by one BDD
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}. Product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} intersects layers with the level sets
$\level{h}{v}=\{s\mid h(s)=v\}$. It removes closed states, discards
$h=\infty$, represents each $(g,v)$ bucket by one BDD, selects by increasing
$(f,g)=(g+v,g)$, and tests for a goal when a bucket is selected
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. These choices and the positive-cost
assumption define the scope of our cumulative guarantees.

\begin{definition}[expansion size, \citealp{speck-et-al-icaps2020}]
\label{def-effort}
Let $B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ denote the unique BDD expanded by \bddastar\ with
$g$-value $g$ and $h$-value $v$ on task \task\ with heuristic $h$, with
$B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ representing the empty set if no such expansion
occurs. The \emph{expansion size} of \bddastar\ is
$\effort(\task,h) = \sum_{\,g + v \leq \copt,\ g < \copt}
|B_{\task,h}(g,v)|$.
\end{definition}

For $\hblind$, \bddastar\ is uniform-cost search and
$\effort(\task,\hblind) = \sum_{g < \copt} \bddsize{L_g}$.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} prove that there are tasks on which even
$\hstar$ has exponentially larger effort
than blind search: a subset can have a much larger BDD than its superset,
so both slicing and pruning can inflate representations. We seek a
heuristic property that controls every value-selected subset.

% ===================== width-bounded heuristics ==========================
\section{Bounded Cofactor Width}

\begin{definition}[cofactor width]
\label{def-width}
The \emph{cofactor width} of a heuristic $h$ under variable order $\pi$
is $\width_\pi(h) = \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} |\sub{i}{h}|$. We call $h$
\emph{$(\pi,W)$-width-bounded} if $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and write
\emph{width} for cofactor width throughout.
\end{definition}

The cofactors are precisely the states of a deterministic layered automaton
that reads $\pi$; hence width is also the largest level of the heuristic's
quasi-reduced multi-terminal BDD \citep{wegener-2000}. It is a
representation property under the search order, not planning novelty width
\citep{lipovetzky-geffner-ecai2012}.

\begin{proposition}[value range]
\label{prop-range}
If $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, then $|\img(h)| \leq W$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, the final cofactors are the attained constants. Write
$\values=|h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}|$ for finite values on planning
states and $T=|\img(h)|$ for terminals of the total extension; then
$\values\leq T\leq W$.

\begin{proposition}[closure]
\label{prop-closure}
Let $h_1, h_2$ have widths $W_1, W_2$ under $\pi$ and let
$\oplus \in \{+, \max, \min\}$. Then
$\width_\pi(h_1 \oplus h_2) \leq W_1 \cdot W_2$.
\end{proposition}

At each cut, a combined cofactor is determined by a pair of operand
cofactors. Thus maximization and admissible additive combinations remain
width-bounded, with multiplicative component bounds.

Call a possibly signed function
$q\colon\states\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$ a \emph{raw admissible and
consistent estimate} if $q(s)\leq \hstar(s)$ for every state,
$q(s)\leq c+q(s')$ for every transition $s\xrightarrow{c}s'$, and
$q(s)\leq0$ on goals.

\begin{proposition}[safe terminal transforms]
\label{prop-transform}
Let $q$ be a raw admissible and consistent estimate, equipped with a
total extension
$q \colon \{0,1\}^{n} \to \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}$. Let
$\phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}
 \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \cup \{\infty\}$
be nondecreasing, satisfy $\phi(0)=0$, $\phi(\infty)=\infty$, and
\[
\phi(a+c) \leq \phi(a)+c
\qquad
\text{for every $a\in\mathbb{Z}$ and integer cost $c\geq 0$.}
\]
Then $h=\phi\circ q$ is admissible and consistent and
$\width_\pi(h)\leq\width_\pi(q)$.
\end{proposition}

Monotonicity and the cost condition transfer consistency and imply
$\phi(a)\leq\max(0,a)$; terminal mapping cannot create cofactors. Thus both
rectification and the cap
$\phi_\kappa(a)=\min(\max(0,a),\kappa)$ for finite $a$ (and
$\phi_\kappa(\infty)=\infty$) are safe and width nonincreasing. The cap has
at most $\kappa+1$ finite values and is pointwise strongest among such safe
transforms with that output ceiling.

Let $\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)$ be the reduced ordered ADD under $\pi$
\citep{bahar-et-al-iccad1993}.

\begin{proposition}[ADD characterization]
\label{prop-add}
$\width_\pi(h) \leq |\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| + |\img(h)|$ and
$|\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| \leq n \cdot \width_\pi(h)$.
\end{proposition}

Every cofactor is a terminal or a residual ADD node, and every ADD node at
a level is a distinct cofactor. Hence small width and a small ADD are
polynomially equivalent only in the order actually used by the search
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.

\begin{remark}[total extensions]
\label{rem-encoding}
Definitions over $\{0,1\}^n$ require fixing $h$ on Boolean assignments
that encode no state. Upper bounds apply to the chosen extension; the
families below use natural arithmetic or table extensions. Lower bounds
witnessed on genuine states apply to every extension.
\end{remark}

% ======================= fragmentation theorems ==========================
\section{Bounded Fragmentation}
\label{sec-fragmentation}

Fix $\pi$ and write $W=\width_\pi(h)$. At every cut, the cofactor of a
value-selected subset is determined by the pair consisting of the state-set
cofactor and the heuristic cofactor.

\begin{theorem}[bucket bound]
\label{thm-bucket}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and $U \subseteq \img(h)$. Then
\[
\bddsize{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}
\;\leq\; \cofcount(\chi_{S \cap h^{-1}(U)})
\;\leq\; W \cdot \cofcount(\chi_S)
\;\leq\; W n \, (\bddsize{S} + 2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At cut $i$, equal pairs $(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)$ induce equal slice
cofactors, so their number is at most
$|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|\,|\sub{i}{h}|\leq
W|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|$. Sum over cuts and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize}. The supplement gives the explicit injection.
\end{proof}

This covers a bucket ($U=\{v\}$), recognized-dead-end removal, and
threshold pruning. No value-selected subset can inflate the reduced BDD by
more than $Wn$ up to the additive term.

\begin{theorem}[partition bound]
\label{thm-partition}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$. Then
\[
\sum_{v \in \img(h)} \bddsize{S \cap \level{h}{v}}
\;\leq\; T W \cdot \cofcount(\chi_S)
\;\leq\; T W n \, (\bddsize{S} + 2)
\;\leq\; W^{2} n \, (\bddsize{S} + 2).
\]
If $S\subseteq\states$ and the sum ranges only over finite values attained
on planning states, the first two bounds hold with $T$ replaced by $\values$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to each of $T$ buckets (or the $\values$
finite planning-state buckets), then use $T\leq W$.
\end{proof}

The effort measure sums each reduced bucket separately even if a shared
unique table reuses nodes. The factor $n$ cannot be removed: the supplement
gives a width-two parity slice whose reduced BDD is $\Theta(n)$ times its
one-node source set.

% ======================= search-effort guarantees ========================
\section{Search-Effort Guarantees}
\label{sec-effort}

\begin{lemma}[bucket identity]
\label{lem-identity}
Let $h$ be consistent. \bddastar\ on \task\ with $h$ selects every
bucket key $(g,v)$ with $g + v \leq \copt$ and $g < \copt$ and
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v} \neq \emptyset$ exactly once before terminating,
and the BDD expanded with key $(g,v)$ represents exactly
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v}$. No other bucket is expanded before
termination.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Consistency makes selection lexicographic in $(f,g)$; positive costs then
prevent a selected key from being repopulated. Induction along cheapest
paths gives optimal $g$-values and complete slices. An open prefix of an
optimal plan prevents termination or selection above $\copt$. The
supplement proves these five claims in full.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[effort bound]
\label{thm-effort}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. Then
\[
\effort(\task, h)
\;\leq\; \values W n \cdot
\bigl(\effort(\task, \hblind) + 2\copt\bigr)
\;\leq\; W^{2} n \cdot
\bigl(\effort(\task, \hblind) + 2\copt\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} and Theorem~\ref{thm-partition} bound all selected
slices of each nonempty $L_g$ by
$\values Wn(\bddsize{L_g}+2)$. If $N$ is the number of nonempty layers
below $\copt$, summing gives
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N)$. Positive integer costs imply
$N\leq\copt$, and $\values\leq W$ gives the coarse bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-effort}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort},
$\effort(\task, h) \leq 3\,\values W n \cdot
\effort(\task, \hblind) \leq
3\, W^{2} n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

Every nonempty pre-goal layer is a proper, nonconstant set and therefore
has at least one BDD node, so $N\leq\effort(\task,\hblind)$.

The guarantee is one-sided: pruning savings remain unbounded. It concerns
frontier sizes in forward product-at-evaluation \bddastar, not primitive
relational products in SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} or \ghseta, and not cumulative
bidirectional effort. Variants visiting the same semantic buckets inherit
the state-set bound.

\subsection{A Quadratic Ratio in $W$ Is Realizable}

\begin{theorem}[quadratic width-ratio family]
\label{thm-tight}
For all integers $W \geq 2$ and $k \geq 4W$ there are tasks
$\task_{k,W}$ with $n = \Theta(k)$ Boolean variables and consistent,
admissible heuristics $h$ with $\width_\pi(h) = W$ such that
$\effort(\task_{k,W}, h) = \Omega(W^{2}) \cdot
\effort(\task_{k,W}, \hblind)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Use a $k$-step binary-choice chain followed by a cost-$W$ goal action and
order the chain bits before the choice bits. The non-goal heuristic is the
negated choice residue modulo $W$; action costs make it admissible and
consistent, and its $W$ residuals give exact width $W$. Blind effort is
$\Theta(k^2)$. On $\Theta(k)$ late layers, all $W$ buckets are selected
and each has $\Omega(kW)$ nodes, yielding $\Omega(k^2W^2)$ effort. The
supplement gives the construction and cofactor witnesses.
\end{proof}

This realizes $\Omega(W^2)$ but does not make the joint $\values Wn$
upper bound tight: the construction has $\values=W$ and $n=\Omega(W)$, so
it leaves open, for example, an $O(Wn)$ bound. It is not a typical-case
prediction.

\subsection{Exponential Width Is Necessary for the Known Pathology}

In the family of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, chain variables precede
two blocks $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and $v_{n+1},\ldots,v_{2n}$ in $\pi_n$.
At the critical layer, a state is not a dead end exactly when
$\bigvee_i(v_i\wedge v_{n+i})$ holds, producing their exponential bucket.

\begin{theorem}[width lower bound]
\label{thm-lb}
For the tasks $\task_n$ and orders $\pi_n$ of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020},
$\width_{\pi_n}(\hstar) \geq 2^{n}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the cut after the first $v$-block, encode each $A\subseteq[n]$ as a
prefix. If $i\in A\setminus A'$, the valid suffix setting only
$v_{n+i}$ distinguishes the prefixes: one state has goal distance one and
the other is a dead end. Thus one cut has $2^n$ distinct \hstar-cofactors,
for every total extension.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm-effort} and~\ref{thm-lb} together delimit the
known pathology: its perfect heuristic has exponential width, and any
consistent heuristic causing superpolynomial deterioration over blind
search must have superpolynomial width under the search order. Bounded
width is sufficient, not necessary, for small buckets. Moreover \hstar\
uses only $O(n)$ values here, so value count alone does not control the
prefix information entangled by its level sets.

\subsection{A Refined Cost Model: Image Calls}
\label{sec-cost}

\begin{definition}[image-aware effort]
\label{def-image-effort}
For $\alpha \geq 0$, the \emph{$\alpha$-effort} of a symbolic search run
is $\effort_\alpha=\sum_B(\alpha+|B|)$ over high-level image calls and
their argument BDDs.
\end{definition}

At $\alpha=0$, this equals Definition~\ref{def-effort} when one call is
made per expanded bucket.

\begin{proposition}[image-call counts]
\label{prop-calls}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h)\leq W$, and let $N_\ell$ be the
number of nonempty semantic blind layers $L_g$ with
$g<\copt$.  An idealized blind implementation that stores each $L_g$ in
one BDD performs $N_\ell$ counted high-level image invocations.
An implementation of \bddastar\ that invokes its cost-image routine once
for every expanded nonempty $(g,v)$ bucket performs
$\sum_g\values_g$ such invocations, where $\values_g$ is the number of
nonempty expanded buckets in layer $g$ and
$\values_g\leq\values\leq W$. Hence it performs at most
$W N_\ell$ calls, and at most $\values N_\ell$ when the finite
valid-state value count is known.
\end{proposition}

Each active layer has at most $\values\leq W$ nonempty buckets, proving the
claim. The construction of Theorem~\ref{thm-tight} realizes
$\Theta(W)$ calls per late layer. This counts high-level cost-image calls,
not the delta-partition relational products of \ghseta; the supplement
gives the full operational qualification.

\subsection{Pruning-Only Search}
\label{sec-prune}

Pruning-only search keeps one BDD per uniform-cost layer and uses an
incumbent solution cost $c$ only to discard $h=\infty$ and
$g+h\geq c$, reapplying the current bound at expansion and stopping when
the next layer cannot improve $c$. Admissibility
makes the discards sound. If $c_g$ is the incumbent when layer $g$ is
expanded, consistency along cheapest paths gives the exact slice
$L_g\cap h^{-1}(\{v\mid v<c_g-g\})$.

\begin{corollary}[pruning bound]
\label{cor-prune}
Let $h$ be admissible and consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. The
cumulative size of the layers expanded by pruning-only search is at most
$W n \cdot (\effort(\task, \hblind) + 2\copt)$, removing the factor
$\values$ due to value partitioning from the refined \bddastar\ bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, with one image operation per layer as in blind
search. With $c = \infty$ throughout
(no solution found yet) and no dead ends, its semantic layers and expansions
coincide with blind forward search; heuristic construction and evaluation can
still consume time.
\end{corollary}

Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} proves the corollary for one threshold slice per
layer. Its $\alpha$-effort is bounded by the same node term plus
$\alpha N_\ell$. Pruning is pointwise sound in either direction of
bidirectional search, but we claim no cumulative bidirectional bound:
closed sets, meeting points, incumbents, and the direction schedule feed
back on the frontiers. Details appear in the supplement.


% ============================ instances ==================================
\section{Width-Bounded Heuristic Families}
\label{sec-instances}

Let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous,
$d=\max_{v\in\vars}|D_v|$, and $m=|\vars|$.

\subsection{Potential Heuristics}

Integer potentials \citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022}
form $p(s)=\sum_vP(v,s(v))$ and expose the safe rectification
$\hpot=\max(0,p)$. Their standard constraints make $p$ a raw admissible
and consistent estimate, so Proposition~\ref{prop-transform} makes
$\hpot$ admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pot}
Let $p$ have integer potentials with $|P(v,w)|\leq M$ for all facts,
extended to non-state assignments by the same sum over the bit
encoding's induced table, with table values on invalid bit codes also
in $[-M,M]$, e.g., $0$, and let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous. Then
\[
\width_\pi(\hpot)\leq\width_\pi(p)\leq d(2mM+1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|\img(\hpot)|\leq mM+1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-pot}
For rectified consistent integer potential heuristics with magnitudes at
most $M$,
\[
\effort(\task,\hpot)
 \leq 3d(mM+1)(2mM+1)n\cdot\effort(\task,\hblind),
\]
a factor polynomial in the task size and the numeric magnitude $M$.
\end{corollary}

The bound counts the accumulated sum and the at most $d$ partial codes of
the current variable; Corollary~\ref{cor-pot} substitutes it into
Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}. It is pseudo-polynomial in numeric $M$, not
polynomial in its bit length, and does not analyze delta-partitioned
\ghseta.

\subsection{Pattern Databases}

A pattern database \citep[PDB;][]{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,%
edelkamp-ecp2001} projects \task\ onto a pattern
$P\subseteq\vars$ and stores the abstract goal distance $h^P(s)$ of the
abstract state induced by $s$; this distance is admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pdb}
Let $P\subseteq\vars$ be any pattern under a variable-contiguous order
$\pi$. Extend its table to invalid encodings by ignoring non-pattern
bits and mapping an invalid pattern-variable code to a fixed sink
terminal. Let
$|\states^P|=\prod_{v\in P}|D_v|$. Then
\[
\width_\pi(h^P)\leq d\,|\states^P|.
\]
\end{proposition}

The residual lookup is determined by a partial abstract state and the
current bit buffer. Pattern variables need not form a prefix: non-pattern
bits are skipped. Hence the usual abstract-state budget is also a width
budget. It has at most $|\states^P|$ finite values, so a single PDB has
effort ratio at most $3d|\states^P|^2n$. Combining PDBs multiplies
component-width bounds.

\subsection{Linear Merge-and-Shrink}

Merge-and-shrink (M\&S) abstractions
\citep{helmert-et-al-jacm2014} iteratively merge factors of a factored
transition system and shrink intermediate abstractions to at most $N$
abstract states. With a \emph{linear} merge strategy, the abstraction
mapping is representable by cascading tables, equivalently a factored
mapping \citep{helmert-et-al-icaps2015}: a chain of tables
$\tau_1, \dots, \tau_m$ where $\tau_j$ maps the pair of the previous
intermediate abstract state and the value of variable $v_j$ to the next
intermediate abstract state. Its final abstract goal distance is admissible
and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-ms}
Let $h^{\alpha}$ be an M\&S heuristic built with a linear merge
strategy whose variable order agrees with the variable-contiguous
$\pi$ and with intermediate abstraction sizes at most $N$. Extend each
table with a distinguished sink for invalid variable codes. Then
$\width_\pi(h^{\alpha}) \leq d \cdot (N+1)$.
\end{proposition}

The table cascade remembers at most $N$ intermediate states (plus the
invalid-code sink) and one bit buffer. The final abstraction has at most
$N$ finite distances, giving effort ratio at most $3dN(N+1)n$.

The alignment premise is essential. Our causal-graph control explicitly
uses \texttt{align\_merge\_order=false}; it is \emph{not} covered by the
a priori formula, though its exact post-construction width in the search
order can instantiate the general theorems. Alignment supplies the formula
but may change heuristic quality. No analogous small-width guarantee is
known for $h^{\max}$, $h^{\mathrm{add}}$, or LM-cut, and
Theorem~\ref{thm-lb} shows that perfect information can require exponential
width. Full family proofs appear in the supplement.

\section{Width-Constrained Heuristic Selection}
\label{sec-synthesis}

The theory motivates a constraint, not a width-only objective: first enforce a
representation-safety budget and then rank the feasible heuristics for
information. Under our total-ADD and goal-zero conventions, width one has only
the attained value zero, so $K=1$ deliberately includes the blind endpoint.
For each candidate we build one total ADD in the fixed search order and count
its distinct residual nodes after every variable prefix; CUDD identity makes
the maximum frontier size the exact cofactor width. Dynamic reordering is
disabled, and terminal and ADD-size checks validate the traversal. Finite PDB
distances are ordinary terminals, abstract dead ends use a distinct sentinel,
and invalid abstract-state codes retain the fixed zero terminal. Residuals at
skipped ADD levels remain in the frontier, so the measurement covers the
implementation's total extension rather than only finite level sets.

Every run constructs the same provenance-preserving, deduplicated pool under
the abstract-state budget $B=100{,}000$: the empty pattern, BDD-order and
goal/causal-order prefixes, a goal-fill pattern, and one CEGAR pattern
\citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019} with seed 2011 and a 10-second construction
limit. A candidate is feasible iff its measured width is at most $K$; none is
grown or repaired after measurement. Patterns are normalized before
materialization, and identical patterns retain every generator label when
deduplicated. The fixed lexicographic score first
prefers initial-state dead-end recognition, then $h(\sinit)$, mean finite
distance, and abstract dead-end fraction, with smaller width, size, and pattern
as late tie-breakers. These are informativeness surrogates, not a runtime
claim.

Same-$g$ batching unions the selected $(g,h)$ bucket with eligible fresh
buckets at that same $g$ and images the union once. Extra sources remain
pending at their original keys: their goal tests, closing, and logical
selection still follow the ordinary $(f,g)$ order. The implementation parks
already-imaged sources, partitions successors immediately, and checks logical
traces and solution costs against an unbatched oracle. We test a fixed
$\Delta=16$ window and gated $\Delta=64$ windows; batching may save calls yet
perform speculative work.

Finally, a construction deadline can discard an incomplete M\&S heuristic and
continue with blind search while charging its setup time. Finite-value capping
preserves consistency and cannot increase width. The evaluated 10,000-state
causal-graph M\&S controls set
\texttt{align\_merge\_order=false}; their exact widths are measured, but the
$d(N+1)$ guarantee for aligned orders does not apply. The supplement gives
the complete algorithms, score, batching gates, and fallback contract.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec-experiments}

We ask how much heuristic information survives a width constraint, whether
same-$g$ batching amortizes images, and how construction cost changes the
comparison. All treatments use forward symbolic search with a task-specific
fixed BDD order on Arrhenius; nonblind heuristics use product-at-evaluation
\bddastar, while blind and fallback runs use the equivalent $h\equiv0$ path.
The 300-second aggregate process-CPU limit includes heuristic construction;
memory is 8\,GiB on one AMD EPYC 9755 core. Micro-PAR2 averages reported CPU
time for solved cells and 600 seconds otherwise. No older-cluster outcome is
pooled into this evaluation.

The screen crosses five controls---blind, uncapped and value-32-capped
10,000-state M\&S, goal-fill PDB, and CEGAR PDB---with fifteen selector
treatments. Ten are unbatched
$K\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256,\infty\}$; four combine selected budgets
with gated $\Delta=64$ batching, and one uses $K=32,\Delta=16$. The source
inventory has 1697 optimal sequential IPC tasks through 2023. Frozen,
disjoint exclusions of 290 zero-cost and 30 normalized-axiom tasks leave 1377
positive-cost, axiom-free tasks. Support is established by direct SAS scans or
a pinned-parser unit-cost proof for resource-heavy translations, with a
separate post-normalization axiom attestation; every selected PDDL source is
byte-attested. The \texttt{release\_no\_lp} matrix excludes potential
heuristics.

\paragraph{Stage 1 (P4 screen).}
The frozen manifest has 50 tasks, exactly two from each of 25 domains, and 20
configurations (1000 cells). It was assembled during development using
archived outcome summaries to enrich difficult or discriminating cases.
Those measurements affected screen composition only and are neither pooled
with P4 nor performance observations; consequently P4 is a selection screen,
not confirmatory evidence. Its single lexicographic order maximizes coverage,
then minimizes micro-PAR2, conditionally total image time, and label. It fixes
both the overall and selector-family winners.

\paragraph{Stage 2 (P5 held-out comparison).}
The separately frozen, outcome-independent manifest has 92 disjoint tasks,
two from each of 46 domains. Within each domain, a fixed-seed SHA-256 ordering
selects the first two eligible names without any planner outcome. P5 adds the
unbatched Stage-1 selector-family winner without retuning to six independently
fixed configurations: the five
controls plus value-32-capped M\&S with a 60-second build limit and blind
fallback. The screen winner is an alias, yielding seven unique configurations
and 644 cells. The primary selector-minus-CEGAR estimand averages paired
coverage equally over domains; a deterministic 100,000-replicate domain
bootstrap gives its 95\% interval. Other comparisons are descriptive.
The interval resamples the 46 fixed domains; it captures neither task
selection nor run-to-run uncertainty and is not an interval for the census.

\paragraph{Stage 3 (P6 full census).}
P6 carries the same seven configurations unchanged to all 1377 tasks (9639
cells), including the screen and held-out tasks. It is a descriptive census,
not another unseen-task test; its sole sensitivity excludes the 50 screen
tasks. P5 and P6 never reopen or rerank the selector, matrix, parameters,
contrasts, or statistical policy. P4, P5, and P6 have separately frozen source
and launch identities throughout. Missing, duplicate, unexpected, or
unattested cells invalidate a stage instead of changing a denominator. The
supplement gives the complete execution, acceptance, measurement, provenance,
and recovery protocol, including the prospective corrections that produced
accepted P4 and P5 and the currently pending P6.

\subsection{Results}

\paragraph{P4 screen.}
All 1000 cells passed protocol checks. Uncapped and value-32-capped M\&S each
solved 43/50; uncapped M\&S won the frozen ordering because its micro-PAR2 was
1.3706 seconds lower. The selector-family winner was $K=1$: it solved 30/50,
tied blind coverage but raised micro-PAR2 by 8.3548 seconds, and selected the
empty width-one PDB in all 50 certified traces, hence was semantically
$h\equiv0$ with wrapper overhead. Batching
lowered coverage. On only 24--27 raw-complete certified pairs per treatment it
reduced completed image calls, but increased image time; the omitted pairs are
censored. Only 589/1000 cells were raw-complete for total-image aggregation,
so the ranking's image-time tie-break was unused. All 950 heuristic
constructions completed, with no construction or pattern fallback. These
screen estimates are descriptive and development-selected.

\paragraph{P5 held-out result.}
All 644 cells passed protocol checks. The frozen width-one selector solved
41/92 and CEGAR 40/92. With two tasks per domain, the domain-macro and
task-micro differences both equal $1/92=1.09$ percentage points. The
predeclared 100,000-replicate domain-bootstrap 95\% interval was
$[-1/46,1/23]$, or $[-2.17,4.35]$ points, with two discordant wins and one
loss; it therefore does not establish a coverage advantage. All 91 eligible
selector traces again chose the blind-equivalent empty PDB. Descriptively, the
60-second capped-M\&S safeguard led the seven-configuration matrix with 43/92
solved, micro-PAR2 334.1384 seconds, and five blind fallbacks. The selector's
micro-PAR2 was 345.1040 versus CEGAR's 351.5588; on 39 jointly solved tasks its
planner-CPU geometric-mean ratio was 1.006. No P5 outcome reranked any
configuration. The supplement reports both tables and every operational
comparison with its eligible, certified, complete, solved, or paired
denominator.

\section{Related Work}

\bddastar\ partitions frontiers by heuristic value
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998}; SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\ instead
partition transitions \citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008}, while ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
uses algebraic diagrams \citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002}. Even \hstar\ can
increase BDD-node effort exponentially \citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}; our
width condition is a representation-safety certificate for one of these
architectures, not a general runtime theorem.

PDBs and their symbolic use are established
\citep{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,edelkamp-ecp2001}; CEGAR generation
\citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019}, symbolic heuristic compilation
\citep{edelkamp-et-al-ecai2012}, and factored M\&S mappings
\citep{torralba-phd2015,helmert-et-al-icaps2015} also predate this work.
Potential heuristics provide another compact family
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}.
We claim neither the pattern generators nor symbolic compilation as new. Our
methodological contribution is to deduplicate a fixed pool with provenance,
measure each total ADD exactly in the search order, and reject over-budget
candidates before ranking. The analytic M\&S bound requires aligned merge and
search orders; the evaluated causal-graph control is deliberately unaligned.

Our batching differs from whole-$g$ rows, best-set-first grouping, and bounded
transition trees \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,torralba-et-al-icaps2013}:
sources share one speculative image but remain logically pending at their
original keys, with trace and solution-cost preservation checked by an exact
oracle. Related work on symbolic versus explicit blind search
\citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025}, variable ordering
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}, and symbolic perimeter abstractions
\citep{torralba-et-al-ijcai2016a,torralba-et-al-aij2018} addresses orthogonal
representation choices. The supplement details these distinctions and our
narrower methodological claims.

\section{Conclusions}

For positive-cost product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\ under a fixed
variable order, cofactor width is a representation-safety certificate. A
width-$W$, $T$-terminal heuristic's slices total at most $TWn$ times any BDD's
augmented size. For a consistent width-$W$, $\values$-valued heuristic, effort
is at most $\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt)$ and at most
$3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$ times blind effort, using $T,\values\leq W$. The known
exponential pathology has exponential width; an $\Omega(W^2)$ family leaves
joint $\values Wn$ tightness open. Integer potentials, bounded-state PDBs, and
aligned linear M\&S are width-bounded. The certificate bounds harm, not
quality, construction cost, or runtime.

We measure exact total-ADD width, filter a fixed PDB pool before deterministic
informativeness ranking, batch same-$g$ images without changing logical order,
and give construction-bounded M\&S a blind fallback. The $d(N+1)$ M\&S bound
requires aligned merge and search orders; the evaluated
\texttt{align\_merge\_order=false} control lies outside it. In an image-aware
model, pruning-only search removes the factor \values\ from the representation
bound using one image per active $g$-layer, but lies outside the empirical
matrix.

The 50-task screen is local: uncapped M\&S wins among 20 configurations; the
selector-family winner is the width-one empty PDB, semantically
$h\equiv0$, which ties blind coverage but is slower. Batching lowers coverage;
on complete pairs it cuts image calls but raises image time. On 92 disjoint
held-out tasks, the selector solves 41 against CEGAR's 40, but its
domain-resampling interval spans zero and it remains blind-equivalent. Capped
M\&S descriptively heads the seven-configuration matrix, solving 43 with
five fallbacks. The pending 1377-task full-suite census must fill its
placeholder in Section~\ref{sec-experiments} before generalization. Width is
the safety constraint; information, image amortization, and bounded setup cost
are separate objectives.

Open questions include better scores without held-out adaptation, larger pools
without width bottlenecks, adaptive batching, zero-cost and bidirectional
bounds, and joint $\values Wn$ tightness. Joint abstraction, merge-order, and
BDD-order selection could couple quality to certified safety.
