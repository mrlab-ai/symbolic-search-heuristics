% ============================== abstract =================================
\begin{abstract}
In product-at-evaluation forward symbolic \astar, heuristic values partition
compact BDD frontiers into buckets; even a perfect heuristic can thereby
increase cumulative BDD-node effort exponentially. We apply fixed-order
cofactor width $W$, the maximum number of distinct heuristic residuals after
any prefix. On solvable positive-cost tasks, a consistent heuristic with
$\values\leq W$ finite values has BDD-node effort at most
$3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$ times that of blind forward search, for $n$ state
bits. The known pathology has exponential width under its analyzed order,
whereas a new family realizes an $\Omega(W^2)$ BDD-effort ratio. We also derive
incomparable cut-sensitive width and total-ADD bounds and certify integer
potentials, bounded-state pattern databases (PDBs), and linear
merge-and-shrink aligned with the search order.

We evaluate exact-width PDB selection and a construction-bounded
merge-and-shrink safeguard. On a 50-task selection screen, the winning
$K=1$ selector always chooses the empty PDB and ties blind coverage (30/50);
an uncertified batching probe loses coverage in every treatment. On a
disjoint 92-task sample, the selector solves 41 tasks versus 40 for a
counterexample-guided PDB, but its domain-resampling interval includes zero
and all 91 eligible traces choose the empty pattern. On the 1377-task census,
capped merge-and-shrink solves 633 tasks; the selector solves 612, three fewer
than blind, and again chooses the empty PDB in all 1355 certified traces.
Cofactor width therefore certifies representational safety in this search
architecture, not heuristic quality, construction cost, or runtime.
\end{abstract}

% ============================ introduction ===============================
\section{Introduction}

Heuristics guide explicit \astar\ by ranking states
\citep{hart-et-al-ieeessc1968,pearl-1984}, but in symbolic search the same
information partitions compact frontiers. Symbolic search expands state sets
represented as binary decision diagrams
\citep[BDDs;][]{mcmillan-1993,edelkamp-helmert-ecp1999,bryant-ieeecomp1986};
because BDD size is not monotone under subsets, an informative split can be
representationally costly. Blind symbolic search, particularly in its
bidirectional form, remains a strong approach to cost-optimal planning
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,speck-et-al-jair2025}, and a key component
of the winning entry of the optimal track of the International Planning
Competition (IPC) 2023 \citep{speck-ipc2023}.

Combining the two paradigms has a long history. \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} represents a heuristic $h$ as one BDD per
heuristic value and splits each search layer into buckets of states that
share a $g$- and an $h$-value. SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\
\citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008} instead fold the heuristic-value change into
the transition relations, and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
\citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002} stores $h$ as an algebraic decision diagram
(ADD). Empirically, all of these variants beat blind symbolic search in
some domains and lose in others
\citep{torralba-phd2015,torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} explained why: in symbolic search the
effort of an expansion is governed by the number of BDD nodes rather than
the number of represented states, and they prove that even the perfect
heuristic \hstar\ can increase the cumulative size of the expanded BDDs
exponentially. One recent direction uses operator-potential heuristics
\citep{fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}, which integrate
linear-programming-based potential heuristics
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015} into
\ghseta\ and report little increased BDD size in practice.
This evidence motivates a structural explanation, but does not itself give
a condition under which a heuristic \emph{provably} cannot cause a large
representational overhead. Recently,
\citet{speck-helmert-ecai2025} gave worst-case performance
guarantees for symbolic search relative to explicit search, but their
analysis concerns the representation of transition relations and goal
formulas and explicitly leaves heuristics aside.

We apply standard fixed-order residual/multi-terminal-BDD (MTBDD) width
\citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} to
heuristic total ADDs, using \emph{cofactor width} as a planning-facing name. It
is the largest distinct-residual count after any prefix of the search order,
equivalently the largest cut through the quasi-reduced total ADD. The
underlying product-graph argument for binary synthesis is standard
\citep[Thm.~3.3.6]{wegener-2000}. Our contribution is to
couple it to exact symbolic-search bucket semantics across cost layers, derive
cumulative effort bounds and separations, and instantiate them for
planning-heuristic families.

Our contributions concern product-at-evaluation forward search; they do not
analyze heuristic-delta transition partitions or cumulative bidirectional
search. First, a fragmentation theorem bounds the
total size of the slices of a state-set BDD $B$ by $TWn(|B|+2)$ for a width-$W$,
$T$-terminal heuristic. Together with an exact bucket identity, it gives two
guarantees: the cut-sensitive search bound
$\values W\sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})$, where $L_g$ is the exact cost layer and
$\cofcount$ sums fixed-order residual counts; and the ratio
$3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$. Under its analyzed order, the known exponential
pathology has exponential width; a new family realizes $\Omega(W^2)$ overhead
but leaves joint $\values Wn$ tightness open. At the per-bucket level, neither
the width nor total-ADD Apply bound dominates before cofactor count is relaxed.
An image-aware model counts
idealized high-level cost-image invocations, while a pruning-only design removes
the factor \values\ by retaining semantic blind layers and using the heuristic
only for sound discards.

Second, we give width-nonincreasing safe terminal transforms, including nested
uniform caps, and bound integer potentials, bounded-state pattern databases
(PDBs), and linear merge-and-shrink aligned with the search order; the latter
has width at most $d(N+1)$, where $d$ is the maximum variable-domain size,
when every cascade output, including the final abstraction, has at most $N$
states. The potential result certifies level sets, not delta-partitioned
operator-potential search. Third, using SymK
\citep{speck-et-al-jair2025}, we implement a PDB selector that measures exact
cofactor width and evaluate it diagnostically alongside an uncertified same-$g$
batching probe and a construction-bounded merge-and-shrink safeguard. A frozen screen
(P4) evaluates the selector and batching; a disjoint held-out stage (P5) and
full census (P6) carry the selector-family winner and add the safeguard
alongside blind, conventional-PDB, and merge-and-shrink controls. The selected
$K=1$ candidate is the blind endpoint in both later stages, so the evaluation
does not test whether a nontrivial width-bounded PDB improves search. Archived
outcomes shape only the screen composition; accepted performance observations
and comparisons remain within stage.

% ============================ background =================================
\section{Background}

We use finite deterministic SAS$^{+}$ tasks
$\task=\langle\vars,\ops,\sinit,\sgoal\rangle$
\citep{backstrom-nebel-compint1995}. Operators have positive integer costs.
The functions $\gstar(s)$ and $\hstar(s)$ are respectively the cheapest
cost from \sinit\ to $s$ and from $s$ to a goal. We set $\gstar(s)=\infty$
when $s$ is unreachable and $\hstar(s)=\infty$ when $s$ is a dead end. We
write $\copt=\hstar(\sinit)$ and assume
$0<\copt<\infty$. A heuristic
$h\colon\states\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible}
if $h\leq\hstar$, and \emph{consistent} if
$h(s)\leq\cost(o)+h(s[o])$ for every applicable $o$ and $h=0$ on goals.
The blind heuristic is $\hblind\equiv0$.

Fix a Boolean state encoding in which every SAS$^{+}$ variable $v$ uses
$\lceil\log_2|D_v|\rceil$ Boolean bits. Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be those
bits in order $\pi$, and fix for every heuristic a total extension to Boolean
strings that encode no state; cofactor width depends on this representation
choice. The order is \emph{variable-contiguous} if
all bits of each SAS$^{+}$ variable are consecutive. A state set $S$ is represented
by the reduced ordered BDD (OBDD) of its characteristic function $\chi_S$;
$\bddsize{S}$ counts its inner nodes \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986}. For a prefix
$\rho\in\{0,1\}^i$, let $f|_\rho$ be the residual function and
$\sub{i}{f}=\{f|_\rho\mid\rho\in\{0,1\}^i\}$ its distinct cofactors.

\begin{definition}[cofactor count]
\label{def-cofcount}
For a function $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the \emph{cofactor count} is
$\cofcount(f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} |\sub{i}{f}|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem-qsize}
For every Boolean $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$,
$\bddsize{f} \leq \cofcount(f) \leq n \cdot (\bddsize{f} + 2)$.
\end{lemma}

$\cofcount(f)$ is the inner-node count of the quasi-reduced OBDD. At every
cut, reduced nodes inject into cofactors, while every cofactor is a constant
or is rooted at a unique reduced node; this proves the lemma. Its factor
$n$ is unavoidable already for $f=x_1$, where
$\cofcount(f)=2n-1$ but $\bddsize{f}=1$.

Symbolic uniform-cost search represents each exact layer
$L_g=\{s\mid\gstar(s)=g\}$ by one BDD
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}. Product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} intersects layers with the level sets
$\level{h}{v}=\{s\mid h(s)=v\}$. It removes closed states, discards
$h=\infty$, represents each $(g,v)$ bucket by one BDD, selects by increasing
$(f,g)=(g+v,g)$, and tests for a goal when a bucket is selected
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. Each open bucket persists until that exact key
is selected. Immediately before expansion, the selected bucket is intersected
with the complement of states closed at an equal or lower $g$; an empty
residual key is skipped, and a nonempty residual is moved entirely to closed
before successors are generated. These queue, stale-duplicate, goal-test, and
positive-cost assumptions define the scope of our cumulative guarantees.

\begin{definition}[expansion size, \citealp{speck-et-al-icaps2020}]
\label{def-effort}
If $B_1,\ldots,B_q$ are the nonempty BDDs actually passed to expansion before
optimal termination, the \emph{expansion size} is
$\effort(\task,h)=\sum_{j=1}^q|B_j|$. Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} later proves
that, under the stated contract, these events correspond exactly to the
nonempty $(g,v)$ slices with $g+v\leq\copt$ and $g<\copt$.
\end{definition}

For $\hblind$, \bddastar\ is uniform-cost search and
$\effort(\task,\hblind) = \sum_{g < \copt} \bddsize{L_g}$.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} prove that there are tasks on which even
$\hstar$ has exponentially larger effort
than blind search: a subset can have a much larger BDD than its superset,
so both slicing and pruning can inflate representations. Expansion size is a
representation proxy, not a bound on runtime, setup cost, memory, or primitive
relational products. We seek a
heuristic property that controls every value-selected subset.

% ===================== width-bounded heuristics ==========================
\section{Bounded Cofactor Width}

\begin{definition}[cofactor width]
\label{def-width}
The \emph{cofactor width} of a heuristic $h$ under variable order $\pi$
is $\width_\pi(h) = \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} |\sub{i}{h}|$. We call $h$
\emph{$(\pi,W)$-width-bounded} if $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and write
\emph{width} for cofactor width throughout.
\end{definition}

The cofactors are precisely the states of a deterministic layered automaton
that reads $\pi$; hence width is also the largest level of the heuristic's
quasi-reduced multi-terminal BDD \citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}. It is a
standard fixed-order residual/MTBDD representation property applied here to
heuristics, not a newly proposed width measure and not planning novelty width
\citep{lipovetzky-geffner-ecai2012}.

\begin{proposition}[value range]
\label{prop-range}
If $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, then $|\hrange(h)| \leq W$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, the final cofactors are the attained constants. Write
$\values=|h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}|$ for finite values on planning
states and $T=|\hrange(h)|$ for terminals of the total extension; then
$\values\leq T\leq W$.

\begin{proposition}[closure]
\label{prop-closure}
Let $h_1, h_2$ have widths $W_1, W_2$ under $\pi$ and let
$\oplus \in \{+, \max, \min\}$. Then
$\width_\pi(h_1 \oplus h_2) \leq W_1 \cdot W_2$.
\end{proposition}

At each cut, a combined cofactor is determined by a pair of operand
cofactors. Thus maxima of admissible consistent components, and sums under an
admissible consistent cost allocation, retain multiplicative component-width
bounds.

Call a possibly signed function
$q\colon\states\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$ a \emph{raw admissible and
consistent estimate} if $q(s)\leq \hstar(s)$ for every state,
$q(s)\leq c+q(s')$ for every transition $s\xrightarrow{c}s'$, and
$q(s)\leq0$ on goals.

\begin{proposition}[safe terminal transforms]
\label{prop-transform}
Let $q$ be a raw admissible and consistent estimate, equipped with a
total extension
$q \colon \{0,1\}^{n} \to \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}$. Let
$\phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}
 \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \cup \{\infty\}$
be nondecreasing, satisfy $\phi(0)=0$, $\phi(\infty)=\infty$, and for
every $a\in\mathbb Z$ and integer $c\geq0$ satisfy
\[
\phi(a+c) \leq \phi(a)+c.
\]
Then $h=\phi\circ q$ is admissible and consistent and
$\width_\pi(h)\leq\width_\pi(q)$.
\end{proposition}

Monotonicity and the cost condition transfer consistency and imply
$\phi(a)\leq\max(0,a)$; terminal mapping cannot create cofactors. Thus both
rectification and the cap
$\phi_\kappa(a)=\min(\max(0,a),\kappa)$ for finite $a$ (and
$\phi_\kappa(\infty)=\infty$) are safe and width nonincreasing. The cap has
at most $\kappa+1$ finite values and is pointwise strongest among such safe
transforms with that output ceiling. If $\kappa_1\leq\kappa_2$, then
$\phi_{\kappa_1}\circ q$ is a terminal relabeling of
$\phi_{\kappa_2}\circ q$, so its cofactor width is no larger. Caps therefore
form a nested safety--informativeness ladder: increasing $\kappa$ weakly
strengthens the heuristic, while decreasing it weakly decreases both the
finite-value count and width, hence the coarse $3\values Wn$ certificate.

Let $\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)$ be the reduced ordered ADD under $\pi$
\citep{bahar-et-al-iccad1993}.

\begin{proposition}[ADD characterization]
\label{prop-add}
$\width_\pi(h) \leq |\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| + |\hrange(h)|$ and
$|\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| \leq n \cdot \width_\pi(h)$.
\end{proposition}

Every cofactor is a terminal or a residual ADD node, and every ADD node at
a level is a distinct cofactor. If $A$ is the number of inner nodes, the
rooted binary DAG reaches at most $A+1$ terminals, so
$\width_\pi(h)\leq A+T\leq2A+1$. Hence small width and a small ADD are
polynomially equivalent only in the order actually used by the search
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.

\begin{remark}[total extensions]
\label{rem-encoding}
Definitions over $\{0,1\}^n$ require fixing $h$ on Boolean assignments
that encode no state. Upper bounds apply to the chosen extension; the
families below use natural arithmetic or table extensions. Lower bounds
witnessed on genuine states apply to every extension.
\end{remark}

% ======================= fragmentation theorems ==========================
\section{Bounded Fragmentation}
\label{sec-fragmentation}

Fix $\pi$ and write $W=\width_\pi(h)$. At every cut, the cofactor of a
value-selected subset is determined by the pair consisting of the state-set
cofactor and the heuristic cofactor.

\begin{theorem}[bucket bound]
\label{thm-bucket}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and $U \subseteq \hrange(h)$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bddsize{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}
  &\leq \cofcount(\chi_{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}) \\
  &\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq Wn(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At cut $i$, equal pairs $(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)$ induce equal slice
cofactors, so their number is at most
$|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|\,|\sub{i}{h}|\leq
W|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|$. Sum over cuts and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize}. The supplement gives the explicit injection.
\end{proof}

This covers a bucket ($U=\{v\}$), recognized-dead-end removal, and
threshold pruning. No value-selected subset can inflate the reduced BDD by
more than $Wn$ up to the additive term.

\begin{theorem}[partition bound]
\label{thm-partition}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{v \in \hrange(h)} \bddsize{S \cap \level{h}{v}}
  &\leq TW\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq TWn(\bddsize{S}+2) \\
  &\leq W^2n(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
If $S\subseteq\states$ and the sum ranges only over finite values attained
on planning states, the first two bounds hold with $T$ replaced by $\values$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to each of $T$ buckets (or the $\values$
finite planning-state buckets), then use $T\leq W$.
\end{proof}

The effort measure sums each reduced bucket separately even if a shared
unique table reuses nodes. The factor $n$ cannot be removed: the supplement
gives a width-two parity slice whose reduced BDD is $\Theta(n)$ times its
one-node source set.

Writing $A$ for the total heuristic ADD's inner-node count and
$b=\bddsize{S}$, relabeling its terminals by membership in $U$
\citep[Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} and applying standard Boolean Apply
\citep[Sec.~4.3]{bryant-ieeecomp1986} gives
$\bddsize{S\cap h^{-1}(U)}\leq bA+b+A$. Thus the two certificates
combine as $\min\{W\cofcount(\chi_S),bA+b+A\}$. With the bucket identity
below, the ADD certificate yields the blind-effort ratio bound
$\values(2A+1)$. Since every ADD
level has at most $W$ nonconstant residuals, $A\leq nW$; the coarse
$3\values Wn$ ratio is therefore not a tighter replacement for ADD size.
The width bound can instead be sharper before $\cofcount$ is relaxed; the
supplement gives separations in both directions. We use width for this
cut-sensitive structure and its compositional family bounds.

% ======================= search-effort guarantees ========================
\section{Forward Product-at-Evaluation Search Guarantees}
\label{sec-effort}

\begin{lemma}[bucket identity]
\label{lem-identity}
Let $h$ be consistent. \bddastar\ on \task\ with $h$ selects every
bucket key $(g,v)$ with $g + v \leq \copt$ and $g < \copt$ and
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v} \neq \emptyset$ exactly once before terminating,
and the BDD expanded with key $(g,v)$ represents exactly
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v}$. No other bucket is expanded before
termination.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Selection is lexicographic in $(f,g)$ by definition; consistency makes
generated successor $f$-keys nondecreasing, while strictly positive costs and
the $g$ tie-break prevent a selected key from being repopulated. The
equal-or-lower-$g$ closed-set intersection removes stale duplicates. Induction along cheapest
paths gives optimal $g$-values and complete slices. An open prefix of an
optimal plan prevents termination or selection above $\copt$. The
supplement proves the five claims---monotone selection, no re-population,
optimal $g$-values, completeness, and no other expansions---in full.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[effort bound]
\label{thm-effort}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\effort(\task,h)
  &\leq \values W
    \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
      \cofcount(\chi_{L_g}) \\
  &\leq \values Wn
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr) \\
  &\leq W^2n
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} and Theorem~\ref{thm-partition} bound all selected
slices of each nonempty $L_g$ by
$\values W\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})$, and Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} bounds this by
$\values Wn(\bddsize{L_g}+2)$. If $N$ is the number of nonempty layers
below $\copt$, summing gives
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N)$. Positive integer costs imply
$N\leq\copt$, and $\values\leq W$ gives the coarse bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-effort}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort},
$\effort(\task, h) \leq 3\,\values W n \cdot
\effort(\task, \hblind) \leq
3\, W^{2} n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

Every nonempty pre-goal layer is a nonempty proper set, so its characteristic
function is nonconstant and has at least one BDD node. Hence
$N\leq\effort(\task,\hblind)$.

The guarantee is one-sided: pruning savings remain unbounded. It concerns
frontier sizes in forward product-at-evaluation \bddastar, not primitive
relational products in SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} or \ghseta, and not cumulative
bidirectional effort. Variants visiting the same semantic buckets inherit
the state-set bound.

\subsection{A Quadratic Ratio in $W$ Is Realizable}

\begin{theorem}[quadratic width-ratio family]
\label{thm-tight}
For all integers $W \geq 2$ and $k \geq 4W$ there are tasks
$\task_{k,W}$ with $n=2k+2$ Boolean variables and consistent, admissible
heuristics $h$ with $\width_\pi(h)=W$ such that
$\effort(\task_{k,W},h)\geq
(W^2/128)\effort(\task_{k,W},\hblind)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Use a $k$-step binary-choice chain followed by a cost-$W$ goal action and
order the chain bits before the choice bits. The non-goal heuristic is the
negated choice residue modulo $W$; action costs make it admissible and
consistent, and its $W$ residuals give exact width $W$. Blind effort is
$\Theta(k^2)$. On $\Theta(k)$ late layers, all $W$ buckets are selected
and each has $\Omega(kW)$ nodes, yielding $\Omega(k^2W^2)$ effort. The
supplement gives the construction and cofactor witnesses.
\end{proof}

This realizes $\Omega(W^2)$ but does not make the joint $\values Wn$
upper bound tight: the construction has $\values=W$ and $n=\Omega(W)$, so
it leaves open, for example, an $O(Wn)$ bound. It is not a typical-case
prediction.

\subsection{Exponential Width in the Known Pathology}

In the family of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, indexed here by $r$, chain
variables precede two blocks $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ and
$v_{r+1},\ldots,v_{2r}$ in an order $\pi_r$ over $4r+2$ state bits.
At the critical layer, a state is not a dead end exactly when
$\bigvee_i(v_i\wedge v_{r+i})$ holds, producing their exponential bucket.

\begin{theorem}[width lower bound]
\label{thm-lb}
For the tasks $\task_r$ and orders $\pi_r$ of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020},
$\width_{\pi_r}(\hstar) \geq 2^{r}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the cut after the first $v$-block, encode each
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}$ as a
prefix. If $i\in A\setminus A'$, the valid suffix setting only
$v_{r+i}$ distinguishes the prefixes: one state has goal distance one and
the other is a dead end. Thus one cut has $2^r$ distinct \hstar-cofactors,
for every total extension.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm-effort} and~\ref{thm-lb} together delimit the
known pathology: its perfect heuristic has exponential width and therefore
evades the bounded-width regime. Our result is a parameterized upper bound,
not a converse: bounded width is sufficient, not necessary, for small buckets.
Moreover \hstar\
uses only $O(n)$ values here, so value count alone does not control the
prefix information entangled by its level sets.

\subsection{A Refined Cost Model: Image Calls}
\label{sec-cost}

\begin{definition}[image-aware effort]
\label{def-image-effort}
For $\alpha \geq 0$, the \emph{$\alpha$-effort} of a symbolic search run
is $\effort_\alpha=\sum_B(\alpha+|B|)$ over idealized high-level cost-image
invocations and their argument BDDs.
\end{definition}

At $\alpha=0$, this equals Definition~\ref{def-effort} only in the idealized
case where each expanded bucket is exactly the argument of one invocation.

\begin{proposition}[image-call counts]
\label{prop-calls}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h)\leq W$, and let $N_\ell$ be the
number of nonempty semantic blind layers $L_g$ with
$g<\copt$.  An idealized blind implementation that stores each semantic
$L_g$ in one BDD performs $N_\ell$ counted high-level cost-image invocations,
one per nonempty semantic layer.
An idealized implementation of \bddastar\ that invokes its high-level
cost-image routine once
for every expanded nonempty $(g,v)$ bucket performs
$\sum_g\values_g$ such invocations, where $\values_g$ is the number of
nonempty expanded buckets in layer $g$ and
$\values_g\leq\values\leq W$. Hence it performs at most
$W N_\ell$ counted invocations, and at most $\values N_\ell$ when the finite
valid-state value count is known.
\end{proposition}

Each active layer has at most $\values\leq W$ nonempty buckets, proving the
claim. The construction of Theorem~\ref{thm-tight} realizes
$\Theta(W)$ such invocations per late semantic layer. This idealized count is
of high-level cost-image invocations, not the delta-partition relational
products of \ghseta; the supplement gives the full operational qualification.
Writing $E_0=\effort(\task,\hblind)$, the node and invocation bounds combine
for every $\alpha\geq0$ as
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \effort_{\alpha,h}
  &\leq \values Wn(E_0+2N_\ell) \\
  &\quad+\alpha\values N_\ell \\
  &\leq3\values Wn(E_0+\alpha N_\ell) \\
  &=3\values Wn\,\effort_{\alpha,\hblind}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\subsection{Pruning-Only Search}
\label{sec-prune}

Pruning-only search keeps one BDD per uniform-cost layer and uses an
incumbent solution cost $c$ only to discard $h=\infty$ and
$g+h\geq c$, reapplying the current bound at expansion and stopping when
the next layer cannot improve $c$. Admissibility
makes the discards sound. If $c_g$ is the incumbent when layer $g$ is
expanded, consistency along cheapest paths gives the exact slice
$L_g\cap h^{-1}(\{v\mid v<c_g-g\})$.

\begin{corollary}[pruning bound]
\label{cor-prune}
Let $h$ be admissible and consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. The
cumulative size of the layers expanded by pruning-only search is at most
$W n \cdot (\effort(\task, \hblind) + 2N_\ell)
\leq W n \cdot (\effort(\task, \hblind) + 2\copt)$, removing the factor
$\values$ due to value partitioning from the refined \bddastar\ bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, with one idealized high-level cost-image invocation
per expanded semantic layer as in blind search. With $c = \infty$ throughout
(no solution found yet) and no dead ends, its semantic layers and expansions
coincide with blind forward search; heuristic construction and evaluation can
still consume time.
\end{corollary}

Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} proves the corollary for one threshold slice per
layer. Its $\alpha$-effort is at most
$Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N_\ell)+\alpha N_\ell$, hence at most $3Wn$
times blind $\alpha$-effort. Pruning is pointwise sound in either direction of
bidirectional search when forward search uses a goal-distance estimate and
backward search uses a distance-from-initial estimate, but we claim no
cumulative bidirectional bound:
closed sets, meeting points, incumbents, and the direction schedule feed
back on the frontiers. Details appear in the supplement.


% ============================ instances ==================================
\section{Width-Bounded Heuristic Families}
\label{sec-instances}

Let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous,
$d=\max_{v\in\vars}|D_v|$, and $m=|\vars|$.

\subsection{Potential Heuristics}

Integer potentials \citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022}
form $p(s)=\sum_vP(v,s(v))$ and expose the safe rectification
$\hpot=\max(0,p)$. Their standard constraints make $p$ a raw admissible
and consistent estimate, so Proposition~\ref{prop-transform} makes
$\hpot$ admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pot}
Let $p$ have integer potentials with $|P(v,w)|\leq M$ for all facts,
extended to non-state assignments by the same sum over the bit
encoding's induced table, with table values on invalid bit codes also
in $[-M,M]$, e.g., $0$, and let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\width_\pi(\hpot)&\leq\width_\pi(p)\leq d(2mM+1),\\
|\hrange(\hpot)|&\leq mM+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-pot}
For rectified consistent integer potential heuristics with magnitudes at
most $M$,
\[
\effort(\task,\hpot)
 \leq 3d(mM+1)(2mM+1)n\cdot\effort(\task,\hblind),
\]
a factor polynomial in the task size and the numeric magnitude $M$.
\end{corollary}

The bound counts the accumulated sum and the at most $d$ partial codes of
the current variable; Corollary~\ref{cor-pot} substitutes it into
Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}. It is pseudo-polynomial in numeric $M$, not
polynomial in its bit length, and does not analyze delta-partitioned
\ghseta.

\subsection{Pattern Databases}

A pattern database \citep[PDB;][]{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,%
edelkamp-ecp2001} projects \task\ onto a pattern
$P\subseteq\vars$ and stores the abstract goal distance $h^P(s)$ of the
abstract state induced by $s$; this distance is admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pdb}
Let $P\subseteq\vars$ be any pattern under a fixed Boolean order
$\pi$. Extend its table to invalid encodings by ignoring non-pattern
bits and mapping an invalid pattern-variable code to a fixed sink
terminal. Let
$|\states^P|=\prod_{v\in P}|D_v|$. Then
\[
\width_\pi(h^P)\leq |\states^P|+1.
\]
\end{proposition}

At any cut, each distinct non-sink cofactor is induced by a relevant
pattern-bit prefix with a valid completion. Such prefixes inject into distinct
abstract states by choosing one completion each; all invalid prefixes share
the fixed sink. Non-pattern bits may be interleaved and are ignored. Hence an
abstract-state budget $N$ gives width at most $N+1$ and at most $N$ finite
values, for an effort ratio of at most $3N(N+1)n$. The sink may coincide with
a PDB value, so the bound is an upper bound. Max combinations retain the
product-width bound; additive combinations require an admissible consistent
cost allocation.

\subsection{Linear Merge-and-Shrink}

Merge-and-shrink (M\&S) abstractions
\citep{helmert-et-al-jacm2014} iteratively merge factors of a factored
transition system and shrink intermediate abstractions to at most $N$
abstract states. With a \emph{linear} merge strategy, the abstraction
mapping is representable by cascading tables, equivalently a factored
mapping \citep{helmert-et-al-icaps2015}: a chain of tables
$\tau_1, \dots, \tau_m$ where $\tau_j$ maps the pair of the previous
intermediate abstract state and the value of variable $v_j$ to the next
intermediate abstract state. Its final abstract goal distance is admissible
and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-ms}
Let $h^{\mathrm{MS}}$ be an M\&S heuristic built with a linear merge
strategy whose variable order agrees with the variable-contiguous
$\pi$, and suppose the valid output set after every cascade table, including
the final abstraction, has at most $N$ states. Extend each
table with a distinguished sink for invalid variable codes. Then
$\width_\pi(h^{\mathrm{MS}}) \leq d \cdot (N+1)$.
\end{proposition}

Here the premise bounds every table output, not merely selected post-shrink
products. The table cascade remembers at most $N$ intermediate states (plus the
invalid-code sink) and one bit buffer. The final abstraction has at most
$N$ finite distances, giving effort ratio at most $3dN(N+1)n$.

The alignment premise is essential. Our causal-graph control explicitly
uses \texttt{align\_merge\_order=false}; it is \emph{not} covered by the
a priori formula, though its exact post-construction width in the search
order can instantiate the general theorems. Alignment supplies the formula
but may change heuristic quality. We establish no analogous small-width
guarantee here for $h^{\max}$, $h^{\mathrm{add}}$, or LM-cut, and
Theorem~\ref{thm-lb} shows that perfect information can require exponential
width. Full family proofs appear in the supplement.

% ============================ synthesis ==================================
\section{Width-Constrained Heuristic Selection}
\label{sec-synthesis}

The theorem controls fragmentation, not successor computation or construction
cost. For a fixed $B=100{,}000$ abstract-state budget, our diagnostic selector
deduplicates candidates from five sources: the empty pattern, the longest BDD-order
prefix, the longest goal/causal prefix, a goal-fill variant that skips
nonfitting variables, and seeded CEGAR. It materializes every unique candidate
within $B$ as a PDB and total ADD \emph{before} filtering exact width by $K$;
thus $K$ does not cap setup. The lexicographic score prefers initial-state
dead-end recognition, larger initial value, larger mean finite goal distance,
larger abstract dead-end fraction, then smaller width and state count. Width
one forces the attained value zero, deliberately including the blind endpoint.
Measurement covers invalid codes and skipped manager levels. The fixed $B$
and 60-second safeguard below are protocol design points, not optimized or
theoretically justified budgets. The matrix has no total-ADD-size budget
ablation, so it cannot establish which quantity is the better operational
constraint.

An uncertified same-$g$ batching probe images a union of selected and eligible
fresh buckets once while leaving extra sources pending at their original keys.
Its oracle compares keys, BDD-node and state counts, and solution cost, but not
set equality; the probe therefore supports no semantic or performance claim.
A separate 60-second M\&S construction deadline falls back to blind search and
charges setup time. The evaluated causal-graph M\&S is unaligned, so the
$d(N+1)$ family formula does not apply; its measured width can still
instantiate the general theorem on solvable cells. Full algorithms and
contracts are in the supplement.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec-experiments}

We ask whether the frozen width filter retains an informative PDB and whether
two safeguards mitigate costs outside the theorem. Potential heuristics,
aligned M\&S, and pruning-only search are absent. A secondary structural
diagnostic was frozen during P6 after a few individual records had been shown
but before any comparative analysis. It contrasts $W$ with total ADD size
$U=A+T$ and logged partition ratios. It was not launch-predeclared, is
descriptive only, and cannot test the theorem because the logs omit cofactor
profiles and paired source/slice sizes. All treatments use forward symbolic
search with a task-specific fixed BDD order in one environment; nonblind
heuristics use product-at-evaluation \bddastar, while blind and fallback runs
use the equivalent $h\equiv0$ path.
One cell is one task--configuration run; one symbolic image is one
successor-set computation.
The 300-second aggregate process-CPU limit includes heuristic construction;
memory is 8\,GiB on one AMD EPYC 9755 core. Micro-PAR2 averages reported CPU
time for solved cells and twice the cutoff (600 seconds) otherwise. No outcome
from an earlier run is reused as a performance observation. Coverage is
planner-reported: costs are reconciled across internal outputs, but plans were
not checked by an external validator.

The screen crosses five controls---blind, uncapped and value-32-capped
10,000-state M\&S, goal-fill PDB, and CEGAR PDB---with fifteen selector
treatments. Ten are unbatched
$K\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256,\infty\}$; four combine selected budgets
with gated $\Delta=64$ batching, and one uses $K=32,\Delta=16$. The source
inventory has 1697 optimal sequential IPC tasks through 2023. Frozen,
disjoint exclusions of 290 zero-cost and 30 normalized-axiom tasks leave 1377
positive-cost, axiom-free tasks. Direct SAS scans or a pinned-parser proof
certify positive costs; a separate post-normalization check certifies the
absence of axioms, and source hashes are frozen. The
\texttt{release\_no\_lp} matrix excludes potential heuristics.

\paragraph{Stage 1 (P4 screen).}
The frozen manifest has 50 tasks, exactly two from each of 25 domains, and 20
configurations (1000 cells). It was assembled during development using
archived outcome summaries to enrich difficult or discriminating cases.
Those measurements affected screen composition only and are not reused as P4
performance observations; consequently P4 is a selection screen,
not confirmatory evidence. Its single lexicographic order maximizes coverage,
then minimizes micro-PAR2, conditionally total image time, and label. It fixes
both the overall and selector-family winners.

\paragraph{Stage 2 (P5 disjoint held-out sample).}
The separately frozen, outcome-independent manifest has 92 tasks disjoint
from the P4 screen, two from each of 46 domains. Within each domain, a
fixed-seed SHA-256 ordering selects the first two eligible names without any
planner outcome. P5 adds the
unbatched Stage-1 selector-family winner without retuning. It evaluates the
five controls and adds value-32-capped M\&S with a 60-second build limit and
blind fallback; the overall screen winner is already one of those controls.
Together with the selector, this yields seven unique configurations and 644
cells. The primary selector-minus-CEGAR estimand averages paired
coverage equally over domains; a deterministic 100,000-replicate domain
bootstrap gives a 95\% domain-resampling stability interval. Other comparisons
are descriptive. The interval resamples the 46 fixed domains; it captures neither task
selection nor run-to-run uncertainty and is not an interval for the census.
Here \emph{held out} means held out from P4 selection; we do not claim that
the P5 tasks were historically unseen over the broader project.

\paragraph{Stage 3 (P6 full census).}
P6 carries the same seven configurations unchanged to all 1377 tasks (9639
cells), including the screen and held-out tasks. It is a descriptive census,
not another sample-based evaluation stage; its sole sensitivity excludes the
50 screen tasks. P5 and P6 never reopen or rerank the selector, matrix,
parameters, contrasts, or statistical policy. P4, P5, and P6 have separately
frozen source and launch identities throughout. Missing, duplicate, unexpected, or
unattested cells invalidate a stage instead of changing a denominator. The
supplement gives the complete execution, acceptance, measurement, provenance,
and recovery protocol, including the prospective corrections that produced
the accepted P4, P5, and P6 records.
Coverage and PAR2 retain the fixed task denominator for protocol-recognized
unsolvable outcomes. The formal effort guarantees apply only to solvable tasks
under the standing assumption $0<\copt<\infty$.

\section{Related Work}

\bddastar\ partitions frontiers by heuristic value
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998}; SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\ instead
partition transitions \citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008}, while ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
uses algebraic diagrams \citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002}. Even \hstar\ can
increase BDD-node effort exponentially \citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. We give
a checkable sufficient condition and show that their construction has
exponential width under its analyzed order. Fixed-order residual width and the
binary product construction are standard; our contribution couples them to
exact bucket semantics, cumulative planning-search effort, separations, and
heuristic-family bounds. The result is a representation-safety certificate for
one architecture, not a general runtime theorem.

PDBs and their symbolic use \citep{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,
edelkamp-ecp2001}, CEGAR generation \citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019}, symbolic
compilation \citep{edelkamp-et-al-ecai2012}, factored M\&S mappings
\citep{torralba-phd2015,helmert-et-al-icaps2015}, and potential heuristics
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}
all predate this work. Our selector's new step is exact total-ADD width
measurement and rejection within a fixed, provenance-preserving deduplicated
pool; the candidate generators and score are not claimed as new. The M\&S
bound requires aligned merge and search orders; the evaluated control is
deliberately unaligned.

Our batching probe differs from whole-$g$ rows, best-set-first grouping, and bounded
transition trees \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,torralba-et-al-icaps2013}:
sources share one speculative image but remain pending at their original keys.
A regression oracle compares expansion keys, BDD-node and represented-state
counts, and solution cost. Because it does not test set equality, batching is
exploratory rather than a certified search transformation.
Related work on symbolic versus explicit blind search
\citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025} and symbolic perimeter abstractions
\citep{torralba-et-al-ijcai2016a,torralba-et-al-aij2018} addresses
complementary representation choices. Variable ordering
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014} is directly coupled to our certificate
because it determines $W$.

% ============================ conclusions ================================
\section{Conclusions}

For solvable positive-cost tasks in product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\
under a fixed variable order, cofactor width gives a representation-safety
certificate. A consistent
width-$W$, $\values$-valued heuristic has effort at most
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt)$ and blind-effort ratio at most
$3\values Wn\leq3W^2n$. The known exponential pathology has exponential
width under its analyzed order; an $\Omega(W^2)$ family leaves joint
$\values Wn$ tightness open.
Before cofactor-count relaxation, the per-bucket width and total-ADD Apply
bounds are incomparable; the latter gives ratio $\values(2A+1)$ for ADD size $A$.
Integer potentials, bounded-state PDBs, and aligned linear M\&S are
width-bounded. The certificate bounds representational harm, not heuristic
quality, construction cost, or runtime.

The matrix filters a fixed PDB pool by exact width but omits potential
heuristics, aligned M\&S, and pruning-only search. The secondary P6 diagnostic
found substantial but family-dependent gaps between $W$ and total ADD size,
with mixed cross-task correlations to logged partition ratios. The records
lack cofactor profiles and paired theorem-source/slice sizes, so this does not
validate the bound. Batching and the unaligned M\&S build deadline remain
separate engineering diagnostics.

Across P4--P6, the frozen selector-family winner's heuristic remains
blind-equivalent:
all 1355 certified P6 traces select the empty PDB, it solves 612/1377 tasks
against blind's 615, and capped M\&S leads with 633. Batching lowers screen
coverage, and the P5 selector--CEGAR interval includes zero. The build-bounded
M\&S safeguard is competitive but descriptive. Thus the evaluated width
filter does not establish practical usefulness on the frozen supported
population. Because $K=1$ admits only the blind endpoint, no ranking score can
rescue that frozen choice. Future selectors must jointly budget certified
width and candidate quality, compare informative candidates, and evaluate the
already available terminal caps without collapsing to $h\equiv0$.

Open problems include joint $\values Wn$ tightness and coupling heuristic
quality to certified safety.
