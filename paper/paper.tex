% ============================== abstract =================================
\begin{abstract}
Heuristics guide explicit \astar\ search well but rarely pay off in symbolic search, which represents sets of states as binary decision diagrams (BDDs).
We analyze why for forward symbolic \astar, which splits every layer of the search into buckets of states with the same heuristic value.
We factor the number of BDD nodes it expands into two effects of the heuristic: pruning, which removes states and thus shrinks the BDDs of the layers, and fragmentation, the cost of representing each layer by one BDD per bucket.
On \num{\HoldoutTasks} planning tasks, pattern database, merge-and-shrink and potential heuristics that more than double the expanded BDD nodes barely shrink the layers and fragment them instead, since BDDs shrink much less than the state sets they represent.
A search that knew the optimal plan cost could prune without fragmenting and would beat blind search far more often, but finding the bound by iterative deepening costs more than the fragmentation it avoids, and the plan costs of a fast satisficing planner are too loose to prune much.
We also prove that cofactor width bounds both effects: fragmentation is at most $2VWn$ for a consistent heuristic with $V$ finite values and width $W$ on $n$ state bits, and a family of planning tasks attains $VWn/120$.
Hence, only heuristics of exponential width, such as the perfect heuristic on known pathological tasks, can increase the effort exponentially, but the bound does not predict fragmentation in practice.
\end{abstract}

% ============================ introduction ===============================
\section{Introduction}

Cost-optimal classical planners commonly use \astar\ \citep{hart-et-al-ieeessc1968} with an admissible heuristic that estimates the goal distance of individual states \citep{pearl-1984}.
Symbolic search instead expands sets of states, which it represents as binary decision diagrams \citep[BDDs;][]{bryant-ieeecomp1986,mcmillan-1993,edelkamp-helmert-ecp1999}.
Blind symbolic search is among the strongest approaches for cost-optimal planning \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,speck-ipc2023,speck-et-al-jair2025}.
Heuristics, however, help symbolic search much less than explicit search.
Symbolic variants of \astar\ such as \bddastar\ \citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998}, SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} \citep{jensen-et-al-aaai2002}, \ghseta\ \citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008} and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}} \citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002} help in some domains and hurt in others \citep{torralba-phd2015,torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} even show that the perfect heuristic \hstar\ can increase the size of the expanded BDDs exponentially, so heuristic accuracy alone cannot explain the effect of a heuristic on symbolic search.

We explain this effect by separating two roles that a heuristic plays in symbolic \astar.
As in explicit search, the heuristic prunes states whose $f$-value exceeds the optimal plan cost.
In addition, it partitions the remaining states of every layer into buckets with the same heuristic value, and symbolic search represents each bucket by its own BDD.
Since the BDD of a subset can be much larger than the BDD of its superset, the buckets of a layer can together need many more nodes than the layer.
We therefore factor the number of expanded BDD nodes into the BDD size of the expanded layers without the partition, which pruning reduces, and a \emph{fragmentation factor}, which measures the cost of the partition.

With this factorization, we analyze pattern database (PDB), merge-and-shrink (M\&S) and potential heuristics in the symbolic planner SymK \citep{speck-et-al-jair2025} on \num{\HoldoutTasks} IPC tasks, with the protocol and the primary analyses of every study fixed before the experiments.
Whenever one of these heuristics expands more than twice as many BDD nodes as blind search, it barely prunes and fragments the layers instead.
Even a heuristic that removes half of the states can leave the BDDs almost as large as before, since BDDs shrink much less than the state sets they represent.
The same diagnosis holds for potentials optimized for all states, a stronger heuristic: they help where they remove most states and fragment the layers elsewhere.

Without fragmentation, heuristics would pay off much more often: a search that knew the optimal plan cost and pruned by $f$-value without splitting the layers would expand fewer BDD nodes than blind search on \OracleAllUnsplitBelow\ of \num{\GenAllPairs} pairs of heuristic and task, compared with \OracleAllEffortBelow\ for symbolic \astar.
Forward search knows no such bound, however.
Using heuristics only to prune dead ends removes the fragmentation but almost all pruning with it, and finding the bound by iterative deepening costs more than the fragmentation it avoids.
With the plan cost of the satisficing planner LAMA as bound, heuristics no longer hurt, but this bound is too loose to prune much, and blind search with the same bound does as well.
No forward use of a heuristic solves significantly more tasks than blind forward search, and blind bidirectional search solves the most tasks.

To understand how much a heuristic can fragment the search, we bound fragmentation by the \emph{cofactor width} of the heuristic, the largest number of distinct residual functions of the heuristic at any cut of the BDD variable order.
Cofactor width is the standard width of a multi-terminal BDD, and our bounds build on the standard product construction \citep{wegener-2000}.
We connect both to the buckets of symbolic search: for a consistent heuristic with $V$ finite values and cofactor width $W$ on $n$ state bits, fragmentation and the number of expanded BDD nodes relative to blind search are at most $2VWn$, and a family of polynomial-size planning tasks attains $VWn/120$.
Hence, only heuristics of exponential width can increase the effort exponentially, such as the perfect heuristic in the construction of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, whereas the width of PDBs and of M\&S heuristics with a suitable merge order is at most linear in their abstraction size.
For the PDBs of our first study, a cut-wise refinement of the bound exceeds the observed fragmentation by a median factor of \SlackProfileMedian, but it does not predict how fragmentation changes under other variable orders.
Since pruning also keeps a subset of each layer selected by heuristic value, the same argument shows that removing states need not shrink the BDDs, but the bounds do not predict how much a given heuristic prunes.

% ============================ background =================================
\section{Background}

We consider finite deterministic SAS$^{+}$ tasks $\task=\langle\vars,\ops,\sinit,\sgoal\rangle$ \citep{backstrom-nebel-compint1995} with positive integer operator costs.
The functions $\gstar(s)$ and $\hstar(s)$ give the cheapest costs from \sinit\ to $s$ and from $s$ to a goal, respectively, with value $\infty$ if no such path exists.
We write $\copt=\hstar(\sinit)$ for the optimal plan cost and assume $0<\copt<\infty$.
A heuristic $h\colon\states\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible} if $h\leq\hstar$, and \emph{consistent} if $h(s)\leq\cost(o)+h(s[o])$ for every applicable operator $o$ and $h=0$ on goals.
The blind heuristic is $\hblind\equiv0$.

\paragraph{Binary Decision Diagrams.}
We encode every SAS$^{+}$ variable $v$ with $\lceil\log_2|D_v|\rceil$ Boolean bits and write $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ for all bits in a fixed variable order $\pi$.
The order is \emph{variable-contiguous} if the bits of each SAS$^{+}$ variable are consecutive.
Some bit strings encode no state, so every heuristic receives a total extension to all of $\{0,1\}^n$; our upper bounds apply to the chosen extension, and lower bounds witnessed on bit strings that encode states apply to every extension.
As in the CUDD library that SymK uses, a state set $S$ is represented by the reduced ordered BDD of its characteristic function $\chi_S$ with complemented edges, and $\bddsize{S}$ counts its regular inner nodes \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986}.
For a function $f$ and a prefix $\rho\in\{0,1\}^i$ of the bits, $f|_\rho$ denotes the residual function, or \emph{cofactor}, of $f$, and $\sub{i}{f}=\{f|_\rho\mid\rho\in\{0,1\}^i\}$ is the set of distinct cofactors at cut $i$, where $f$ and $\neg f$ count as distinct.
We write $\cutcount{i}{f}=|\sub{i}{f}|$.

\begin{definition}[cofactor count]
\label{def-cofcount}
For a function $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the \emph{cofactor count} is $\cofcount(f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \cutcount{i}{f}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem-qsize}
For every Boolean $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, $\bddsize{f} \leq \cofcount(f) \leq n \cdot (\bddsize{f} + 2)$.
If $f$ is nonconstant, also $\cofcount(f)\leq2n\bddsize{f}$.
\end{lemma}

The cofactor count is the number of nodes of the quasi-reduced BDD of $f$, counting both polarities (supplement).
The factor $n$ is unavoidable already for $f=x_1$, where $\cofcount(f)=2n-1$ but $\bddsize{f}=1$.

\paragraph{Symbolic Search.}
Symbolic uniform-cost search represents each layer $L_g=\{s\mid\gstar(s)=g\}$ of states with cheapest cost $g$ by one BDD \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
Forward \bddastar\ \citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} additionally intersects the layers with the level sets $\level{h}{v}=\{s\mid h(s)=v\}$ of the heuristic, and we consider the product-at-evaluation variant \citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}.
It discards states with $h=\infty$, represents each bucket of states with $g$-value $g$ and $h$-value $v$ by one BDD and selects buckets by increasing $(f,g)=(g+v,g)$.
Before it expands a bucket, it removes the states that are already closed with their cheapest $g$-value, and below, ``bucket'' means this remaining set.
The search tests for a goal when it selects a bucket and moves the entire bucket to the closed list before generating its successors.
These assumptions about the queue, duplicates, goal tests and positive costs define the scope of our bounds.

\begin{definition}[expansion size, \citealp{speck-et-al-icaps2020}]
\label{def-effort}
Let $B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ denote the BDD that \bddastar\ expands with $g$-value $g$ and $h$-value $v$ on task \task\ with heuristic $h$, or the empty set if no such expansion occurs.
For consistent $h$, the \emph{expansion size}, or \emph{effort}, of \bddastar\ is $\effort(\task,h) = \sum_{\,g + v \leq \copt,\ g < \copt} |B_{\task,h}(g,v)|$.
\end{definition}

Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} below shows that exactly these nonempty buckets are expanded before the search terminates.
With $\hblind$, \bddastar\ is uniform-cost search and $\effort(\task,\hblind) = \sum_{g < \copt} \bddsize{L_g}$.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} show that on some tasks even \hstar\ has exponentially larger effort than blind search.

% ===================== pruning versus fragmentation ======================
\section{Pruning and Fragmentation}

A heuristic changes the effort of symbolic search in two ways.
It prunes states whose $f$-value exceeds \copt, which reduces the expanded part of each layer, and it splits that part into several buckets.
For a completed search, let $A_g(h)$ be the set of $h$-values whose nonempty buckets are expanded with $g$-value $g$, and let $S_g^h=\bigcup_{v\in A_g(h)}B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ be the expanded part of layer $g$.
We define the \emph{unsplit effort} and the \emph{fragmentation factor} as
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \unioneffort(\task,h)&=\textstyle\sum_g\bddsize{S_g^h},\\
 \fragfactor(\task,h)&=\effort(\task,h)/\unioneffort(\task,h),
 \end{aligned}
\]
whenever the denominator is positive.
By definition, $\effort=\unioneffort\cdot\fragfactor$ (Figure~\ref{fig:factorization}).
The unsplit effort measures the expanded states as if each layer were represented by one BDD, and the fragmentation factor measures the cost of splitting them by heuristic value.
Blind search has one bucket per layer, so $\fragfactor(\task,\hblind)=1$.
The fragmentation factor can be smaller than one, since the BDD of a union can be larger than the BDDs of its disjoint parts together.

To compare a heuristic with blind search on the same task, we use the \emph{effort ratio} $r=\effort(\task,h)/\effort(\task,\hblind)$ and the \emph{unsplit ratio} $u=\unioneffort(\task,h)/\effort(\task,\hblind)$.
Since blind search does not fragment, $r=u\cdot\fragfactor$: a heuristic reduces effort only if its pruning ($u<1$) outweighs its fragmentation.
The two effects also act on different layers.
By Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} below, \bddastar\ expands exactly the states of layer $g$ with $h\leq\copt-g$, so a heuristic with largest finite value $H$ prunes nothing in the layers $g\leq\copt-H$, whereas it can fragment every layer.

% ===================== width-bounded heuristics ==========================
\section{Cofactor Width}

To bound fragmentation, we need a property of the heuristic that controls the BDD size of every subset it selects by value.
We use the number of distinct cofactors of the heuristic at each cut.
The supplement contains full proofs of all results in this and the following two sections.

\begin{definition}[cofactor width]
\label{def-width}
The \emph{cofactor width} of a heuristic $h$ under variable order $\pi$ is $\width_\pi(h) = \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} \cutcount{i}{h}$.
We call $h$ \emph{$(\pi,W)$-width-bounded} if $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and we write \emph{width} for cofactor width.
\end{definition}

The cofactors of $h$ are the states of a layered deterministic automaton that reads the bits in order $\pi$, so width is the largest level of the quasi-reduced multi-terminal BDD (MTBDD) of $h$ \citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}.
Width is thus a standard representation property applied to heuristics, and it is unrelated to novelty width in planning \citep{lipovetzky-geffner-ecai2012}.
Width bounds the number of heuristic values, since the cofactors at the last cut are the attained constants.
We write $\values=|h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}|$ for the number of finite values on planning states and $T=|\hrange(h)|$ for the number of values of the total extension, so $\values\leq T\leq W$.
Since a cofactor of a combined heuristic is determined by a pair of cofactors of its operands, the width of a sum, maximum or minimum of two heuristics is at most the product of their widths.
Mapping the values of an admissible and consistent estimate by a nondecreasing function $\phi$ with $\phi(0)=0$ and $\phi(a+c)\leq\phi(a)+c$ for all $c\geq0$, such as rectification $\max(0,q)$ or a cap $\min(\max(0,q),\kappa)$, preserves admissibility and consistency and never increases width.
Width and the size of the reduced ordered algebraic decision diagram (ADD) of the heuristic \citep{bahar-et-al-iccad1993} bound each other up to a factor $n$ and an additive $|\hrange(h)|$, but only under the variable order that the search actually uses \citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.
The supplement proves these properties.

\paragraph{Common Heuristics.}
Under a variable-contiguous order, three common families have bounded width (supplement).
A pattern database \citep[PDB;][]{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,edelkamp-ecp2001} for a pattern $P$ has width at most the number $|\states^P|$ of its abstract states.
A merge-and-shrink (M\&S) heuristic \citep{helmert-et-al-jacm2014} with a linear merge order that agrees with the BDD order has width at most $d(N+1)$, where $d$ is the largest domain size and $N$ bounds the outputs of every table in its cascade \citep{helmert-et-al-icaps2015}.
A rectified potential heuristic \citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015} with integer potentials in $[-M,M]$ on $m$ variables has width at most $d(2mM+1)$.
We give no analogous bound for $h^{\max}$, $h^{\mathrm{add}}$ or LM-cut, but their width can be measured after construction.

% ======================= fragmentation theorems ==========================
\section{Bounding Fragmentation}
\label{sec-fragmentation}

Fix $\pi$ and write $W=\width_\pi(h)$.
The key observation is that, at every cut, the cofactor of a subset selected by heuristic value is determined by the pair of the cofactor of the state set and the cofactor of the heuristic.
For a state set $S$, the \emph{cofactor-profile product} therefore multiplies the two cofactor counts cut by cut:
\[
  \cutprod{S}{h}=\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \cutcount{i}{\chi_S}\cutcount{i}{h}\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S).
\]

\begin{theorem}[bucket bound]
\label{thm-bucket}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and $U \subseteq \hrange(h)$.
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bddsize{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}
  &\leq \cofcount(\chi_{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}) \leq \cutprod{S}{h} \\
  &\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S) \leq Wn(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $F=S\cap h^{-1}(U)$ and let $\rho$ be a prefix of length $i$.
The cofactor $\chi_F|_\rho$ maps a suffix $\sigma$ to $1$ if and only if $\chi_S|_\rho(\sigma)=1$ and $h|_\rho(\sigma)\in U$, so it is determined by the pair $(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)$.
Hence, $\cutcount{i}{\chi_F}\leq\cutcount{i}{\chi_S}\cutcount{i}{h}$, and summing over all cuts gives $\cofcount(\chi_F)\leq\cutprod{S}{h}$.
The remaining inequalities follow from Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} and $\cutcount{i}{h}\leq W$.
\end{proof}

The theorem covers single buckets ($U=\{v\}$) as well as the removal of dead ends and pruning by a threshold.
Up to the additive term, no subset selected by heuristic value has a BDD more than $Wn$ times as large as that of its source set.
Example~\ref{ex-parity} shows that the factor $n$ is needed.

\begin{example}
\label{ex-parity}
Let $S=\{x_1=1\}$ and let $h=x_1\oplus x_2\oplus x_3$ be a parity heuristic with values $0$ and $1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:example}).
The BDD of $S$ has one node, but its bucket $S\cap h^{-1}(0)$, the states with $x_1=1$ and $x_2\oplus x_3=1$, has four nodes without complemented edges.
At the cut after $x_2$, $S$ has two cofactors (true and false) and $h$ has two ($x_3$ and $\neg x_3$), so the bucket has at most four cofactors; it has three: $x_3$, $\neg x_3$ and false.
With $n$ bits, the same bucket has $\Theta(n)$ nodes even with complemented edges.
\end{example}

Summing Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} over all buckets of a set bounds the cost of partitioning it.

\begin{theorem}[partition bound]
\label{thm-partition}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$.
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\textstyle\sum_{v \in \hrange(h)} \bddsize{S \cap \level{h}{v}}
  &\leq T\cutprod{S}{h} \leq TW\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq TWn(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
If $S\subseteq\states$ and the sum ranges only over the finite values attained on planning states, every bound holds with $T$ replaced by $\values$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to each of the $T$ buckets (or the $\values$ finite buckets of planning states), then relax the profile.
\end{proof}

The sum counts each bucket separately, even if a shared unique table reuses nodes across buckets.
Applied to the expanded parts of the layers of a completed search, Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} bounds the fragmentation factor, since every expanded bucket is a single-value subset of $S_g^h$:
\[
 \fragfactor(\task,h)\leq\profilescore(\task,h)=
 \frac{\sum_g|A_g(h)|\,\cutprod{S_g^h}{h}}{\unioneffort(\task,h)}.
\]
We call $\profilescore$ the \emph{profile bound}.
It needs the expanded parts of the layers and can thus only be computed after the search, whereas width relaxes it to a property of the heuristic alone.

The total ADD size of the heuristic gives an alternative bound.
Let $A$ be the number of inner nodes of the ADD of $h$ and $b=\bddsize{S}$.
Relabeling the ADD terminals by membership in $U$ \citep[Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} yields an ordinary BDD with at most $A$ nodes, so the standard Apply algorithm \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986} gives $\bddsize{S\cap h^{-1}(U)}\leq2(A+1)b+A$.
The supplement shows that this \emph{ADD bound} and the cofactor-profile product are incomparable, so we report both.

% ======================= search-effort guarantees ========================
\section{Bounding Search Effort}
\label{sec-effort}

To lift the bounds from single sets to complete searches, we first characterize exactly which buckets \bddastar\ expands.

\begin{lemma}[bucket identity]
\label{lem-identity}
Let $h$ be consistent.
\bddastar\ on \task\ with $h$ selects every bucket key $(g,v)$ with $g + v \leq \copt$, $g < \copt$ and $L_g \cap \level{h}{v} \neq \emptyset$ exactly once before terminating, and the BDD expanded with key $(g,v)$ represents exactly $L_g \cap \level{h}{v}$.
No other bucket is expanded before termination.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Consistency makes the $f$-values of generated successors nondecreasing, and with positive costs and ties broken by increasing $g$, every predecessor that could add states to a key is selected first, so no selected key receives new states.
Induction along cheapest paths gives cheapest $g$-values and complete buckets, and an open prefix of an optimal plan prevents selection above \copt.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[effort bound]
\label{thm-effort}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and let $\values_g=|\{v\in h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}\mid g+v\leq\copt,\ L_g\cap\level{h}{v}\ne\emptyset\}|$.
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\effort(\task,h)
  &\leq \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
    \values_g\cutprod{L_g}{h}
  \leq \values W
    \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
      \cofcount(\chi_{L_g}) \\
  &\leq \values Wn
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Lemma~\ref{lem-identity}, the search expands exactly $\values_g$ buckets of layer $g$, and Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} bounds each by $\cutprod{L_g}{h}$.
Using $\values_g\leq\values$, relaxing the profile and applying Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} give the remaining inequalities, since positive integer costs imply that at most \copt\ layers below \copt\ are nonempty.
\end{proof}

Every nonempty layer below \copt\ contains no goal state, so its characteristic function is nonconstant and Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} gives $\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})\leq2n\bddsize{L_g}$.
Substituting this bound into Theorem~\ref{thm-effort} and using $\values\leq W$ yields a bound relative to blind search.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-effort}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, $\effort(\task, h) \leq 2\,\values W n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind) \leq 2\, W^{2} n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

The guarantee is one-sided: a heuristic can still reduce effort arbitrarily by pruning.
Pruning, however, also selects a subset of each layer by heuristic value, so Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} limits it in the same way.

\begin{corollary}[pruning bound]
\label{cor-unsplit}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, $\unioneffort(\task,h)\leq W\sum_{g<\copt,\,L_g\ne\emptyset}\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})\leq2Wn\cdot\effort(\task,\hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

It holds since $S_g^h=L_g\cap h^{-1}(\{v\mid v\leq\copt-g\})$ by Lemma~\ref{lem-identity}.
Removing states thus guarantees no smaller BDD: in Example~\ref{ex-parity}, keeping only the states with $h=0$ removes half of $S$ but enlarges its BDD from one to $\Theta(n)$ nodes.
It concerns the expanded BDDs of forward product-at-evaluation \bddastar, not the relational products of SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} or \ghseta\ and not bidirectional search.

\subsection{The Bound Is Tight}

As long as the width is at most linear in $n$, the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor-effort} cannot be improved by more than a constant factor in any of its three parameters.

\begin{theorem}[joint lower bound]
\label{thm-tight}
For every $2\leq\values\leq W$ and every power of two $B\geq4W$, there is a polynomial-size task with $n=\Theta(B)$ Boolean state bits and a consistent, admissible heuristic $h$, represented by an ADD with $O(BW)$ nodes, with exactly $\values$ finite values and $\width_\pi(h)=W$ such that
\[
  \frac{\effort(\task,h)}{\effort(\task,\hblind)}
  \geq \frac{\values Wn}{120}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Write $B=2^m$.
The task generates an address $a$ of $m$ bits, $B$ modular bits $y$ and $B$ data bits $z$, keeps exactly the states with $z_a=1$ by a multiplexer filter and carries this set unchanged through $B$ layers before reaching the goal.
The heuristic colors the residue $\sum_iy_i\bmod W$ with $\values$ values.
At every central cut of the $y$-bits, each bucket has $BW$ distinct cofactors that combine an address and a residue, which yields at least $\values WB^3/2$ nodes over all buckets and layers, whereas blind effort is at most $15B^2$ and $n\leq4B$ (supplement).
\end{proof}

Hence, none of $\values$, $W$ and $n$ can be dropped from the bound.
For each power of two $B$, the construction covers the full region $2\leq\values\leq W\leq B/4$, and varying $B$ scales $n$.
The construction is a worst case and not a prediction for typical tasks.
In this family, the heuristic prunes nothing, so its effort ratio equals its fragmentation factor, and both bounds also hold for fragmentation alone.

\begin{corollary}[fragmentation bound]
\label{cor-frag}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, $\fragfactor(\task,h)\leq\profilescore(\task,h)\leq2\values Wn$.
The family of Theorem~\ref{thm-tight} has $\fragfactor(\task,h)\geq\values Wn/120$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every expanded set $S_g^h$ is nonempty and contains no goal state, so Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} gives $\cofcount(\chi_{S_g^h})\leq2n\bddsize{S_g^h}$.
With $\cutprod{S}{h}\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S)$ and $|A_g(h)|\leq\values$, the numerator of $\profilescore$ is at most $2\values Wn\,\unioneffort(\task,h)$.
In the family of Theorem~\ref{thm-tight}, every bucket of every layer below \copt\ satisfies $g+v\leq\copt$, so $S_g^h=L_g$ and $\unioneffort(\task,h)=\effort(\task,\hblind)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Perfect Heuristic Has Exponential Width}

In the tasks of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, where $n$ is their size parameter and not the number of state bits, the order $\pi_n$ places chain variables before two blocks $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and $v_{n+1},\ldots,v_{2n}$.
At the critical layer, a state is not a dead end exactly when $\bigvee_i(v_i\wedge v_{n+i})$ holds, which yields their exponentially large bucket.

\begin{theorem}[width lower bound]
\label{thm-lb}
For the tasks $\task_n$ and orders $\pi_n$ of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, $\width_{\pi_n}(\hstar) \geq 2^{n}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the cut after the first block, encode each $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ as a prefix.
If $i\in A\setminus A'$, setting only $v_{n+i}$ in the suffix distinguishes the two prefixes: one state has goal distance one and the other is a dead end.
Thus, one cut has $2^n$ distinct cofactors of \hstar\ for every total extension.
\end{proof}

By Corollary~\ref{cor-effort} and $\values\leq W$, a heuristic can increase the effort exponentially only if its width is exponential, and Theorem~\ref{thm-lb} confirms that the perfect heuristic of these tasks has exponential width.
Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} applied to their exponentially large bucket already implies this; the direct argument gives the exact cut.
Exponential width is necessary for such an increase but not sufficient, and bounded width is sufficient but not necessary for small buckets.
Moreover, \hstar\ takes only $O(n)$ values here, so the number of values alone does not control fragmentation.

% ============================ experiments ================================
\section{Experiments}
\label{sec-experiments}

Our experiments answer three questions.
Which of the two effects explains when a heuristic hurts symbolic search (Q1)?
Do heuristics pay off once they no longer fragment the layers (Q2)?
How tight are our bounds, and do they predict fragmentation (Q3)?

\paragraph{Setup.}
All experiments use SymK \citep{speck-et-al-jair2025} with forward product-at-evaluation \bddastar\ and a fixed, task-specific BDD variable order, the Gamer order unless stated otherwise.
SymK selects buckets by increasing $(f,g)$ and tests for goals at selection, as Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} assumes.
We use the optimal sequential IPC tasks and remove 290 tasks with zero-cost operators, which Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} excludes, and 30 tasks with axioms, which leaves \num{\CapFullTasks} of \num{1697} tasks from \CapFullDomains\ domains.
Each run is limited to 300 seconds of CPU time and 8\,GiB of memory.
After using 50 tasks for a screening study of PDB selection, we split the remaining \num{\CapPrimaryTasks} tasks into a domain-balanced development set of 275 tasks, on which we developed all measurements, and a disjoint holdout set of \num{\HoldoutTasks} tasks from \HoldoutDomains\ domains, on which we report all results.
For each study, we fixed the protocol, the primary analysis and the decision criteria before inspecting any holdout result; the supplement gives them together with all data, and we mark all post-hoc analyses.

\paragraph{Heuristics.}
The \emph{PDB study}, designed to evaluate PDB selection by width, runs blind search and 16 PDB selectors that limit cofactor width to $K\in\{2,4,\ldots,32\}$ without or with value caps, limit ADD size at five matching budgets or impose no limit (\num{\HoldoutCells} runs).
The \emph{family study} runs blind search and five heuristics from three families (\num{\GenCells} runs).
Three are M\&S heuristics: two with \num{10000} abstract states and a merge strategy that does or does not agree with the BDD order, and one with \num{50000} states and a merge strategy that does not.
The other two are a single PDB whose pattern CEGAR selects within \num{1000000} abstract states and integer potentials with $|P(v,w)|\leq4$ optimized for the initial state.
Both studies record the expanded buckets and the unsplit sets of every search.
For each heuristic and each task that it and blind search both solve, we form a \emph{pair} and compute its effort ratio $r$, unsplit ratio $u$ and fragmentation factor, and we call the pair \emph{harmful} if $r>1$, \emph{helpful} if $r<1$ and \emph{neutral} otherwise; all \NeutralPairs\ neutral pairs expand exactly the blind layers ($u=\fragfactor=1$).

\paragraph{Q1: Why Heuristics Hurt.}
Figure~\ref{fig:harm} and Table~\ref{tab:families} show that harmful heuristics barely prune but fragment.
Of the \num{\GenAllPairs} pairs of the family study, \num{\GenAllHarmful} are harmful.
On these pairs, the median unsplit ratio lies between \GenMinHurtUnion\ and \GenMaxHurtUnion\ across the three families, whereas the median fragmentation factor lies between \GenMinHurtFrag\ and \GenMaxHurtFrag.
In contrast, on the \num{\GenAllHelpful} pairs where the heuristic helps, it prunes strongly (median $u=\GenAllHelpUnion$) and fragments little (median \GenAllHelpFrag).
The informative finding is that $u$ stays close to one for harmful pairs, even though many of them remove states (see below).
As a prespecified check, we asked that the share $S$ of fragmentation in the harmful effort, $S=\sum\ln\fragfactor/\sum\ln r$ over the harmful pairs, is at least \GenShareThreshold\ and that at least \GenStrongThresholdPercent\% of the pairs with $r>2$ have $\fragfactor>u$.
All three families meet it; a share above one means that harmful heuristics still prune slightly overall.
A post-hoc bootstrap that resamples domains gives 95\% intervals for $S$ above \GenShareThreshold\ for every family (lowest \BootPotShareLow--\BootPotShareHigh\ for potentials, pooled \BootAllShareLow--\BootAllShareHigh) and \BootAllUnionLow--\BootAllUnionHigh\ for the pooled median $u$ of harmful pairs.
Since $S=1-\sum\ln u/\sum\ln r$, and since $r>2$ implies $\fragfactor>u$ whenever $u<\sqrt2$, the check fails only if harmful heuristics substantially enlarge the expanded parts of the layers, so it only rules out this alternative explanation.
In terms of coverage, no heuristic solves more tasks than blind search (\GenBlindSolved).

The PDB study shows the same pattern in a post-hoc analysis.
Harmful PDBs barely prune (median $u=\HarmHurtUnionMedian$) but fragment (median \HarmHurtFragMedian), and all \HarmDoubled\ pairs with $r>2$ have a fragmentation factor above two, while none has $u>2$.
More informative PDBs prune more but also fragment more: with \emph{concordance}, the fraction of pairs of PDBs for the same task that a score orders correctly, averaged equally over domains, the more informative PDB expands fewer states and has smaller unsplit effort (\HoldoutStatesQualityMacro\ and \HoldoutUnionQualityMacro), but fragments less only with concordance \HoldoutFragmentQualityMacro.


A post-hoc analysis shows why harmful heuristics prune so little.
Figure~\ref{fig:layers} shows the layers of a typical harmful M\&S pair, the one whose ratio of expanded states, unsplit ratio and fragmentation factor lie closest to the medians over all harmful M\&S pairs, on a Trucks task with $\copt=\ExCost$ and $h(\sinit)=\ExInitH$.
In the first \ExUnprunedLayers\ layers, the expanded part has exactly as many BDD nodes as the whole layer, but the heuristic splits it into up to \ExMaxBuckets\ buckets with up to \ExMaxUnprunedFrag\ times as many nodes.
In layers \ExEnlargedFirst\ to \ExEnlargedLast, the heuristic prunes states, but the expanded part needs up to \ExMaxEnlarged\ times as many nodes as the whole layer, and only the remaining layers shrink.
Overall, the heuristic expands only \ExS\ times as many states as blind search, but its unsplit ratio is \ExU, and with fragmentation factor \ExFrag\ its effort ratio is \ExR.

\paragraph{Q2: Heuristics without Fragmentation.}
A search that knows \copt, expands the layers by increasing $g$ and removes states with $f>\copt$ without splitting the layers expands exactly the sets $S_g^h$ (Lemma~\ref{lem-identity}), so its effort ratio is $u$.
In a post-hoc comparison, such an oracle search would expand fewer BDD nodes than blind search on \OracleAllUnsplitBelow\ of the \num{\GenAllPairs} pairs of the family study, compared with \OracleAllEffortBelow\ for \bddastar, and the geometric mean of $u$ is \OracleAllGeoU\ versus \OracleAllGeoR\ for $r$ (M\&S \OracleMsGeoU\ versus \OracleMsGeoR, PDB \OraclePdbGeoU\ versus \OraclePdbGeoR, potentials \OraclePotGeoU\ versus \OraclePotGeoR).
Forward search, however, learns \copt\ only when it finds a plan.
Three further studies therefore compare practical searches that avoid fragmentation with \bddastar\ and blind forward search, each on the holdout set with its own runs of both and with Holm correction over its own sign tests (Table~\ref{tab:remedy}).
\emph{Prune-only} search expands the blind layers whole and uses the heuristic only to remove dead ends.
\emph{Batched} search keeps the order of \bddastar\ but computes the successors of buckets with the same $g$-value and $f$-values at most one apart as one BDD if their union has no more nodes than its parts.
\emph{Unsplit} search runs iterative deepening on an $f$-bound $B$ that starts at $h(\sinit)$: each iteration expands the layers by increasing $g$, each as one BDD, and removes states with $g+h>B$, so the first plan it finds is optimal whenever $B\geq\copt$; otherwise, $B$ grows at least to the smallest removed $f$-value and by a step that doubles in every iteration.
\emph{Bounded} search first runs LAMA \citep{richter-westphal-jair2010} for at most 30 seconds and then prune-only search that removes states whose $f$-value is at least the cost of LAMA's plan, so it either finds a cheaper plan, which is optimal, or proves LAMA's plan optimal.
Development runs fixed the window of batching and the bound schedule of unsplit search and checked bounded search, which we did not tune.

Without a bound, avoiding fragmentation does not make the heuristics pay off.
Prune-only search recovers the losses of potentials (+\RemPotPruneVsPlainGains/$-$\RemPotPruneVsPlainLosses\ against \bddastar) but expands as many BDD nodes as blind search (median ratio \RemPotPruneEffortRemedy), since it removes dead ends on at most \RemPhMaxPruneDeadEnds\ tasks.
Batching leaves the expanded buckets unchanged, and unsplit search spends more on earlier iterations and on overshooting \copt\ than it saves: the last iteration accounts for a median of only \UbMinLastShare--\UbMaxLastShare\% of its effort, and its median effort ratio to blind search exceeds that of \bddastar\ for every heuristic (potentials: \UbPotEffortUnsplit\ versus \UbPotEffortPlain).
With the bound of LAMA, the heuristics no longer hurt: bounded search solves significantly more tasks than \bddastar\ with the PDB (+\BdPdbVsPlainGains/$-$\BdPdbVsPlainLosses) and potentials (+\BdPotVsPlainGains/$-$\BdPotVsPlainLosses) and slightly but not significantly more than blind forward search with every heuristic.

Their \PotAllHarmful\ harmful pairs from \PotAllHarmfulDomains\ domains meet the prespecified criterion of Q1 with $S=\PotAllShare$, and all \PotAllStrong\ pairs with $r>2$ have $\fragfactor>u$.

Effort predicts runtime well: for the plain heuristics, the ratios of expanded BDD nodes and of search time to those of blind forward search have a Spearman correlation of \RemEffTimeAllRho\ (\RemEffTimeMinRho--\RemEffTimeMaxRho\ per family).

\paragraph{Q3: Tightness of the Bounds.}
No observed fragmentation exceeds its bounds on any of the \num{\SlackObservations} solved and completely recorded runs of the PDB study.
For these PDBs, fragmentation is moderate: it exceeds one in \SlackFragAboveOnePercent\% of the runs, with median \SlackFragMedian, 90th percentile \SlackFragNinety\ and maximum \SlackFragMax.
The heuristics of the family study are far from the worst case: their median widths range from \WidthMedianMin\ to \WidthMedianMax\ with \ValuesMedianMin--\ValuesMedianMax\ values on tasks with a median of \BitsMedianMin--\BitsMedianMax\ state bits, so $2\values Wn$, with the number of ADD values for $\values$, exceeds their observed fragmentation by a median factor of \WidthSlackMedian\ and at least \WidthSlackMin\ (post hoc).
In a post-hoc analysis of the PDB study, the median factor by which a bound exceeds the observed fragmentation is \SlackProfileMedian\ for the profile bound, \SlackWidthMedian\ for its relaxation to width and \SlackAddMedian\ for the ADD bound.
However, the bounds do not predict fragmentation.
The profile bound orders the PDBs of a task by fragmentation with concordance \HoldoutRepresentationProfileMacro, but $1/\unioneffort$, which shares its denominator, already reaches \HoldoutDenominatorNullMacro.
When only the variable order of one PDB changes, the profile bound predicts the effect of switching from the Gamer to the Fast Downward order (concordance \OrderProfileMacro), but not among the Gamer orders of four unseen random seeds (\SeedCartesianMacro, below the \SeedNullMacro\ of $1/\unioneffort$).
Likewise, aligning the M\&S merge order with the BDD order, which bounds its width, changes fragmentation by a geometric mean factor of only \PruneAlignedFragRatio\ on \PruneAlignedTasks\ tasks (post hoc).
Nor does width yield better PDBs: a cap-aware selector fits more informative PDBs into a width budget, but its coverage gain at a budget of 8 does not replicate at 16, and no PDB selector solves more tasks than blind search (supplement).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Pairs cover only tasks that both searches solve, so we cannot decompose the \PruneBlindOnlyMin--\PruneBlindOnlyMax\ tasks per heuristic configuration that only blind search solves.
On these \UnsolvedRuns\ runs, the heuristic search had already expanded a median of \UnsolvedEffortMedian\ times the whole blind effort when it ran out of time, and in \UnsolvedLayersAbove\ of the \UnsolvedLayers\ layers it had begun (\UnsolvedLayersPercent\%), its buckets already had more BDD nodes than the whole blind layer, which only fragmentation or enlarged subsets can cause (post hoc).
We exclude tasks with zero-cost operators, which Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} does not cover, and study forward product-at-evaluation search with one variable order per task and three heuristic families; operator potentials in \ghseta\ and symbolic PDBs as in Gamer remain open.
Coverage near the time limit of 300 seconds is noisy: blind forward search solves between \RemSolvedBlindFw\ and \UbSolvedBlindFw\ holdout tasks in different studies, so we compare configurations only within a study.

% ============================ related work ===============================
\section{Related Work}

\citet{edelkamp-kissmann-aaai2008} and \citet{kissmann-phd2012} derive polynomial upper and exponential lower bounds on the BDD sizes of the layers of blind symbolic search on planning benchmarks, whereas we bound heuristic search relative to blind search on the same task.
Guarantees for symbolic versus explicit search concern transition relations and goal formulas and explicitly leave heuristics aside \citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025}.
Residual width, products of cofactors and Apply are standard \citep{wegener-2000,bryant-ieeecomp1986}; new are their connection to the buckets of symbolic search, the cumulative bounds relative to blind search and the matching planning family.

\citet{edelkamp-aips2002} computes PDBs symbolically, and the planner Gamer guides \bddastar\ with symbolic PDBs \citep{kissmann-edelkamp-aaai2011,edelkamp-et-al-aaai2015}.
PDBs, pattern selection and their symbolic computation are well studied \citep{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,edelkamp-ecp2001,haslum-et-al-aaai2007,rovner-et-al-icaps2019,edelkamp-et-al-ecai2012}.
\citet{fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024} use operator-potential heuristics in \ghseta, which expands the same buckets as \bddastar\ but computes $h$ by splitting the transition relation.
Compared with blind forward search, their best potentials expand more BDDs, each smaller on average, and often fewer nodes in total \citep{fiser-et-al-aaai2022}: in our terms, fragmentation grows, but pruning outweighs it.
Their potentials are optimized for the average state while keeping the value of the initial state maximal, whereas ours are capped integers.
Prune-only search resembles the pruning of blind symbolic search with $h^2$ mutexes \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}, with dead ends of a heuristic instead of mutexes.
ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}} \citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002} instead maps states to their values in one ADD, which by the argument of Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} has at most $\cutprod{S}{h}$ nodes for a set $S$ and thus avoids the factor \values\ of Theorem~\ref{thm-partition}.
Symbolic breadth-first heuristic search \citep{jensen-et-al-icaps2006} expands unsplit layers and prunes them with an upper bound on the plan cost, as our unsplit search does with its iterated bound.
Partitioned BDDs in verification likewise represent a function by several BDDs over a partition of its inputs, which can be smaller or larger than one BDD \citep{narayan-et-al-iccad1996}.
Symbolic perimeter abstractions \citep{torralba-et-al-ijcai2016a,torralba-et-al-aij2018} combine symbolic search with abstraction heuristics in other ways.

% ============================ conclusions ================================
\section{Conclusions}

We separated the two effects of a heuristic in symbolic search by factoring the number of expanded BDD nodes into unsplit effort and fragmentation.
On our benchmarks, PDB, M\&S and potential heuristics that more than doubled the expanded BDD nodes barely pruned but fragmented the layers, and even when they removed many states, their BDDs barely shrank.
Cofactor width bounds fragmentation, pruned sets and the effort relative to blind search, tightly for a wide range of parameters, but it does not predict fragmentation under unseen variable orders.
Heuristics therefore pay off in symbolic \astar\ only where they remove most states, which even stronger potentials did on fewer than half of the tasks.
Pruning without fragmenting would pay off far more often, but iterative deepening on the bound cost more than it saved, and the plan costs of LAMA removed the harm of the heuristics without enabling much pruning, which suggests that tight bounds on the plan cost are the missing ingredient.
