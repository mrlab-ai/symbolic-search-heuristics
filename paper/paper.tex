% Keep the abstract within the provisional 250-word target.
\begin{abstract}
Forward symbolic \astar\ partitions BDD frontiers by heuristic value. Because
BDD size is not monotone under subsets, even a perfect heuristic can increase
expansion size exponentially. We apply fixed-order MTBDD width---called
\emph{cofactor width} $W$---to heuristic ADDs. For exact cost layers $L_g$, a
consistent heuristic with $\values$ finite values has the cut-sensitive bound
$\values W\sum_g\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})$. This certificate and the ordinary
global-ADD Apply bound can each be linearly sharper than the other. For $n$
state bits it implies a blind-effort ratio of at most
$2\values Wn\leq2W^2n$. A family with $\values=W$ and $n=\Theta(W)$ attains
$\Omega(W^2)$; joint $\values Wn$ tightness remains open. The known
exponential pathology has exponential width. We also derive safe terminal
transforms and bounds for common heuristic families.

As a case study, we use uniform caps in a width-constrained PDB selector.
On score-identical raw pools, candidate containment prevents any loss in the
exact selector's score or semantic nontriviality. At $K=8$ on the fixed
\CapPrimaryTasks-task primary complement, equal-domain macro coverage rises from
\CapPrimaryExactMacroPercent\% to \CapPrimaryCapMacroPercent\%
(\CapPrimaryWins/\CapPrimaryLosses\ wins/losses). Among
\CapPrimaryTracePairs\ tasks with both complete identical-pool traces, capping
adds \CapPrimarySemanticWins\ nontrivial selections and removes none; on the
separate 590 pairs with complete operational metrics, expanded BDD nodes fall
11.6\%. Unaligned M\&S retains the highest descriptive coverage, so this is not
overall planner dominance.
\end{abstract}

% ============================ introduction ===============================
\section{Introduction}

Heuristics guide explicit \astar\ by ranking states
\citep{hart-et-al-ieeessc1968,pearl-1984}, but in symbolic search the same
information partitions compact frontiers. Symbolic search expands state sets
represented as binary decision diagrams
\citep[BDDs;][]{mcmillan-1993,edelkamp-helmert-ecp1999,bryant-ieeecomp1986};
because BDD size is not monotone under subsets, an informative split can be
representationally costly. Blind symbolic search, particularly in its
bidirectional form, remains a strong approach to cost-optimal planning
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,speck-et-al-jair2025}, and a key component
of the winning entry of the optimal track of the International Planning
Competition (IPC) 2023 \citep{speck-ipc2023}.

Combining the two paradigms has a long history. \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} represents a heuristic $h$ as one BDD per
heuristic value and splits each search layer into buckets of states that
share a $g$- and an $h$-value. SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\
\citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008} instead fold the heuristic-value change into
the transition relations, and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
\citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002} stores $h$ as an algebraic decision diagram
(ADD). Empirically, all of these variants beat blind symbolic search in
some domains and lose in others
\citep{torralba-phd2015,torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} explained why: in symbolic search the
effort of an expansion is governed by the number of BDD nodes rather than
the number of represented states, and they prove that even the perfect
heuristic \hstar\ can increase the cumulative size of the expanded BDDs
exponentially. Against this backdrop, heuristic symbolic search has seen
limited uptake. A notable recent direction uses operator-potential heuristics
\citep{fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}, which integrate
linear-programming-based potential heuristics
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015} into
\ghseta\ and report little increased BDD size in practice.
This evidence motivates a structural explanation, but does not itself give
a condition under which a heuristic \emph{provably} cannot cause a large
representational overhead. Recently,
\citet{speck-helmert-ecai2025} gave worst-case performance
guarantees for symbolic search relative to explicit search, but their
analysis concerns the representation of transition relations and goal
formulas and explicitly leaves heuristics aside.

We apply standard fixed-order residual/multi-terminal-BDD (MTBDD) width
\citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} to
heuristic total ADDs, using \emph{cofactor width} as a planning-facing name. It
is the largest distinct-residual count after any prefix of the search order,
equivalently the largest cut through the quasi-reduced total ADD. The
underlying product-graph argument for binary synthesis is standard
\citep[Thm.~3.3.6]{wegener-2000}. Our contribution is to
couple it to exact symbolic-search bucket semantics across cost layers, derive
cumulative effort bounds and separations, and instantiate them for
planning-heuristic families. Operationally, width is measured on the same
total ADD used by search, so it can serve as a pre-search acceptance
certificate and guide safe terminal coarsening.

Our central contribution is a structural safety regime for the pathology of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, together with its limits and one operational
case study. A fragmentation theorem bounds the total size of all value slices
of a state set $S$ by $TW\cofcount(\chi_S)$ for a width-$W$,
$T$-terminal heuristic. Together with an exact bucket identity, it yields the
cut-sensitive cumulative bound and the blind-effort ratio
$2\values Wn\leq2W^2n$. Before the cofactor count is relaxed, this certificate
and the standard total-ADD Apply bound are incomparable. A new diagonal family
has $\values=W$, $n=\Theta(W)$, and ratio $\Omega(W^2)$, which also realizes
each pairwise product up to a constant; joint $\values Wn$ tightness remains
open. The known exponential pathology has exponential width under its analyzed
order.

We give width-nonincreasing safe terminal transforms, including nested
uniform caps, and bound integer potentials, bounded-state PDBs, and linear
M\&S aligned with the search order. We then prove that cap-aware PDB selection
is backward compatible with exact-width selection: on score-identical raw pools it
cannot lower the frozen score or lose semantic nontriviality, and an exact
fallback is the exact selector's winner.

Using SymK \citep{speck-et-al-jair2025}, we evaluate exact-width and
cap-aware selectors at $K=8$ in a fresh common-environment matrix. On the fixed
\CapPrimaryTasks-task primary complement, cap-aware selection improves the
prespecified macro coverage by \CapPrimaryMacroDeltaPoints\ percentage points
(\CapPrimaryWins\ wins and \CapPrimaryLosses\ losses). Among
\CapPrimaryTracePairs\ tasks with both complete identical-pool traces, it adds
\CapPrimarySemanticWins\ semantically nontrivial selections and loses none. On
the separate 590 pairs with complete operational metrics, it reduces expanded
BDD nodes by 11.6\% and image time by 10.7\%. Blind,
conventional CEGAR PDB, and unaligned M\&S provide descriptive context; M\&S
retains higher full-census coverage.

% ============================ background =================================
\section{Background}

We use finite deterministic SAS$^{+}$ tasks
$\task=\langle\vars,\ops,\sinit,\sgoal\rangle$
\citep{backstrom-nebel-compint1995}. Operators have positive integer costs.
The functions $\gstar(s)$ and $\hstar(s)$ are respectively the cheapest
cost from \sinit\ to $s$ and from $s$ to a goal, with value $\infty$ for a
dead end. We write $\copt=\hstar(\sinit)$ and assume
$0<\copt<\infty$. A heuristic
$h\colon\states\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible}
if $h\leq\hstar$, and \emph{consistent} if
$h(s)\leq\cost(o)+h(s[o])$ for every applicable $o$ and $h=0$ on goals.
The blind heuristic is $\hblind\equiv0$.

Fix a Boolean state encoding in which every SAS$^{+}$ variable $v$ uses
$\lceil\log_2|D_v|\rceil$ Boolean bits. Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be those
bits in order $\pi$, and give every heuristic a total extension to Boolean
strings that encode no state. The order is \emph{variable-contiguous} if
all bits of each SAS$^{+}$ variable are consecutive. A state set $S$ is represented
by the reduced ordered BDD (OBDD) of its characteristic function $\chi_S$;
$\bddsize{S}$ counts its inner nodes \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986}. For a prefix
$\rho\in\{0,1\}^i$, let $f|_\rho$ be the residual function and
$\sub{i}{f}=\{f|_\rho\mid\rho\in\{0,1\}^i\}$ its distinct cofactors.

\begin{definition}[cofactor count]
\label{def-cofcount}
For a function $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the \emph{cofactor count} is
$\cofcount(f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} |\sub{i}{f}|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem-qsize}
For every Boolean $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$,
$\bddsize{f} \leq \cofcount(f) \leq n \cdot (\bddsize{f} + 2)$.
If $f$ is nonconstant, also
$\cofcount(f)\leq2n\bddsize{f}$.
\end{lemma}

$\cofcount(f)$ is the inner-node count of the quasi-reduced OBDD. At every
cut, reduced nodes inject into cofactors, while every cofactor is a constant
or is rooted at a unique reduced node; this proves the lemma. Its factor
$n$ is unavoidable already for $f=x_1$, where
$\cofcount(f)=2n-1$ but $\bddsize{f}=1$. For the last inequality, the displayed
upper bound suffices when $\bddsize{f}\geq2$. A nonconstant one-node BDD is a
literal $x_j$ or $\neg x_j$, whose cofactor count is $2n-j\leq2n$.

Symbolic uniform-cost search represents each exact layer
$L_g=\{s\mid\gstar(s)=g\}$ by one BDD
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}. Product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} intersects layers with the level sets
$\level{h}{v}=\{s\mid h(s)=v\}$. It removes closed states, discards
$h=\infty$, represents each $(g,v)$ bucket by one BDD, selects by increasing
$(f,g)=(g+v,g)$, and tests for a goal when a bucket is selected
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. Each open bucket persists until that exact key
is selected. Immediately before expansion, its resident set is intersected
with the complement of states already closed at their best known $g$;
``bucket'' below means this residual set. Selection moves the entire residual
bucket to closed before generating successors. These queue, duplicate,
goal-test, and positive-cost assumptions define the scope of our cumulative
guarantees.

\begin{definition}[expansion size, \citealp{speck-et-al-icaps2020}]
\label{def-effort}
Let $B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ denote the unique BDD expanded by \bddastar\ with
$g$-value $g$ and $h$-value $v$ on task \task\ with heuristic $h$, with
$B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ representing the empty set if no such expansion
occurs. In the consistent product-at-evaluation setting above, the
\emph{expansion size} of \bddastar\ is
$\effort(\task,h) = \sum_{\,g + v \leq \copt,\ g < \copt}
|B_{\task,h}(g,v)|$; Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} proves that these and only these
nonempty buckets are expanded before termination.
\end{definition}

For $\hblind$, \bddastar\ is uniform-cost search and
$\effort(\task,\hblind) = \sum_{g < \copt} \bddsize{L_g}$.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} prove that there are tasks on which even
$\hstar$ has exponentially larger effort
than blind search: a subset can have a much larger BDD than its superset,
so both slicing and pruning can inflate representations. We seek a
heuristic property that controls every value-selected subset.

% ===================== width-bounded heuristics ==========================
\section{Bounded Cofactor Width}

\begin{definition}[cofactor width]
\label{def-width}
The \emph{cofactor width} of a heuristic $h$ under variable order $\pi$
is $\width_\pi(h) = \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} |\sub{i}{h}|$. We call $h$
\emph{$(\pi,W)$-width-bounded} if $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and write
\emph{width} for cofactor width throughout.
\end{definition}

The cofactors are precisely the states of a deterministic layered automaton
that reads $\pi$; hence width is also the largest level of the heuristic's
quasi-reduced multi-terminal BDD \citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}. It is a
standard fixed-order residual/MTBDD representation property applied here to
heuristics. It is neither a newly proposed width measure nor the unrelated
planning notion of novelty width \citep{lipovetzky-geffner-ecai2012}.

\begin{proposition}[value range]
\label{prop-range}
If $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, then $|\hrange(h)| \leq W$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, the final cofactors are the attained constants. Write
$\values=|h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}|$ for finite values on planning
states and $T=|\hrange(h)|$ for terminals of the total extension; then
$\values\leq T\leq W$.

\begin{proposition}[closure]
\label{prop-closure}
Let $h_1, h_2$ have widths $W_1, W_2$ under $\pi$ and let
$\oplus \in \{+, \max, \min\}$. Then
$\width_\pi(h_1 \oplus h_2) \leq W_1 \cdot W_2$.
\end{proposition}

At each cut, a combined cofactor is determined by a pair of operand
cofactors. Thus maximization and admissible additive combinations remain
width-bounded, with multiplicative component bounds.

Call a possibly signed function
$q\colon\states\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$ a \emph{raw admissible and
consistent estimate} if $q(s)\leq \hstar(s)$ for every state,
$q(s)\leq c+q(s')$ for every transition $s\xrightarrow{c}s'$, and
$q(s)\leq0$ on goals.

\begin{proposition}[safe terminal transforms]
\label{prop-transform}
Let $q$ be a raw admissible and consistent estimate, equipped with a
total extension
$q \colon \{0,1\}^{n} \to \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}$. Let
$\phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}
 \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \cup \{\infty\}$
be nondecreasing, satisfy $\phi(0)=0$, $\phi(\infty)=\infty$, and for
every $a\in\mathbb Z$ and integer $c\geq0$ satisfy
\[
\phi(a+c) \leq \phi(a)+c.
\]
Then $h=\phi\circ q$ is admissible and consistent and
$\width_\pi(h)\leq\width_\pi(q)$.
\end{proposition}

Monotonicity and the cost condition transfer consistency and imply
$\phi(a)\leq\max(0,a)$; terminal mapping cannot create cofactors. Thus both
rectification and the cap
$\phi_\kappa(a)=\min(\max(0,a),\kappa)$ for finite $a$ (and
$\phi_\kappa(\infty)=\infty$) are safe and width nonincreasing. The cap has
at most $\kappa+1$ finite values and is pointwise strongest among such safe
transforms with that output ceiling. If $\kappa_1\leq\kappa_2$, then
$\phi_{\kappa_1}\circ q$ is a terminal relabeling of
$\phi_{\kappa_2}\circ q$, so its cofactor width is no larger. Caps therefore
form a nested safety--informativeness ladder: increasing $\kappa$ weakly
strengthens the heuristic, while decreasing it weakly decreases both the
finite-value count and width, hence the coarse $2\values Wn$ certificate.

Let $\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)$ be the reduced ordered ADD under $\pi$
\citep{bahar-et-al-iccad1993}.

\begin{proposition}[ADD characterization]
\label{prop-add}
$\width_\pi(h) \leq |\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| + |\hrange(h)|$ and
$|\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| \leq n \cdot \width_\pi(h)$.
\end{proposition}

Every cofactor is a terminal or a residual ADD node, and every ADD node at
a level is a distinct cofactor. Hence small width and a small ADD are
polynomially equivalent only in the order actually used by the search
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.

\begin{remark}[total extensions]
\label{rem-encoding}
Definitions over $\{0,1\}^n$ require fixing $h$ on Boolean assignments
that encode no state. Upper bounds apply to the chosen extension; the
families below use natural arithmetic or table extensions. Lower bounds
witnessed on genuine states apply to every extension.
\end{remark}

% ======================= fragmentation theorems ==========================
\section{Bounded Fragmentation}
\label{sec-fragmentation}

Fix $\pi$ and write $W=\width_\pi(h)$. At every cut, the cofactor of a
value-selected subset is determined by the pair consisting of the state-set
cofactor and the heuristic cofactor.

\begin{theorem}[bucket bound]
\label{thm-bucket}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and $U \subseteq \hrange(h)$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bddsize{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}
  &\leq \cofcount(\chi_{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}) \\
  &\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq Wn(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At cut $i$, equal pairs $(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)$ induce equal slice
cofactors, so their number is at most
$|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|\,|\sub{i}{h}|\leq
W|\sub{i}{\chi_S}|$. Sum over cuts and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize}. The supplement gives the explicit injection.
\end{proof}

This covers a bucket ($U=\{v\}$), recognized-dead-end removal, and
threshold pruning. No value-selected subset can inflate the reduced BDD by
more than $Wn$ up to the additive term.

\begin{theorem}[partition bound]
\label{thm-partition}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{v \in \hrange(h)} \bddsize{S \cap \level{h}{v}}
  &\leq TW\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq TWn(\bddsize{S}+2) \\
  &\leq W^2n(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
If $S\subseteq\states$ and the sum ranges only over finite values attained
on planning states, the first two bounds hold with $T$ replaced by $\values$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to each of $T$ buckets (or the $\values$
finite planning-state buckets), then use $T\leq W$.
\end{proof}

The effort measure sums each reduced bucket separately even if a shared
unique table reuses nodes. The factor $n$ cannot be removed: the supplement
gives a width-two parity slice whose reduced BDD is $\Theta(n)$ times its
one-node source set.

Writing $A$ for the total heuristic ADD's inner-node count and
$b=\bddsize{S}$, relabeling its terminals by membership in $U$
\citep[Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} and applying standard Boolean Apply
\citep[Sec.~4.3]{bryant-ieeecomp1986} gives
$\bddsize{S\cap h^{-1}(U)}\leq bA+b+A$. Thus the two certificates
combine as $\min\{W\cofcount(\chi_S),bA+b+A\}$. With the bucket identity
below, the ADD certificate yields the blind-effort ratio bound
$\values(2A+1)$. A nonconstant ADD has one root and at most $W$ nodes on
each other level, so $A\leq1+(n-1)W$; since then $W\geq2$, the ADD ratio is
at most $2\values Wn$. Thus the coarse width ratio is not a tighter
replacement for ADD size.
The width bound can instead be sharper before $\cofcount$ is relaxed. The
certificates are genuinely incomparable: if $h$ is parity and $S$ is Hamming
weight $0$ modulo $3$, then $W\cofcount(\chi_S)=\Theta(n)$ while the Apply
bound is $\Theta(n^2)$; for $h=x_1$ and $S=\{x_1=1\}$, Apply gives $3$ while
$W\cofcount(\chi_S)=\Theta(n)$. The supplement proves both separations. We use
width for this cut-sensitive structure and its compositional family bounds.

% ======================= search-effort guarantees ========================
\section{Forward Product-at-Evaluation Search Guarantees}
\label{sec-effort}

\begin{lemma}[bucket identity]
\label{lem-identity}
Let $h$ be consistent. \bddastar\ on \task\ with $h$ selects every
bucket key $(g,v)$ with $g + v \leq \copt$ and $g < \copt$ and
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v} \neq \emptyset$ exactly once before terminating,
and the BDD expanded with key $(g,v)$ represents exactly
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v}$. No other bucket is expanded before
termination.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Selection is lexicographic in $(f,g)$ by definition; consistency makes
generated successor $f$-keys nondecreasing, and positive costs prevent a
selected key from being repopulated. Induction along cheapest
paths gives optimal $g$-values and complete slices. An open prefix of an
optimal plan prevents termination or selection above $\copt$. The
supplement proves the five claims---monotone selection, no re-population,
optimal $g$-values, completeness, and no other expansions---in full.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[effort bound]
\label{thm-effort}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\effort(\task,h)
  &\leq \values W
    \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
      \cofcount(\chi_{L_g}) \\
  &\leq \values Wn
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr) \\
  &\leq W^2n
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} and Theorem~\ref{thm-partition} bound all selected
slices of each nonempty $L_g$ by
$\values W\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})$, and Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} bounds this by
$\values Wn(\bddsize{L_g}+2)$. If $N$ is the number of nonempty layers
below $\copt$, summing gives
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N)$. Positive integer costs imply
$N\leq\copt$, and $\values\leq W$ gives the coarse bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-effort}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort},
$\effort(\task, h) \leq 2\,\values W n \cdot
\effort(\task, \hblind) \leq
2\, W^{2} n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

Every nonempty pre-goal layer is proper, so Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} gives
$\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})\leq2n\bddsize{L_g}$. Summing the first inequality of
Theorem~\ref{thm-effort} proves the corollary directly.

The guarantee is one-sided: pruning savings remain unbounded. It concerns
frontier sizes in forward product-at-evaluation \bddastar, not primitive
relational products in SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} or \ghseta, and not cumulative
bidirectional effort. Variants visiting the same semantic buckets inherit
the state-set bound.

\subsection{A Quadratic Ratio in $W$ Is Realizable}

\begin{theorem}[quadratic width-ratio family]
\label{thm-tight}
For all integers $W \geq 2$ and $k \geq 4W$ there are tasks
$\task_{k,W}$ with $n=2k+2$ Boolean variables and consistent, admissible
heuristics $h$ with $\width_\pi(h)=W$ such that
$\effort(\task_{k,W},h)\geq
(W^2/12)\effort(\task_{k,W},\hblind)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Use a $k$-step binary-choice chain followed by a cost-$W$ goal action and
order the chain bits before the choice bits. The non-goal heuristic is the
negated choice residue modulo $W$; action costs make it admissible and
consistent, and its $W$ residuals give exact width $W$. Blind effort is
$(k+1)(3k+4)/2$. Summing the modular-bucket witnesses over all layers from
$2W$ through $k$ gives at least
$W^2(k-2W+1)(k-2W+2)/2$ heuristic effort. Their ratio is at least $W^2/12$.
The supplement gives the construction and cofactor witnesses.
\end{proof}

Taking $k=4W$ gives $\values=W$ and $n=8W+2\leq9W$. Hence the same
family has ratios at least
$\values W/12$, $\values n/108$, and $Wn/108$. If a universal upper
bound drops any one factor from $\values Wn$ while retaining the other two
multiplicatively, that pairwise dependence is therefore already
asymptotically tight on this diagonal slice. The theorem does not establish
that all three factors are simultaneously necessary, and it is not a
typical-case prediction.

\subsection{Exponential Width Is Necessary for the Known Pathology}

In the family of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, chain variables precede
two blocks $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and $v_{n+1},\ldots,v_{2n}$ in $\pi_n$.
At the critical layer, a state is not a dead end exactly when
$\bigvee_i(v_i\wedge v_{n+i})$ holds, producing their exponential bucket.

\begin{theorem}[width lower bound]
\label{thm-lb}
For the tasks $\task_n$ and orders $\pi_n$ of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020},
$\width_{\pi_n}(\hstar) \geq 2^{n}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the cut after the first $v$-block, encode each
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ as a
prefix. If $i\in A\setminus A'$, the valid suffix setting only
$v_{n+i}$ distinguishes the prefixes: one state has goal distance one and
the other is a dead end. Thus one cut has $2^n$ distinct \hstar-cofactors,
for every total extension.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm-effort} and~\ref{thm-lb} together delimit the
known pathology: its perfect heuristic has exponential width and therefore
evades the bounded-width regime. Our result is a parameterized upper bound,
not a converse: bounded width is sufficient, not necessary, for small buckets.
Moreover \hstar\
uses only $O(n)$ values here, so value count alone does not control the
prefix information entangled by its level sets.

The supplement derives additional consequences for idealized image-call
counts and pruning-only search.

\section{Width-Bounded Heuristic Families}
\label{sec-instances}

Let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous,
$d=\max_{v\in\vars}|D_v|$, and $m=|\vars|$.

\subsection{Potential Heuristics}

Integer potentials \citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022}
form $p(s)=\sum_vP(v,s(v))$ and expose the safe rectification
$\hpot=\max(0,p)$. Their standard constraints make $p$ a raw admissible
and consistent estimate, so Proposition~\ref{prop-transform} makes
$\hpot$ admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pot}
Let $p$ have integer potentials with $|P(v,w)|\leq M$ for all facts,
extended to non-state assignments by the same sum over the bit
encoding's induced table, with table values on invalid bit codes also
in $[-M,M]$, e.g., $0$, and let $\pi$ be variable-contiguous. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\width_\pi(\hpot)&\leq\width_\pi(p)\leq d(2mM+1),\\
|\hrange(\hpot)|&\leq mM+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-pot}
For rectified consistent integer potential heuristics with magnitudes at
most $M$,
\[
\effort(\task,\hpot)
 \leq 2d(mM+1)(2mM+1)n\cdot\effort(\task,\hblind),
\]
a factor polynomial in the task size and the numeric magnitude $M$.
\end{corollary}

The bound counts the accumulated sum and the at most $d$ partial codes of
the current variable; Corollary~\ref{cor-pot} substitutes it into
Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}. It is pseudo-polynomial in numeric $M$, not
polynomial in its bit length, and does not analyze delta-partitioned
\ghseta.

\subsection{Pattern Databases}

A pattern database \citep[PDB;][]{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,%
edelkamp-ecp2001} projects \task\ onto a pattern
$P\subseteq\vars$ and stores the abstract goal distance $h^P(s)$ of the
abstract state induced by $s$; this distance is admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pdb}
Let $P\subseteq\vars$ be any pattern under a variable-contiguous order
$\pi$. Extend its table to invalid encodings by ignoring non-pattern
bits and mapping an invalid pattern-variable code to $0$. Let
$|\states^P|=\prod_{v\in P}|D_v|$. Then
\[
\width_\pi(h^P)\leq |\states^P|.
\]
\end{proposition}

The residual lookup is determined by a partial abstract state and the current
bit buffer; invalid prefixes share the valid zero terminal. Pattern variables
need not form a prefix because non-pattern bits are skipped. Hence an
abstract-state budget itself induces a width bound. A PDB has at most
$|\states^P|$ finite values, so its effort ratio is at most
$2|\states^P|^2n$. A distinct invalid-code sink instead gives width at most
$|\states^P|+1$. Combining PDBs multiplies
component-width bounds.

\subsection{Linear Merge-and-Shrink}

Merge-and-shrink (M\&S) abstractions
\citep{helmert-et-al-jacm2014} iteratively merge factors of a factored
transition system and shrink intermediate abstractions to at most $N$
abstract states. With a \emph{linear} merge strategy, the abstraction
mapping is representable by cascading tables, equivalently a factored
mapping \citep{helmert-et-al-icaps2015}: a chain of tables
$\tau_1, \dots, \tau_m$ where $\tau_j$ maps the pair of the previous
intermediate abstract state and the value of variable $v_j$ to the next
intermediate abstract state. Its final abstract goal distance is admissible
and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-ms}
Let $h^{\alpha}$ be an M\&S heuristic built with a linear merge
strategy whose variable order agrees with the variable-contiguous
$\pi$, and suppose the valid output set after every cascade table, including
the final abstraction, has at most $N$ states. Extend each
table with a distinguished sink for invalid variable codes. Then
$\width_\pi(h^{\alpha}) \leq d \cdot (N+1)$.
\end{proposition}

Here the premise bounds every table output, not merely selected post-shrink
products. The table cascade remembers at most $N$ intermediate states (plus the
invalid-code sink) and one bit buffer. The final abstraction has at most
$N$ finite distances, giving effort ratio at most $2dN(N+1)n$.

The alignment premise is essential. Our causal-graph control explicitly
uses \texttt{align\_merge\_order=false}; it is \emph{not} covered by the
a priori formula, though its exact post-construction width in the search
order can instantiate the general theorems. Alignment supplies the formula
but may change heuristic quality. We establish no analogous small-width
guarantee here for $h^{\max}$, $h^{\mathrm{add}}$, or LM-cut, and
Theorem~\ref{thm-lb} shows that perfect information can require exponential
width. Full family proofs appear in the supplement.

\section{Case Study: Cap-Aware Width-Constrained PDB Selection}
\label{sec-synthesis}

The theory turns terminal coarsening into a controlled intervention.  Both
selectors invoke the same five generator recipes and options under a
$100{,}000$-abstract-state budget: the empty pattern, a prefix of the BDD
order, a goal/causal-order prefix, a goal/causal-order fill pattern that skips
oversized variables, and one CEGAR pattern \citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019}
with seed 2011 and a 10-second pattern-generation limit.  Patterns are
normalized before provenance-preserving deduplication, and every generator
label of a duplicate is retained.  The cap-aware path additionally removes
domain-one variables before normalization; this preserves abstract semantics
but can change the literal pattern record, so reconstructed raw-pool identity
is verified pairwise rather than assumed.  The empty pattern guarantees a
feasible width-one endpoint.

For each raw PDB, we build the heuristic's total ADD in the fixed search order
and count distinct residual nodes after every Boolean-bit prefix.  Dynamic
reordering is disabled; residuals at skipped manager levels remain in the
frontier, and invalid encodings retain the default-zero extension. Thus the
measured maximum is the exact cofactor width of the total extension used by
search. A candidate
is feasible exactly when its measured width is at most the fixed budget
$K=8$.

The exact selector tests only the uncapped PDB.  For each pattern, the
cap-aware selector materializes one raw PDB and derives
\[
  h_\kappa(s)=
  \begin{cases}
    \min\{h(s),\kappa\},&h(s)<\infty,\\
    \infty,&h(s)=\infty
  \end{cases}
\]
for caps $\kappa\in\{0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256\}$ below the raw
maximum finite value, followed by the exact transform.  Proposition
\ref{prop-transform} preserves admissibility and consistency, and the nested
cap order is nondecreasing in both information and width.  The selector
therefore retains the strongest feasible tested ladder member of each pattern
before comparing patterns. This per-pattern
step prevents a weak cap of one pattern from defeating a stronger feasible
transform of that same pattern.

Both selectors then apply the same frozen lexicographic score: initial-state
dead-end recognition, initial value, mean finite value, and abstract dead-end
fraction, followed by smaller width, abstract-state count, and pattern as tie
breakers.  The score is an information surrogate, not a runtime claim.  A
selection is called \emph{semantic-nontrivial} when its finite-value sum is
positive or it recognizes an abstract dead end.

\begin{proposition}[selector dominance]
\label{prop-selector-dominance}
Suppose both selectors complete on the same score-bearing normalized raw-PDB
pool. The
cap-aware final candidate set contains the exact selector's final candidate
set, and its selected lexicographic score is no smaller. Consequently, if the
exact winner recognizes the initial state as a dead end or is
semantic-nontrivial, so is the cap-aware winner. If neither winner recognizes
the initial state as a dead end, the cap-aware initial value is no smaller.
Moreover, if the cap-aware winner is an exact endpoint, both selectors return
the same pattern and heuristic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every exact-feasible raw PDB, the exact transform is the last endpoint of
the cap-aware ladder and is retained as that pattern's strongest feasible
member. Thus every exact candidate remains available when the same score is
maximized. The three consequences follow from the score's first four
components. For the last claim, let $e$ and $p$ be the exact-selector and
cap-aware winners, and suppose $p$ is exact. Candidate containment gives
$\mathrm{score}(p)\geq\mathrm{score}(e)$, while $p$ belongs to the exact set
and hence $\mathrm{score}(e)\geq\mathrm{score}(p)$. The deterministic total
score, including the pattern tie breaker, therefore gives $p=e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[selector safety]
\label{cor-selector-safety}
On every task satisfying Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, each completed selection by
either selector has effort at most $2K^2n$ times blind effort. At $K=8$, this
factor is at most $128n$. If the selected transform has finite cap $\kappa$,
the sharper factor is $2K\min\{K,\kappa+1\}n$.
\end{corollary}

Every returned transform is admissible and consistent, has measured width at
most $K$, and has at most $K$ finite values; a cap has at most $\kappa+1$.
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}. The guarantee concerns representation
effort, not heuristic quality or runtime.

For empirical attribution, we certify trace completeness and reconstructed
raw-pool identity pairwise. The supplement gives the producer, parser, score,
and trace contracts.

\section{Related Work}

\bddastar\ partitions frontiers by heuristic value
\citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998}; SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\ instead
partition transitions \citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008}, while ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
uses algebraic diagrams \citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002}. Even \hstar\ can
increase BDD-node effort exponentially \citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. We give
a checkable sufficient condition and show that their construction has
exponential width under its analyzed order; this is a representation-safety
certificate for one architecture, not a general runtime theorem.

PDBs and their symbolic use \citep{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,
edelkamp-ecp2001}, CEGAR generation \citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019}, symbolic
compilation \citep{edelkamp-et-al-ecai2012}, factored M\&S mappings
\citep{torralba-phd2015,helmert-et-al-icaps2015}, and potential heuristics
\citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}
all predate this work. We do not claim the generators or symbolic PDB
compilation as new. Our method verifies raw-pool identity, measures each total
ADD in the search order, filters by width, and selects a safe terminal
coarsening before cross-pattern ranking. The M\&S bound requires aligned merge
and search orders; the control is deliberately unaligned. The width parameter
of relaxed BDD heuristics for delete-free planning bounds a BDD constructed
over action choices \citep{castro-et-al-icaps2019}; our width instead belongs
to the total state-to-value ADD that slices search frontiers. Work on symbolic
versus explicit blind search \citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025}, variable ordering
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}, and symbolic perimeter abstractions
\citep{torralba-et-al-ijcai2016a,torralba-et-al-aij2018} concerns orthogonal
representation choices.

\section{Limitations}

The guarantee is specific to fixed-order product-at-evaluation forward
symbolic search with positive costs, consistent heuristics, and the stated
duplicate and goal-test policy. It bounds BDD-node representation effort, not
runtime, memory, heuristic quality, or plan quality. Width is order-dependent,
and the sharper certificate needs the cofactor counts of exact search layers.
Our logs do not contain those layer profiles, so the experiment studies a
theorem-guided selector rather than numerically validating
Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}. The ADD comparison is
also formal: we did not evaluate a matched selector that filters candidates by
global ADD size instead of width.

Empirically, this is a fixed-census case study at one primary budget $K=8$ and
one deterministic cap ladder. The outcome-informed development screen is
disclosed and excluded from the primary complement, but there is only one run
per cell and a hard resource cutoff. The primary coverage gain of
\CapPrimaryMacroDeltaPoints\ percentage points is modest and has no
population-inferential interpretation. Operational ratios
condition on completed metric pairs and therefore cannot explain coverage
discordances or joint timeouts; leave-one-domain-out stability does not remove
that censoring. Finally, unaligned M\&S has higher descriptive coverage. A
multi-$K$ replication with full cofactor profiles and a matched ADD-size
baseline would test how much of the result is specific to $K=8$, the cap
ladder, and the width certificate.

% ============================ conclusions ================================
\section{Conclusions}

For product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar, cofactor width provides a fixed-order
representation-safety certificate: a consistent width-$W$, $\values$-valued
heuristic has blind-effort ratio at most $2\values Wn\leq2W^2n$. The known
pathology has exponential width, while our diagonal lower family attains
$\Omega(W^2)$ for $\values=W$ and $n=\Theta(W)$; this realizes each pairwise
product up to a constant, but joint $\values Wn$ tightness remains open. Common heuristic families are
width-bounded. These are representation, not runtime or quality, guarantees.

Safe terminal coarsening yields a cap-aware $K=8$ PDB selector that cannot
lower the exact selector's score or semantic nontriviality on score-identical
pools. Across the
\CapPrimaryTracePairs\ completed identical-pool pairs, it adds
\CapPrimarySemanticWins\ semantic-nontrivial selections and loses none. On the
fixed \CapPrimaryTasks-task primary complement, macro coverage rises by
\CapPrimaryMacroDeltaPoints\ points
(\CapPrimaryWins/\CapPrimaryLosses\ wins/losses); on the separate 590
metric-complete pairs, expanded BDD nodes fall 11.6\%. This is not overall
dominance: unaligned M\&S retains higher census coverage, and the experiment
does not validate the worst-case bound.
