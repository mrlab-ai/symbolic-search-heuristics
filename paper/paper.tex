\begin{abstract}
Why do some heuristics help symbolic \astar\ while others destroy its
compactness? Heuristic accuracy, the usual explanation of search performance,
cannot answer this question: even the perfect heuristic can split a compact
binary decision diagram (BDD) into exponentially larger pieces. We study
fixed-order forward symbolic \astar\ that partitions each frontier by
heuristic value. At every variable cut, our cofactor-profile product multiplies
the numbers of residual functions of a search layer and of the heuristic's
algebraic decision diagram (ADD). It bounds every heuristic split and yields a
certificate on the cumulative number of expanded BDD nodes. If $W$ is the
largest residual count of the heuristic (its cofactor width), $V$ its number
of finite values and $n$ the number of state bits, heuristic search expands at
most $2VWn$ times as many BDD nodes as blind search, and a polynomial-size
planning family attains $VWn/120$. Cofactor width itself is standard
decision-diagram width; its connection to exact search buckets is new. Safe
value caps never increase the certificate, which yields a width-constrained
pattern database (PDB) selector. With width budget $K=8$ on
\CapPrimaryTasks\ tasks from \CapFullDomains\ domains, cap-aware selection
changes domain-averaged coverage by \CapPrimaryMacroDeltaPoints\ percentage
points relative to exact-width selection and expands 11.6\% fewer BDD nodes on
590 completed pairs. In a predictor study, width orders bucket fragmentation
much better than heuristic quality (\SemanticUnionWidthMacro\ versus
\SemanticUnionQualityMacro\ concordance) and comparably to total ADD size
(\SemanticUnionAddMacro), but only \SemanticUnionPairTasks\ tasks support the
comparison, below the preregistered floor of \SemanticUnionTaskFloor. All
empirical results are descriptive, and unaligned merge-and-shrink retains
higher coverage.
\end{abstract}

% ============================ introduction ===============================
\section{Introduction}

Heuristics guide explicit \astar\ by ranking individual states
\citep{hart-et-al-ieeessc1968,pearl-1984}. Symbolic search instead expands
sets of states represented as binary decision diagrams
\citep[BDDs;][]{mcmillan-1993,edelkamp-helmert-ecp1999,bryant-ieeecomp1986},
and a heuristic used there also partitions these sets. Since the BDD of a
subset can be much larger than the BDD of its superset, a split that is
informative for search can be costly to represent. This raises our central
question: which structural property of a heuristic determines how much it
fragments symbolic frontiers, and can this property guide heuristic
construction?

Heuristic accuracy, the usual explanation in explicit search, does not
suffice. \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} show that even the perfect heuristic
\hstar\ can increase the cumulative size of the expanded BDDs exponentially.
Symbolic heuristic search algorithms also behave inconsistently in practice.
\bddastar\ \citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998} stores one BDD per heuristic value,
SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} and \ghseta\ \citep{jensen-et-al-aij2008} fold the
heuristic into the transition relations, and ADDA\ensuremath{^{*}}
\citep{hansen-et-al-sara2002} stores the heuristic as an algebraic decision
diagram (ADD); each of them helps in some domains and hurts in others
\citep{torralba-phd2015,torralba-et-al-aij2017}. Blind symbolic search thus
remains strong for cost-optimal planning
\citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017,speck-et-al-jair2025,speck-ipc2023}. Work on operator-potential heuristics reports
little BDD growth in practice \citep{fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}
but does not characterize when partitioning by a heuristic is safe, and
existing worst-case guarantees for symbolic versus explicit search concern
transition relations and goal formulas and explicitly leave heuristics aside
\citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025}.

We give such a characterization relative to blind symbolic search. At each
cut of the fixed BDD variable order, our \emph{cofactor-profile product}
multiplies the number of residual functions of a state set by that of the
heuristic. This product bounds the residual pairs that actually co-occur
without modeling their correlation. Its heuristic factor, maximized over all
cuts, is the standard residual width of a multi-terminal BDD (MTBDD)
\citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}, which we call \emph{cofactor
width}, and the product argument behind binary synthesis is also standard
\citep[Thm.~3.3.6]{wegener-2000}. Our contribution is to connect these
quantities to the exact buckets of symbolic search, to accumulate them over
cost layers and to derive tight bounds relative to blind search. Cofactor
width, the frontier-independent relaxation of the profile, can be measured
before search.

For fixed-order forward symbolic \astar\ that partitions each frontier by
heuristic value, our main theorem bounds the cumulative number of expanded BDD
nodes by $O(VWn)$ times that of blind search, where $V$ is the number of
finite heuristic values, $W$ the cofactor width and $n$ the number of state
bits. A polynomial-size planning family attains $\Omega(VWn)$ throughout the
feasible parameter range, so none of the three factors can be dropped. The
known exponential example has exponential width under its analyzed order.
Bounded width thus protects against this pathology; it is sufficient, but not
necessary, for compact splits.

Safe terminal maps such as uniform caps never increase any cut count. We bound
the width of integer potentials, PDBs and linear merge-and-shrink (M\&S)
aligned with the search order, and we use caps in a width-constrained PDB
selector. Whenever the cap-aware and the exact-width selector complete on the
same raw pool, the cap-aware candidate set contains every exact-feasible
candidate and therefore cannot lower their shared information score. This
guarantees candidate-set and score dominance, not faster search.

In SymK \citep{speck-et-al-jair2025} with width budget $K=8$, cap-aware
selection changes domain-averaged coverage by \CapPrimaryMacroDeltaPoints\
percentage points (\CapPrimaryWins/\CapPrimaryLosses\ task wins/losses) and
expands 11.6\% fewer BDD nodes on 590 completed pairs, while unaligned M\&S
retains higher coverage. A predictor study finds width far more concordant
with bucket fragmentation than heuristic quality but misses its preregistered
support floor.

% ============================ background =================================
\section{Background}

We use finite deterministic SAS$^{+}$ tasks
$\task=\langle\vars,\ops,\sinit,\sgoal\rangle$
\citep{backstrom-nebel-compint1995}. Operators have positive integer costs.
The functions $\gstar(s)$ and $\hstar(s)$ are the cheapest costs from
\sinit\ to $s$ and from $s$ to a goal, respectively, with value $\infty$ if no
such path exists. We write $\copt=\hstar(\sinit)$ and assume
$0<\copt<\infty$. A heuristic
$h\colon\states\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible}
if $h\leq\hstar$, and \emph{consistent} if
$h(s)\leq\cost(o)+h(s[o])$ for every applicable $o$ and $h=0$ on goals.
The blind heuristic is $\hblind\equiv0$.

We encode every SAS$^{+}$ variable $v$ with $\lceil\log_2|D_v|\rceil$
Boolean bits and write $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ for all bits in the fixed variable
order $\pi$. The order is \emph{variable-contiguous} if the bits of each
SAS$^{+}$ variable are consecutive. Some bit strings encode no state, so every
heuristic receives a total extension to all of $\{0,1\}^n$. As in our CUDD
implementation, a state set $S$ is represented by the reduced ordered BDD
(OBDD) of its characteristic function $\chi_S$ with complemented edges, and
$\bddsize{S}$ counts its regular inner nodes \citep{bryant-ieeecomp1986}. A
signed pointer records a regular node together with its polarity, so $f$ and
$\neg f$ share regular nodes but remain distinct residual functions. For a
prefix $\rho\in\{0,1\}^i$, $f|_\rho$ denotes the residual function of $f$, and
$\sub{i}{f}=\{f|_\rho\mid\rho\in\{0,1\}^i\}$ is the set of distinct cofactors
at cut $i$.

\begin{definition}[cofactor count]
\label{def-cofcount}
For a function $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the \emph{cofactor count} is
$\cofcount(f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} |\sub{i}{f}|$.
\end{definition}

We write $\cutcount{i}{f}=|\sub{i}{f}|$ for the number of cofactors at cut
$i$. For a state set $S$ and a heuristic $h$, the \emph{cofactor-profile
product} is
\[
  \cutprod{S}{h}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
  \cutcount{i}{\chi_S}\cutcount{i}{h}.
\]
It multiplies the two residual counts cut by cut; in particular,
$\cutprod{S}{h}\leq\width_\pi(h)\cofcount(\chi_S)$ for the cofactor width
$\width_\pi(h)$ of Definition~\ref{def-width}.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem-qsize}
For every Boolean $f$ over $x_1, \dots, x_n$,
$\bddsize{f} \leq \cofcount(f) \leq n \cdot (\bddsize{f} + 2)$.
If $f$ is nonconstant, also
$\cofcount(f)\leq2n\bddsize{f}$.
\end{lemma}

The cofactor count $\cofcount(f)$ is the number of states of the
quasi-reduced OBDD of $f$, counting both polarities. At each cut, the
residuals are the frontier nodes in each reached polarity plus at most two
signed constants, and counting edges of the projected binary DAG bounds their
number by $\bddsize{f}+2$; conversely, every regular node is witnessed by a
residual at its own level. The supplement gives the full proof. The factor $n$
is unavoidable already for $f=x_1$, where $\cofcount(f)=2n-1$ but
$\bddsize{f}=1$. The last inequality follows from the first one when
$\bddsize{f}\geq2$, and a nonconstant one-node BDD is a literal with cofactor
count at most $2n$.

Symbolic uniform-cost search represents each exact layer
$L_g=\{s\mid\gstar(s)=g\}$ by one BDD \citep{torralba-et-al-aij2017}.
Product-at-evaluation forward \bddastar\ \citep{edelkamp-reffel-ki1998}
additionally intersects these layers with the level sets
$\level{h}{v}=\{s\mid h(s)=v\}$ of the heuristic
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}. It discards states with $h=\infty$, represents
each $(g,v)$ bucket by one BDD, selects buckets by increasing
$(f,g)=(g+v,g)$ and tests for a goal when it selects a bucket. An open bucket
persists until its exact key is selected. Immediately before expansion, its
resident set is intersected with the complement of the states already closed
at their best known $g$-value; below, ``bucket'' means this residual set.
Selection moves the entire residual bucket to the closed list before its
successors are generated. These assumptions about the queue, duplicates, goal
tests and positive costs define the scope of our cumulative guarantees.

\begin{definition}[expansion size, \citealp{speck-et-al-icaps2020}]
\label{def-effort}
Let $B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ denote the unique BDD expanded by \bddastar\ with
$g$-value $g$ and $h$-value $v$ on task \task\ with heuristic $h$, with
$B_{\task,h}(g,v)$ representing the empty set if no such expansion
occurs. In the consistent product-at-evaluation setting above, the
\emph{expansion size} of \bddastar\ is
$\effort(\task,h) = \sum_{\,g + v \leq \copt,\ g < \copt}
|B_{\task,h}(g,v)|$; Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} proves that these and only these
nonempty buckets are expanded before termination.
\end{definition}

We also call expansion size the \emph{effort} of a search.

For $\hblind$, \bddastar\ is uniform-cost search and
$\effort(\task,\hblind) = \sum_{g < \copt} \bddsize{L_g}$.
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020} prove that on some tasks even \hstar\ has
exponentially larger effort than blind search. Since a subset can have a much
larger BDD than its superset, both slicing and pruning can inflate
representations. We seek a property of the heuristic that controls the BDD
size of every subset it selects by value.

% ===================== width-bounded heuristics ==========================
\section{Bounded Cofactor Width}

\begin{definition}[cofactor width]
\label{def-width}
The \emph{cofactor width} of a heuristic $h$ under variable order $\pi$
is $\width_\pi(h) = \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} |\sub{i}{h}|$. We call $h$
\emph{$(\pi,W)$-width-bounded} if $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, and write
\emph{width} for cofactor width throughout.
\end{definition}

The cofactors are precisely the states of a deterministic layered automaton
that reads the bits in order $\pi$, so width is also the largest level of the
heuristic's quasi-reduced MTBDD \citep[Thm.~3.2.2; Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000}.
Width is thus a standard representation property applied to heuristics, not a
new measure, and it is unrelated to novelty width in planning
\citep{lipovetzky-geffner-ecai2012}.

\begin{proposition}[value range]
\label{prop-range}
If $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$, then $|\hrange(h)| \leq W$.
\end{proposition}

The proposition holds because the cofactors at the last cut are the attained
constants. We write $\values=|h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}|$ for the number
of finite values on planning states and $T=|\hrange(h)|$ for the number of
terminals of the total extension; hence $\values\leq T\leq W$.

\begin{proposition}[closure]
\label{prop-closure}
Let $h_1, h_2$ have widths $W_1, W_2$ under $\pi$ and let
$\oplus \in \{+, \max, \min\}$. Then
$\width_\pi(h_1 \oplus h_2) \leq W_1 \cdot W_2$.
\end{proposition}

At each cut, a combined cofactor is determined by a pair of operand
cofactors. Maxima of heuristics, and sums under an admissible cost partition,
therefore keep the product of the component widths as a bound.

Potential heuristics are computed from functions that can be negative. We
call a function
$q\colon\states\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$ a \emph{raw admissible and
consistent estimate} if $q(s)\leq \hstar(s)$ for every state,
$q(s)\leq c+q(s')$ for every transition $s\xrightarrow{c}s'$, and
$q(s)\leq0$ on goals.

\begin{proposition}[safe terminal transforms]
\label{prop-transform}
Let $q$ be a raw admissible and consistent estimate, equipped with a
total extension
$q \colon \{0,1\}^{n} \to \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}$. Let
$\phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty\}
 \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \cup \{\infty\}$
be nondecreasing, satisfy $\phi(0)=0$, $\phi(\infty)=\infty$, and for
every $a\in\mathbb Z$ and integer $c\geq0$ satisfy
\[
\phi(a+c) \leq \phi(a)+c.
\]
Then $h=\phi\circ q$ is admissible and consistent and
$\cutcount{i}{h}\leq\cutcount{i}{q}$ for every cut $i$. Consequently,
$\width_\pi(h)\leq\width_\pi(q)$ and
$\cutprod{S}{h}\leq\cutprod{S}{q}$ for every state set $S$.
\end{proposition}

Monotonicity and the cost condition transfer consistency and imply
$\phi(a)\leq\max(0,a)$, and mapping terminals cannot create cofactors. Hence
rectification and the cap $\phi_\kappa(a)=\min(\max(0,a),\kappa)$ for finite
$a$, with $\phi_\kappa(\infty)=\infty$, are safe and do not increase width.
The cap has at most $\kappa+1$ finite values and is pointwise the strongest
safe transform with output ceiling $\kappa$. If $\kappa_1\leq\kappa_2$, then
$\phi_{\kappa_1}\circ q$ is a terminal relabeling of $\phi_{\kappa_2}\circ q$,
so its cut counts, its profile product with any fixed set and its width are no
larger. Caps therefore form a nested ladder between safety and
informativeness: increasing $\kappa$ weakly strengthens the heuristic, while
decreasing it weakly contracts both the profile-sensitive bound and the coarse
$2\values Wn$ bound of Section~\ref{sec-effort}.

Width is closely related to the size of the reduced ordered ADD
$\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)$ under $\pi$ \citep{bahar-et-al-iccad1993}.

\begin{proposition}[ADD characterization]
\label{prop-add}
$\width_\pi(h) \leq |\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| + |\hrange(h)|$ and
$|\mathrm{ADD}_\pi(h)| \leq n \cdot \width_\pi(h)$.
\end{proposition}

Every cofactor is a terminal or a residual ADD node, and the ADD nodes at
one level are distinct cofactors. Small width and a small ADD are therefore
polynomially equivalent, but only under the order that the search actually
uses \citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}.

\begin{remark}[total extensions]
\label{rem-encoding}
Definitions over $\{0,1\}^n$ require fixing $h$ on Boolean assignments
that encode no state. Upper bounds apply to the chosen extension; the
families below use natural arithmetic or table extensions. Lower bounds
witnessed on genuine states apply to every extension.
\end{remark}

% ======================= fragmentation theorems ==========================
\section{Bounded Fragmentation}
\label{sec-fragmentation}

Fix $\pi$ and write $W=\width_\pi(h)$. The key observation is that, at
every cut, the cofactor of a subset selected by heuristic value is determined
by the pair of the state-set cofactor and the heuristic cofactor.

\begin{theorem}[bucket bound]
\label{thm-bucket}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and $U \subseteq \hrange(h)$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bddsize{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}
  &\leq \cofcount(\chi_{S \cap h^{-1}(U)}) \\
  &\leq \cutprod{S}{h} \\
  &\leq W\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq Wn(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At cut $i$, equal pairs $(\chi_S|_\rho,h|_\rho)$ induce equal slice
cofactors, so their number is at most
$\cutcount{i}{\chi_S}\cutcount{i}{h}$. Sum over cuts, relax each heuristic
cut count to $W$, and apply Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize}. The supplement gives the
explicit injection.
\end{proof}

The theorem covers single buckets ($U=\{v\}$), the removal of recognized dead
ends and threshold pruning. Up to the additive term, no subset selected by
heuristic value has a reduced BDD more than $Wn$ times as large as that of its
source set.

\begin{theorem}[partition bound]
\label{thm-partition}
Let $S \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{v \in \hrange(h)} \bddsize{S \cap \level{h}{v}}
  &\leq T\cutprod{S}{h} \\
  &\leq TW\cofcount(\chi_S) \\
  &\leq TWn(\bddsize{S}+2) \\
  &\leq W^2n(\bddsize{S}+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
If $S\subseteq\states$ and the sum ranges only over finite values attained
on planning states, every $T$-dependent bound holds with $T$ replaced by
$\values$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} to each of $T$ buckets (or the $\values$
finite planning-state buckets), then relax the profile and use $T\leq W$.
\end{proof}

The sum counts each reduced bucket separately, even if a shared unique table
reuses nodes across buckets. The factor $n$ cannot be removed: the supplement
gives a width-two parity slice whose reduced BDD is $\Theta(n)$ times as large
as its one-node source set.

Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} is a \emph{certificate}: an upper bound on slice size
that is computable from the heuristic and the source set. Total ADD size gives
a second certificate. Let $A$ be the number of inner nodes of the total
heuristic ADD and $b=\bddsize{S}$. Relabeling the ADD terminals by membership
in $U$ \citep[Sec.~9.2]{wegener-2000} gives an ordinary OBDD with at most $A$
nodes, and materializing both polarities of $S$ takes at most $2b$ nodes, so
the standard Apply algorithm \citep[Sec.~4.3]{bryant-ieeecomp1986} gives
$\bddsize{S\cap h^{-1}(U)}\leq2(A+1)b+A$. The two certificates thus combine to
$\min\{\cutprod{S}{h},2(A+1)b+A\}$. With the bucket identity of the next
section, the ADD certificate bounds the ratio to blind effort by
$\values(3A+2)$. A nonconstant ADD has one root and at most $W$ nodes on every
other level, so $A\leq1+(n-1)W$ and this ratio is at most $3\values Wn$; width
is therefore not a uniformly stronger scalar than ADD size. The profile
instead exploits the cut-by-cut alignment between frontier and heuristic, and
width is its frontier-independent envelope. The two certificates are
incomparable. For parity $h$ and the set of strings whose Hamming weight is
$0$ modulo $3$, $\cutprod{S}{h}=\Theta(n)$ while Apply gives $\Theta(n^2)$; for
$h=x_1$ and $S=\{x_1=1\}$, the Apply certificate, which accounts for CUDD's
complemented edges, gives $5$ while the profile is $\Theta(n)$. The supplement
proves both separations. We therefore take the minimum; width supplies
compositional family bounds and a certificate available before search.

% ======================= search-effort guarantees ========================
\section{Search Effort Guarantees}
\label{sec-effort}

To lift the slice bounds to complete searches, we first characterize exactly
which buckets \bddastar\ expands.

\begin{lemma}[bucket identity]
\label{lem-identity}
Let $h$ be consistent. \bddastar\ on \task\ with $h$ selects every
bucket key $(g,v)$ with $g + v \leq \copt$ and $g < \copt$ and
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v} \neq \emptyset$ exactly once before terminating,
and the BDD expanded with key $(g,v)$ represents exactly
$L_g \cap \level{h}{v}$. No other bucket is expanded before
termination.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Consistency makes the $f$-keys of generated successors nondecreasing, and
with positive costs and tie-breaking by increasing $g$, every predecessor that
could populate a key is selected first, so no selected key is repopulated.
Induction along cheapest paths gives optimal $g$-values and complete slices,
and an open prefix of an optimal plan prevents selection above $\copt$. The
supplement gives the full proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[effort bound]
\label{thm-effort}
Let $h$ be consistent with $\width_\pi(h) \leq W$. Then
for
$\values_g=|\{v\in h(\states)\setminus\{\infty\}\mid
g+v\leq\copt,\ L_g\cap\level{h}{v}\ne\emptyset\}|$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\effort(\task,h)
  &\leq \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
    \values_g\cutprod{L_g}{h} \\
  &\leq \values\sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
    \cutprod{L_g}{h} \\
  &\leq \values W
    \sum_{\substack{g<\copt\\L_g\ne\emptyset}}
      \cofcount(\chi_{L_g}) \\
  &\leq \values Wn
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr) \\
  &\leq W^2n
    \bigl(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2\copt\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Lemma~\ref{lem-identity} selects exactly $\values_g$ buckets on layer $g$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm-bucket} bounds each by $\cutprod{L_g}{h}$.
Then $\values_g\leq\values$, profile relaxation, and
Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} give the next three inequalities. If $N$ is the number
of nonempty layers below $\copt$, summing gives
$\values Wn(\effort(\task,\hblind)+2N)$. Positive integer costs imply
$N\leq\copt$, and $\values\leq W$ gives the coarse bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor-effort}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort},
$\effort(\task, h) \leq 2\,\values W n \cdot
\effort(\task, \hblind) \leq
2\, W^{2} n \cdot \effort(\task, \hblind)$.
\end{corollary}

Every nonempty pre-goal layer is a proper subset of the states, so its
characteristic function is nonconstant and Lemma~\ref{lem-qsize} gives
$\cofcount(\chi_{L_g})\leq2n\bddsize{L_g}$. Substituting this bound into the
third inequality of Theorem~\ref{thm-effort} proves the corollary.

The guarantee is one-sided: a heuristic can still save arbitrarily much effort
by pruning. It concerns frontier sizes in forward product-at-evaluation
\bddastar, not primitive relational products in SetA\ensuremath{^{*}} or
\ghseta, and not cumulative bidirectional effort. Variants that visit the same
semantic buckets inherit the bound on their state sets.

\subsection{The Coarse Bound Is Jointly Tight}

The coarse bound cannot be improved by more than a constant factor in any of
its three parameters.

\begin{theorem}[joint lower bound]
\label{thm-tight}
For every $2\leq\values\leq W$ and every power of two $B\geq4W$, there
is a polynomial-size task with $n=\Theta(B)$ Boolean state bits and a
consistent, admissible heuristic $h$, represented by an $O(BW)$-node ordered
ADD, with exactly $\values$ finite values and $\width_\pi(h)=W$ such that
\[
  \frac{\effort(\task,h)}{\effort(\task,\hblind)}
  \geq \frac{\values Wn}{120}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Write $B=2^m$, $C=\values-1$, and $N=2B+m$. The variable order and task
phases are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \textsf{control}\mid a_1\cdots a_m\mid y_1\cdots y_B\mid
 z_0\cdots z_{B-1}\mid t,\\[-2pt]
 \textsf{generate}\xrightarrow{z_a=1}s_0\to\cdots\to s_{B-1}
 \to t.
\end{gathered}
\]
Cost-$C$ choices generate the address $a$, modular bits $y$, and data bits
$z$. A cost-one multiplexer filter retains exactly $z_a=1$, a control chain
carries that set unchanged through $B$ cost-one layers, and a cost-$C$ action
sets the goal bit $t$. On $\mathbb Z_W$, color residues by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lambda(r)&=
 \begin{cases}
   r,&r<\values-1,\\
   \values-1,&r\geq\values-1,
 \end{cases}\\
 h&=\begin{cases}
   0,&t=1,\\
   \lambda(\sum_i y_i\bmod W),&t=0.
 \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]
No nonzero cyclic shift fixes any singleton color class or the final nonempty
proper cyclic interval. Hence the $W$ prefix residues induce
$W$ different heuristic residuals at a central $y$-cut, and no cut has more;
$\width_\pi(h)=W$, and its ordered ADD has $O(BW)$ nodes. Every action changes
$h$ by at most its cost, so $h$ is consistent and admissible.

All $\values$ buckets are selected in each retained layer. Fix one such
layer and bucket $v$. After $j$ modular bits, an address $d$ and prefix
residue $r$ induce the residual
\[
 z_d\wedge\neg t\wedge
 \left[\lambda\left(r+\sum_{i=j+1}^{B}y_i\bmod W\right)=v\right].
\]
Indeed, for $0\leq\ell<B$ and $0\leq v\leq C$,
$NC+1+\ell+v\leq NC+B+C=\copt$.
For $W-1\leq j\leq B-W$, the $BW$ address--residue pairs give distinct
residuals. Each depends on the next $y$-bit because the remaining bits can
realize a boundary of the translated color class. Every displayed residual
is false when $t=1$, so no two are complements. They therefore induce $BW$
distinct regular next-level OBDD nodes at each of $B-2W+2$ cuts. Across
$\values$ buckets and $B$ retained layers, heuristic effort is at least
$\values B(BW)(B-2W+2)\geq\values WB^3/2$. The ordered multiplexer has only
$O(B)$ nodes per retained layer; including generation, blind effort is at
most $15B^2$. Finally $n\leq4B$, which gives the stated ratio. The supplement
gives the operator encoding and all counting details.
\end{proof}

Together with Corollary~\ref{cor-effort}, Theorem~\ref{thm-tight} closes the
worst-case gap for fixed-order product-at-evaluation search: none of
$\values$, $W$ and $n$ can be removed from the ratio. For each power of two
$B$, the construction covers the full region $2\leq\values\leq W\leq B/4$,
and varying $B$ scales $n$. The repeated multiplexer layers are a worst-case
construction, not a prediction for typical tasks.

\subsection{Exponential Width Is Necessary for the Known Pathology}

In the family of \citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020}, chain variables precede
two blocks $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and $v_{n+1},\ldots,v_{2n}$ in $\pi_n$.
At the critical layer, a state is not a dead end exactly when
$\bigvee_i(v_i\wedge v_{n+i})$ holds, which yields their exponentially large
bucket.

\begin{theorem}[width lower bound]
\label{thm-lb}
For the tasks $\task_n$ and orders $\pi_n$ of
\citet{speck-et-al-icaps2020},
$\width_{\pi_n}(\hstar) \geq 2^{n}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At the cut after the first $v$-block, encode each
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ as a
prefix. If $i\in A\setminus A'$, the valid suffix setting only
$v_{n+i}$ distinguishes the prefixes: one state has goal distance one and
the other is a dead end. Thus one cut has $2^n$ distinct \hstar-cofactors,
for every total extension.
\end{proof}

Together, Theorems~\ref{thm-effort} and~\ref{thm-lb} explain the known
pathology: its perfect heuristic has exponential width and thus lies outside
the bounded-width regime. The converse does not hold, since bounded width is
sufficient but not necessary for small buckets. Moreover, \hstar\ takes only
$O(n)$ values here, so the number of values alone does not control how much
prefix information its level sets entangle. The supplement derives further
consequences for idealized image-call counts and for search that uses the
heuristic only for pruning.


% ============================ instances ==================================
\section{Width-Bounded Heuristic Families}
\label{sec-instances}

Three common heuristic families have bounded width. Throughout, $\pi$ is
variable-contiguous, $d=\max_{v\in\vars}|D_v|$ and $m=|\vars|$.

\subsection{Bounded Integer Potentials}

Potential heuristics \citep{pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,fiser-et-al-aaai2022}
assign an integer potential $P(v,w)$ to every fact and compute
$p(s)=\sum_vP(v,s(v))$; we use the safe rectification $\hpot=\max(0,p)$. For
potentials with $|P(v,w)|\leq M$ and an induced lookup table whose values on
invalid codes also lie in $[-M,M]$, the supplement proves
\[
 \width_\pi(\hpot)\leq d(2mM+1),\qquad
 |\hrange(\hpot)|\leq mM+1,
\]
and hence an effort ratio of at most $2d(mM+1)(2mM+1)n$. Standard potential
constraints make $p$ a raw admissible and consistent estimate, so $\hpot$ is
admissible and consistent by Proposition~\ref{prop-transform}. The bound is
pseudo-polynomial, i.e., polynomial in the magnitude $M$ but not in its bit
length, and it does not cover delta-partitioned \ghseta.

\subsection{Pattern Databases}

A pattern database \citep[PDB;][]{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,%
edelkamp-ecp2001} projects \task\ onto a pattern
$P\subseteq\vars$ and stores the abstract goal distance $h^P(s)$ of the
abstract state induced by $s$; this distance is admissible and consistent.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-pdb}
Let $P\subseteq\vars$ be any pattern under a variable-contiguous order
$\pi$. Extend its table to invalid encodings by ignoring non-pattern
bits and mapping an invalid pattern-variable code to $0$. Let
$|\states^P|=\prod_{v\in P}|D_v|$. Then
\[
\width_\pi(h^P)\leq |\states^P|.
\]
\end{proposition}

The residual of the lookup is determined by a partial abstract state and the
bits of the current variable read so far, and all invalid prefixes share the
valid terminal zero. Pattern variables need not form a prefix of the order
because non-pattern bits are skipped. A budget on abstract states thus
directly bounds width. Since a PDB has at most $|\states^P|$ finite values,
its effort ratio is at most $2|\states^P|^2n$. With a separate sink for invalid
codes, the width bound becomes $|\states^P|+1$. Maxima of PDBs, and sums under
an admissible cost partition, have at most the product of the component
widths; this statement about representation does not by itself establish
admissibility.

\subsection{Linear Merge-and-Shrink}

Merge-and-shrink (M\&S) abstractions \citep{helmert-et-al-jacm2014}
iteratively merge the factors of a factored transition system and shrink
intermediate abstractions. A linear merge strategy represents the abstraction
mapping as a cascade of tables \citep{helmert-et-al-icaps2015}. The supplement
proves the following bound for merge orders aligned with the search order.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop-ms}
If a linear merge order agrees with variable-contiguous $\pi$, every cascade
table has at most $N$ valid outputs, and invalid codes share a sink, then
$\width_\pi(h^{\alpha}) \leq d \cdot (N+1)$.
\end{proposition}

The premise bounds the output of every table, not only selected products
after shrinking, and yields an effort ratio of at most $2dN(N+1)n$. Alignment
is essential. Since \texttt{align\_merge\_order} is false for our causal-graph
M\&S control, the formula does not certify it. We give no analogous family
bound for $h^{\max}$, $h^{\mathrm{add}}$ or LM-cut, but the exact width
measured after construction can still instantiate the general theorems.

% ============================ synthesis ==================================
\section{Case Study: Cap-Aware Width-Constrained PDB Selection}
\label{sec-synthesis}

The theory turns terminal coarsening into a controlled intervention: capping a
heuristic never increases its width, so a cap can make an infeasible PDB fit a
width budget. Two selectors invoke the same five pattern generators under a
budget of
$100{,}000$ abstract states: the empty pattern, a prefix of the BDD order, a
prefix of the goal/causal order, a goal/causal-order fill pattern that skips
oversized variables, and one CEGAR pattern \citep{rovner-et-al-icaps2019} with
seed 2011 and a 10-second limit for pattern generation. Patterns are
normalized and deduplicated, keeping every generator label of a duplicate.
The cap-aware path also removes variables with one-value domains first, which
preserves the abstraction but can change the literal pattern, so we verify
pairwise that both selectors see the same raw pool. The empty pattern
guarantees a feasible width-one endpoint.

For each raw PDB, we build the total ADD of the heuristic in the fixed search
order and count the distinct residual nodes after every prefix of Boolean
bits. Dynamic reordering is disabled, residuals at skipped manager levels
remain in the frontier, and invalid encodings keep the default-zero extension.
The measured maximum is therefore the exact cofactor width of the total
extension that the search uses. A candidate is feasible exactly if its
measured width is at most the fixed budget $K=8$.

The exact selector tests only the uncapped PDB. For each pattern, the
cap-aware selector builds the raw PDB once and derives
\[
  h_\kappa(s)=
  \begin{cases}
    \min\{h(s),\kappa\},&h(s)<\infty,\\
    \infty,&h(s)=\infty
  \end{cases}
\]
for the caps $\kappa\in\{0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256\}$ below the maximum
finite value of the raw PDB, followed by the uncapped transform.
Proposition~\ref{prop-transform} preserves admissibility and consistency.
Raising the cap weakly strengthens the heuristic, and lowering it cannot
increase any cut count, so the selector keeps the strongest feasible member of
each pattern's ladder before comparing patterns.

Both selectors then rank the remaining candidates by the same frozen
lexicographic score: initial-state dead-end recognition, initial value, mean
finite value and fraction of abstract dead ends, with smaller width, fewer
abstract states and the pattern as tie-breakers. The score is a surrogate for
heuristic information, not a runtime claim. We call a selection
\emph{semantic-nontrivial} if its finite values have a positive sum or it
recognizes an abstract dead end.

\begin{proposition}[candidate-set and score dominance]
\label{prop-selector-dominance}
Suppose both selectors complete on the same score-bearing normalized raw-PDB
pool. The
cap-aware final candidate set contains the exact selector's final candidate
set, and its selected lexicographic score is no smaller. Consequently, if the
exact winner recognizes the initial state as a dead end or is
semantic-nontrivial, so is the cap-aware winner. If neither winner recognizes
the initial state as a dead end, the cap-aware initial value is no smaller.
Moreover, if the cap-aware winner is an exact endpoint, both selectors return
the same pattern and heuristic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every exact-feasible raw PDB, the exact transform is the last endpoint of
the cap-aware ladder and is retained as that pattern's strongest feasible
member. Thus every exact candidate remains available when the same score is
maximized. The three consequences follow from the score's first four
components. For the last claim, let $e$ and $p$ be the exact-selector and
cap-aware winners, and suppose $p$ is exact. Candidate containment gives
$\mathrm{score}(p)\geq\mathrm{score}(e)$, while $p$ belongs to the exact set
and hence $\mathrm{score}(e)\geq\mathrm{score}(p)$. The deterministic total
score, including the pattern tie breaker, therefore gives $p=e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[selector safety]
\label{cor-selector-safety}
On every task satisfying Theorem~\ref{thm-effort}, each completed selection by
either selector has effort at most $2K^2n$ times blind effort. At $K=8$, this
factor is at most $128n$. If the selected transform has finite cap $\kappa$,
the sharper factor is $2K\min\{K,\kappa+1\}n$.
\end{corollary}

Every returned transform is admissible and consistent, has measured width at
most $K$ and has at most $K$ finite values, and a cap $\kappa$ has at most
$\kappa+1$ finite values; Corollary~\ref{cor-effort} then gives the claim. The
guarantee concerns representation effort, not heuristic quality or runtime.

For attribution, we certify pairwise that both traces are complete and that
the reconstructed raw pools are identical; the supplement gives the
contracts.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec-experiments}

We ask four questions about fixed task sets. \emph{Q0:} Does cofactor width
order isolated bucket fragmentation better than the frozen information-quality
score, and comparably to total ADD size? \emph{Q1:} On completed pairs with
identical pools, how often does the larger cap-aware candidate set yield a
semantic-nontrivial selection? \emph{Q2:} Does cap-aware selection improve
coverage over exact-width selection? \emph{Q3:} Which construction and search
costs accompany this difference? Three conventional configurations provide
descriptive context.

Q0 uses a separately frozen, corrected measurement with
\SemanticUnionTasks\ tasks and 16 configurations. Fragmentation $F$ is the
total number of BDD nodes of all expanded buckets divided by the total number
of BDD nodes of their unions per $g$-value. On a shared set of strictly
ordered pairs, we compare the ordinal concordance of $F$ with $W$, with total
ADD size $U$ and with the quality score, weighting domains equally. The gate
combines fixed effect criteria (concordance of $W$ at least 0.65, at least
0.60 when any single domain is left out, above that of quality and within 0.02
of that of $U$) with minimum support of 31 domains and
\SemanticUnionTaskFloor\ tasks; a failed gate permits no rescue tuning.

Frozen exclusions of 290 tasks with zero-cost operators and 30 tasks with
axioms after normalization leave \CapFullTasks\ of 1697 optimal sequential IPC
tasks, from \CapFullDomains\ domains. Using one planner revision, the matrix
runs blind search, unaligned causal-graph M\&S with 10,000 states, one CEGAR
PDB with 100,000 states, and the exact and cap-aware selectors with $K=8$.
Every run uses product-at-evaluation forward symbolic \astar\ with the same
task-specific BDD order; blind search uses $h\equiv0$. Each run has a limit of
300\,seconds of aggregate process CPU time and 8\,GiB of memory, and
micro-PAR2 assigns 600\,seconds to unsolved runs. The matrix has
\CapFullCells\ cells.

We froze the $K=8$ contrast and the rule for promoting it before inspecting
aggregate results of a separate 50-task development screen. The rule required
complete records, matching raw pools and solution costs, more
semantic-nontrivial selections for the cap-aware selector and nonnegative
coverage discordance; $K=32$ could not substitute. The primary task set is
the complement of the screen: \CapPrimaryTasks\ tasks that span all
\CapFullDomains\ domains but are neither random nor unseen in earlier project
stages. The primary estimand is the difference in coverage between cap-aware
and exact selection, averaged equally over domains, and task-level counts
report the paired discordance. Adding the screen tasks back gives the
prespecified sensitivity analysis on all \CapFullTasks\ tasks.

Missing or duplicate cells, incomplete traces or disagreeing costs
invalidate the analysis instead of changing a denominator. A separately frozen
secondary contract reports micro-PAR2 including construction, planner CPU time
on positive jointly solved pairs, and image time, expanded nodes and
construction time on eligible pairs. The audit of expansion effort is post
hoc and changes no frozen estimand. The supplement records launch rules,
digests and parser checks.

\section{Related Work}

Even \hstar\ can cause exponential BDD effort in symbolic search
\citep{speck-et-al-icaps2020}; we show that their construction has
exponential cofactor width. Residual width, products and Apply are standard
\citep{wegener-2000,bryant-ieeecomp1986}. New here are their connection to
exact search buckets, the cut-aligned cumulative and blind-relative bounds,
the incomparability with Apply and the matching planning family.

PDBs, automatic pattern selection and symbolic compilation predate this work
\citep{culberson-schaeffer-compint1998,edelkamp-ecp2001,
haslum-et-al-aaai2007,rovner-et-al-icaps2019,edelkamp-et-al-ecai2012}. PDB
compression may merge entries or coarsen values
\citep{felner-et-al-jair2007,sturtevant-et-al-aaai2017}; our uniform cap is
not a new compression primitive. Its role is to preserve consistency, contract
every cut and meet an exact width budget before ranking. Total ADD size is the
closest incomparable representation budget, and we claim no empirical
superiority over it.

Factored M\&S mappings and potential heuristics are established
\citep{torralba-phd2015,helmert-et-al-icaps2015,pommerening-et-al-aaai2015,
fiser-et-al-aaai2022,fiser-et-al-aij2024}; \ghseta\ with delta partitions is a
different architecture. Relaxed BDD heuristics bound action-choice diagrams
\citep{castro-et-al-icaps2019}. Our certificate depends on the variable order
\citep{kissmann-hoffmann-jair2014}. Guarantees for symbolic versus explicit
search \citep{speck-helmert-ecai2025} and symbolic perimeter abstractions
\citep{torralba-et-al-ijcai2016a,torralba-et-al-aij2018} address different
mechanisms.

\section{Limitations}

Our guarantee is specific to fixed-order forward product-at-evaluation search
with positive costs, consistent heuristics and our duplicate policy. It bounds
BDD nodes, not time or memory; bounded width is sufficient but not necessary,
and the matching construction is a worst case. The full census lacks cut
profiles. The first profile experiment failed its gates, and the corrected
study is conditional on solved runs, has too few supporting tasks and cannot
explain coverage or timeouts; its diagnostic planner-time ratio is
\SemanticUnionInstrumentationRatio. Neither study licenses predictor
validation or new tuning.

The $K=8$ case study runs each configuration once per task with a hard cutoff,
and its outcome-informed development screen is disclosed and excluded. The
coverage difference has no population-inferential interpretation, the effort
ratios condition on completed pairs, and construction is 2.3\% slower. In a separate replication with several
budgets, the comparison between width budgets and matched ADD-size budgets
reverses across budgets and no construction gate passes; we select no
favorable budget.

% ============================ conclusions ================================
\section{Conclusions}

We showed that cut-aligned cofactor profiles bound the effort of
product-at-evaluation \bddastar\ by $2\values Wn$ times that of blind search,
that a planning family attains $\values Wn/120$, and that safe coarsening never
increases the certificate. Width ordered fragmentation much better than
heuristic quality in an underpowered study, and cap-aware selection at $K=8$
added \CapPrimarySemanticWins\ informative selections with a small coverage
gain. Cofactor width is thus a representation certificate with a scoped
practical use, not a guarantee of runtime or planner dominance.
